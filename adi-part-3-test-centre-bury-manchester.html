ADI Part 3 at Bury: pass rate and test centre guide

Updated 25 November 2026 · Source: DVSA ADI part 3 test statistics (DRT522B, published 25 June 2026, data to March 2026)

Bury (Manchester) is one of the eight DVSA test centres in the Manchester area that publish ADI Part 3 results. In 2025/26 its pass rate was 20.0%, which is 7.8 percentage points below the Great Britain figure of 27.8%. That makes it the fourth lowest of the eight Manchester centres.

At a glance: DVSA withheld the number of ADI Part 3 tests at Bury for 2025/26 because it is small, so only the pass rate is public. Its pass rate was 44.4% in 2024/25, a change of -24.4 points, and 26.3% in 2018/19.

What the figures tell you

  • Compared with Great Britain: Bury was above the national rate in 1 of the 7 years shown, and at or below it in 6.
  • Trend: its pass rate moved from 26.3% in 2018/19 to 20.0% in 2025/26, a change of -6.3 points. Across Great Britain the rate moved from 37.1% to 27.8% over the same period (-9.2 points).
  • Volume: DVSA withheld the number of ADI Part 3 tests at Bury for 2025/26 because it is small, so only the pass rate is public.
Read it with care. One year at one centre is a small sample. A few extra passes or fails can change the rate by several points, and the pass rate says as much about who took their test at the centre and how well prepared they were as about the test itself.

If your test is booked at Bury

  • Check your booking confirmation for the exact centre and time, and plan your journey and your pupil's journey with a margin.
  • Choose a pupil who is comfortable on the roads in the area. GOV.UK says the pupil can be a partly trained learner, a fully trained learner or a full licence holder.
  • Prepare for the test itself rather than the centre: about 45 minutes, a short opening on goals and risk, around 40 minutes with the pupil driving, and a short reflection at the end. See the test, minute by minute.
  • Know your pass mark. You need 31 or more out of 51 to pass. See how it is marked.

Questions people ask

Is Bury an ADI Part 3 test centre?

DVSA publishes ADI Part 3 results for Bury (Manchester), so ADI Part 3 tests are carried out there. Check your booking for the exact centre.

What is the ADI Part 3 pass rate at Bury?

DVSA's published pass rate for Bury was 20.0% in 2025/26 and 44.4% in 2024/25. The Great Britain figure was 27.8% in 2025/26.

Is the ADI Part 3 harder at Bury?

A pass rate cannot tell you that. It reflects the candidates who took their test there and how well prepared they were. With small numbers, a few results can change a centre's rate by several points.

How many tests are there at this centre?

DVSA withheld the number of ADI Part 3 tests at Bury for 2025/26 because it is small, so only the pass rate is public.

What score do I need to pass the ADI Part 3?

31 or more out of 51. Grade B is 31 to 42, Grade A is 43 to 51, and 0 to 30 is a fail. A score of 7 or less in the risk management category is also a fail.

Source and method. All figures are from the DVSA tables DRT522B ("ADI part 3 test pass rate by gender: test centres", updated 25 June 2026, data to March 2026) and DRT521C ("ADI part 3 tests conducted, passed and pass rates", Great Britain, updated 3 September 2026), published on GOV.UK. Years are financial years (April to March); 2020/21 is left out because the pandemic disrupted testing. DVSA notes that ADI Part 3 data is recorded by hand, so accuracy can be affected. It also withholds counts where they are small enough to identify individuals (shown here as 'withheld'), and a centre appears only if more than one examiner carries out these tests. Small numbers move pass rates a lot: one extra pass or fail can shift a centre's rate by several points. Treat one year at one centre with care. DVSA also notes that before December 2022 not all ADI Part 3 tests were recorded, so older pass rates carry extra uncertainty. Differences, rankings and comparisons on this page are calculated by DriveSQ from the DVSA figures and are not DVSA headline figures. A centre's pass rate says nothing about how any individual test was marked.
