Test Centre

Oldham Driving Test Centre

Everything about Bromley Street DTC — including details most driving schools never share with students.

Address: Bromley Street, Oldham OL8 4PQ — just off Manchester Street near the town ring road
Test Duration: Approximately 38–40 minutes driving + pre-test briefing
Parking: Bromley Street has limited spaces. Side streets off Manchester Street are an alternative — allow 15 minutes. Do not park on double yellows on the approach.
Documents: Provisional licence, theory pass certificate (within 2 years), glasses if prescribed
Test Fee: £62 weekday / £75 weekend (DVSA 2025 rates)
What nobody tells you: The DTC exit from Bromley Street feeds directly into Manchester Street — a busy multi-lane road with bus traffic. The examiner watches mirror use and lane choice from the moment you pull away. Many candidates stall or hesitate here under pressure. We practise this exit specifically before test day.
Town centre approach: Most tests begin by heading toward the Oldham ring road or Manchester Street one-way. Within the first 3 minutes you'll face a junction or roundabout — the examiner is already assessing. Don't treat the start as a warm-up.
Typical first move: Right out of DTC onto Bromley Street → Manchester Street → either ring road north toward Rochdale Road, or south toward the town gyratory and Lees Road. Both directions require immediate lane positioning.
Pass rate: Oldham DTC sits around the national average. Quality of preparation — especially knowledge of the one-way system and hill starts — is the biggest variable.
Real Test Routes

The 8 Oldham Test Routes
Published Nowhere Else

Our instructors have mapped every route used by Oldham DTC examiners. Students practise them all, so test day holds no surprises.

1
High Fail Rate

Manchester Street One-Way Gyratory

Oldham town centre operates a complex one-way gyratory on Manchester Street, Clegg Street, and Union Street. The system flows anti-clockwise and the lane markings change direction at several junctions without prominent overhead signs. Candidates who haven't driven it multiple times frequently take the wrong lane approaching Oldham Mumps or the ring road junction. Examiners route almost every test through part of this system. The approach to the A627(M) slip from the gyratory is where wrong-lane serious faults are most commonly awarded.

📍 Manchester Street → Union Street → Clegg Street → Oldham ring road → Mumps bridge
2
5-Arm Roundabout

Watersheddings Roundabout

Watersheddings is a 5-arm roundabout at the junction of Rochdale Road A671, Block Lane, Watersheddings Street, Featherstall Road North, and the approach from the town centre. It is one of the most complex roundabouts on any Greater Manchester test route. Lane discipline matters enormously — candidates must be in the correct lane well in advance, signal correctly when exiting, and time entry gaps precisely. Many candidates treat it like a simple give-way when it requires specific lane pre-planning for each destination. Our students walk through every exit combination before their test.

📍 Rochdale Road A671 at Block Lane junction — all approaches practised
3
Speed Cameras

Rochdale Road A671 Northbound

Rochdale Road A671 heads north from Oldham through Watersheddings toward Rochdale. It transitions from 30mph near town to 40mph past the Watersheddings roundabout. Speed camera enforcement operates in the 40mph section. Candidates who stay at 30mph on the 40mph stretch get "undue hesitancy" faults — just as serious as speeding. There is also a significant uphill section on the A671 near Block Lane where engine braking and gear selection matter for the examiner. The dual carriageway approach to the Heywood Way junction requires confident lane positioning.

📍 A671 northbound from Watersheddings → Block Lane → Rochdale boundary
4
Hill Starts

Werneth Residential Network

Werneth (OL8) is the hilliest residential area on any Oldham test route. Featherstall Road South, Park Road, and the surrounding streets all involve junctions on significant gradients. At uphill junctions, rolling back even briefly is a serious fault. At downhill junctions, candidates must control speed with the footbrake while giving way before moving off. Our Werneth hill-start programme covers every major junction in the network — uphill moves, downhill give-ways, and parallel parking on a gradient (which can appear as the test manoeuvre here).

📍 Featherstall Road South → Park Road → Honeywell Lane → Oldham Way A627
5
Rural Approach

Lees Road A669 and Springhead

Lees Road A669 runs east from Oldham toward the Saddleworth border. The road is 40mph in the open section past Waterhead and narrows at Lees village. The junction at Springhead (A669 / A670 Mossley Road) is a staggered crossroads that many candidates misread as a straightforward T-junction — there are two separate junctions close together requiring two separate give-way decisions. Beyond Springhead, test routes occasionally reach Grotton and return via the B6194 through Lydgate, a narrow rural road with passing places that tests observation and forward planning.

📍 Lees Road A669 → Waterhead → Springhead crossroads → return via A669
6
Dual Carriageway

Shaw Road A663 and Crompton Way

Shaw Road A663 runs north from Oldham through Royton toward Shaw — a 40mph dual carriageway section with a dedicated right-turn lane at the Royton Centre junction. The merge from Oldham Way onto A663 northbound tests lane discipline under speed. Crompton Way is the return route — a 40mph road with a complex roundabout at the junction of the A663 and Shaw Road (two roads of the same name at this point, confusing candidates). The bus lane on Shaw Road is operational 7–9am and 3–6pm — entering it during test hours is a serious fault.

📍 Oldham Way → Shaw Road A663 → Royton → Crompton Way → return
7
Residential

Fitton Hill and Bardsley Estate

Fitton Hill (OL8) is a dense post-war housing estate with narrow roads, unmarked speed humps, and many of the post-2022 20mph zones that sat navs still show as 30mph. Bardsley, adjacent to Fitton Hill, has similar characteristics. Junction priorities on the estate are a mix of give-way lines and unmarked junctions where the "right has priority" rule is implied. Candidates unfamiliar with the area often over-brake at non-junctions and then hesitate at real ones. Our Fitton Hill programme covers every key junction and all current speed limits.

📍 Fitton Hill Road → Bardsley → Lime Side → Ashton Road A627
8
Manoeuvre Zone

Bromley Street Bay Parking

Bay parking is the most commonly tested manoeuvre at Oldham DTC — examiners use the DTC car park on Bromley Street. Reverse bay park is tested more frequently than drive-in. The bays are a standard size but the approach angle from Bromley Street requires precise positioning from the start. Candidates who rely on feel rather than reference points consistently over-correct. DriveSQ teaches a reference-point system specific to this car park. Parallel parking is tested on Bromley Street itself using the marked bays — the road camber (slopes away from the pavement) affects the front-wheel position, which many candidates don't account for.

📍 Bromley Street DTC car park · Bromley Street parallel bays · Ledger Way residential

Local Knowledge

8 Oldham Hazards Other
Schools Never Cover

Specific to Oldham. Not generic advice — the real things that catch candidates off guard at Bromley Street DTC.

The Oldham Gyratory Wrong-Lane Trap

Manchester Street feeds into a one-way gyratory with lane options that change at every arm. Candidates who are slow to read lane markings get locked into the wrong lane and either make a dangerous lane change or take the wrong exit. The worst point is the approach to Mumps bridge — two lanes narrow to one and the correct choice depends on which exit you need. This is the single most common serious fault at Oldham DTC. If you haven't driven the gyratory at least 10 times, you don't know it well enough.

Watersheddings — Wrong Exit

The 5-arm Watersheddings roundabout has exits that look similar from the approach. Candidates who enter on autopilot frequently take Featherstall Road North when the examiner called for Rochdale Road, or vice versa. The roundabout also has a lane-merge on the Rochdale Road exit that candidates miss entirely — staying in the correct lane through the merge is observed by the examiner. Wrong exit = serious fault. Wrong lane on exit = serious fault. Our students practise every combination.

Werneth Hill Start — Rolling Back

Park Road and Featherstall Road South in Werneth have junctions on gradients between 1-in-8 and 1-in-12. Manual candidates who use only the handbrake for hill starts risk rolling back into the following vehicle if they release too early. The correct technique (clutch biting point + slight acceleration before releasing handbrake) is taught by all good instructors — but Werneth tests it under pressure, on a real road, with traffic behind. Our Werneth hill-start sessions are conducted at peak times so candidates experience real traffic.

Shaw Road A663 Bus Lane Hours

Shaw Road A663 has a bus lane operational 7–9am and 3–6pm (inbound/outbound). Morning and early-afternoon test times frequently overlap with operational hours — a 9am test could return via Shaw Road exactly during the peak window. Entering the bus lane in a private car during operational hours is an automatic serious fault. Many instructors simply say "don't use the bus lane" without explaining which section, which direction, or which hours apply in Oldham. We cover this specifically.

Springhead Staggered Crossroads

The A669/A670 junction at Springhead looks like a straightforward crossroads on Google Maps but is actually two separate T-junctions offset by about 30 metres. Candidates who treat it as one junction go straight across — which crosses oncoming traffic without a give-way. The correct approach is: give way and turn left onto A670 Mossley Road, then treat the second junction as a fresh T-junction. This specific error is rarely taught because most instructors don't take the Lees Road route far enough east to reach it.

Oldham Metrolink Tram Stop (Shaw Road)

The Metrolink Oldham Loop (Oldham Central, Oldham Mumps, Derker, Freehold, Fitton Hill, and Hollinwood stops) runs on-street sections near the test route. Trams use dedicated lanes but also cross road junctions on the one-way section. Candidates must give way to trams, not overtake them at stops, and not park or stop on tram tracks. Most of our competitors in the area don't drill tram-stop etiquette — DriveSQ covers this explicitly because it appears on the Oldham test route.

Fitton Hill 20mph Zones (Post-2022)

Fitton Hill and Bardsley estate roads changed from 30mph to 20mph in 2022–2023 as part of Greater Manchester's borough-wide rollout. Many sat navs and Google Maps still show 30mph for these roads. Candidates who learned on these roads before the change, or who use sat nav speed guidance, frequently drive at 30mph in a 20mph zone — a serious fault on test. Our instructors know the current limit on every road in the Fitton Hill test zone.

Oldham Royal Hospital A&E Approach

The Royal Oldham Hospital is on Rochdale Road A671 — the primary northbound test road. Emergency vehicles use the A671 as their primary corridor from the M60 and from Rochdale. Candidates must know how to respond to blue-light vehicles on a dual carriageway: pull left safely (don't brake suddenly), stop if necessary, don't run red lights to clear the way. This specific scenario appears on the Oldham test route more frequently than most areas because of the hospital's location directly on the examiner's preferred route.

Where is Oldham Driving Test Centre?
Oldham DTC is on Bromley Street, Oldham OL8 4PQ — just off Manchester Street near the Oldham ring road. The nearest landmark is Manchester Street retail strip. Arrive 15 minutes early for parking on Bromley Street or nearby side streets.
What are the real test routes from Oldham DTC?
Examiners use: (1) Manchester Street one-way gyratory and town centre ring road, (2) Rochdale Road A671 northbound past Royal Oldham Hospital and Watersheddings, (3) Shaw Road A663 / Crompton Way toward Royton, (4) Lees Road A669 east toward Springhead and Waterhead, (5) Werneth residential network with hill starts, (6) Fitton Hill / Bardsley estate roads, (7) Chadderton Way / Broadway OL9, (8) Bromley Street bay parking manoeuvre. What is the hardest part of the Oldham test?
The Manchester Street one-way gyratory causes the most serious faults at Oldham DTC. The system is complex and not well-signed, so wrong-lane errors happen frequently to unprepared candidates. Watersheddings roundabout (5-arm) is second — wrong exit or wrong lane on exit. Werneth hill starts are third — rolling back on a gradient junction is an immediate serious fault in manual cars.

Is automatic better than manual for Oldham?
Oldham is genuinely one of the areas where automatic makes the most practical difference. Werneth's gradient junctions are where manual candidates most commonly fail.
