FAQs — MSPSL Technique in M14

What does MSPSL stand for?

MSPSL stands for Mirrors, Signal, Position, Speed, Look — the core decision-making routine used when approaching any hazard or change of direction. It replaces older acronyms like MSM (Mirror, Signal, Manoeuvre) and is the sequence examiners expect to see applied consistently throughout your test.

When do you use the MSPSL routine?

MSPSL applies every time you approach a junction, change lanes, turn, or alter speed. In M14 you'll use it dozens of times per lesson — at roundabouts, crossroads, traffic lights, and when preparing to overtake parked cars. The habit becomes automatic with repetition.

What happens if you forget MSPSL on the test?

Forgetting mirrors before a signal or position change is one of the most common test faults. If the examiner sees you signal without checking mirrors first, it's typically marked as a driving fault. If you change position or speed without checking mirrors and it affects another road user, it can be marked as serious.

How does DriveSQ teach MSPSL in M14?

Your DriveSQ instructor in M14 narrates the MSPSL sequence during early lessons so you hear it applied to every junction and manoeuvre. As you improve, the commentary fades and the sequence happens naturally. By mock test stage, MSPSL is completely automatic and your conscious attention is free for higher-level planning.
