DVSA SCHEME

What is Pass Plus?

Pass Plus is a voluntary training programme developed by the DVSA (Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency) for newly qualified drivers. It covers six modules designed to build the experience and confidence you don't fully develop during standard lessons — and there is no test at the end. Your ADI assesses your progress throughout.

01
Town Driving
Busy urban roads, bus lanes, complex junctions, pedestrian crossings, and city-centre one-way systems.
02
All-Weather Driving
Rain, spray, reduced visibility, wet braking distances, and adapting your driving in changing conditions.
03
Out of Town / Rural
Country roads, narrow lanes, passing places, farm entrances, and junctions with limited visibility.
04
Night Driving
Driving after dark, managing headlights, reading road markings under artificial light, and avoiding dazzle.
05
Dual Carriageways
Lane discipline, joining at speed, overtaking safely, and leaving dual carriageways at higher speeds.
06
Motorway Driving
Smart motorways, contra-flow, contraflow signals, high-speed lane changing, and joining at 70 mph.
How does Pass Plus work?

You complete a minimum of 6 hours of driving with a DVSA-registered ADI. There is no separate test — your instructor assesses your competence across all six modules as you drive. On completion, DriveSQ registers your Pass Plus certificate with the DVSA and provides the documentation you need to claim insurance discounts.

The 6 Pass Plus Modules —
Manchester Roads

Every module is completed on real Manchester roads with your DriveSQ ADI. Here's what you'll cover on each one.

Module 1 — Town Driving
City centre complexity · bus lanes · trams · junctions

Manchester city centre provides some of the most demanding urban driving conditions in the north of England. You'll navigate tram crossings, bus-only corridors, pedestrian zones, and heavily-used junctions. The Oxford Road corridor — one of the busiest bus routes in Europe — is a core part of this module.

Deansgate Piccadilly Oxford Road bus corridor Tram crossings Portland Street
Module 2 — All-Weather Driving
Rain · spray · reduced visibility · wet braking

Manchester receives more annual rainfall than virtually every other major UK city — so this module is rarely theoretical here. You'll learn to adapt braking distances on wet surfaces, manage motorway spray at speed, and drive safely in reduced-visibility conditions on both urban and faster roads.

Wet motorway spray Reduced visibility Aquaplaning awareness Adapted braking
Module 3 — Out of Town / Rural
Country roads · narrow lanes · Peak District approach

From Manchester city centre, the A6 Buxton Road takes you directly toward the Peak District within 20 minutes. You'll experience narrow lanes, farm junctions with limited sightlines, passing places, and the kind of moorland approach roads that new drivers rarely encounter during lessons in an urban area.

A6 Buxton Road Peak District approach Narrow lanes Farm junctions Passing places
Module 4 — Night Driving
City lighting · suburban transitions · unlit stretches

Manchester's night driving environment is genuinely varied — from the bright lighting of the city centre and key arterials, through semi-lit suburban roads in areas like Fallowfield, Withington, and Levenshulme, to darker stretches on outward routes. You'll practise managing your headlights, avoiding dazzle, and reading road markings after dark.

City centre lighting Suburban transitions Unlit arterials Headlight management
Module 5 — Dual Carriageways
Lane discipline · overtaking · joining at speed

Greater Manchester has an extensive dual carriageway network used every day by commuters and through traffic. Your Pass Plus will include joining and leaving at speed, safe overtaking, lane discipline in busy conditions, and driving at higher sustained speeds without motorway regulations.

A57M Mancunian Way A56 Chester Road A6 Wellington Road A34 Kingsway Lane discipline
Module 6 — Motorway Driving
M60 orbital · M56 Airport · M62 · smart motorways

Manchester sits at the centre of one of England's most complex motorway networks. Your motorway module will cover the M60 orbital, M56 to Manchester Airport, sections of the M62 toward Leeds, and the M6 interchange. You'll cover joining at speed, lane discipline, smart motorway overhead signals, and the correct response to contraflow situations.

Pass Plus Manchester FAQs

Is Pass Plus worth it?
For most new drivers in Manchester, yes. First-year insurance often runs £1,000–£3,000+. A Pass Plus certificate typically saves 10–30% with participating insurers — meaning savings of £100–£900+ in year one alone. At £210 for the course, it usually pays for itself many times over. Beyond the money, the confidence gained on motorways and in all-weather conditions has real safety value that doesn't show up in the numbers.
How long does Pass Plus take?
Pass Plus is a minimum of 6 hours of driving covering all 6 modules. There is no test at the end — completion is assessed by your ADI instructor throughout the course. Most students complete it across 2–3 sessions. DriveSQ instructors are flexible on scheduling and can arrange sessions to suit your availability.
Does every insurer accept Pass Plus?
No — not every insurer offers a Pass Plus discount. However, the majority of major UK insurers recognise it, including Direct Line, Churchill, Admiral, and many comparison-site providers. We recommend checking with your insurer before booking, though in our experience most Manchester drivers find at least one insurer offering a meaningful discount. The DVSA publishes a list of participating insurers on their website.
Can I do Pass Plus in an automatic car?
Yes. DriveSQ offers Pass Plus in both manual and automatic cars at the same price of £35/hr. If you passed your test in an automatic, your Pass Plus will also be completed in an automatic. Your DVSA certificate will reflect this and will be accepted by participating insurers.
How soon after passing my test can I do Pass Plus?
You can start Pass Plus immediately after passing your practical test — there is no minimum wait time. In fact, the sooner the better: your skills are fresh, your confidence is at its peak from recent training, and you can present your certificate to insurers when taking out your very first policy. We can often schedule your first Pass Plus session within a few days of your test.
