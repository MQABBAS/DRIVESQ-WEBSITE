Can Learners Drive on Motorways?

Since June 2018, learner drivers can drive on motorways — but only with a DVSA-approved instructor in a dual-controlled car. You cannot practise on motorways with a friend or family member. DriveSQ offers motorway lessons on the M60, M62, and M56 from Manchester.

Manchester Motorway Network

Greater Manchester sits at the hub of four major motorways:


  • M60 — the Manchester orbital. Busiest section: Trafford Park to Stockport (over 100,000 vehicles/day)

  • M62 — east-west link to Leeds and Liverpool. Joins the M60 at junction 18

  • M56 — south to the airport and Chester. Accessed via Princess Parkway

  • M602 — short urban motorway from Eccles to Salford city centre

Joining a Motorway

Use the slip road to build speed to match motorway traffic (usually 60-70mph). Check mirrors, signal right, find a gap in lane 1, and merge smoothly. The M60 junction 5 (Princess Parkway) and junction 12 (Bredbury) are excellent practice spots — DriveSQ uses them regularly.

Lane Discipline

The golden rule: drive in the left lane unless overtaking. On the M60, "lane hogging" in lanes 2 or 3 causes congestion and is now an offence (£100 fine, 3 points). When overtaking, mirror-signal-mirror, move out, pass, mirror-signal-mirror, move back. Never undertake.

Smart Motorways Around Manchester

The M60 between junctions 8-18 and the M62 use smart motorway technology: variable speed limits (displayed on overhead gantries), hard-shoulder running during busy periods, and red X signs (lane closed — you MUST move out). Ignoring a red X is now automatically enforced by cameras.

Local Road Knowledge and Driving Education

Building local road knowledge is one of the most underestimated aspects of driver training. Knowing which junctions require extra observation, where speed limits change, and where vulnerable road users are most likely to appear turns a competent learner into a genuinely safe driver for life. DriveSQ lessons are planned around the actual roads you will drive every day after passing.

Key Driving Knowledge for Greater Manchester

  • Speed limits are maximums, not targets — always drive at the speed appropriate for the road conditions, visibility and traffic density.
  • Highway Code Rule 170 requires you to give way to pedestrians when turning into a side road if they are already crossing.
  • Roundabout lane selection must be decided before the give-way line — changing lanes inside a roundabout is one of the most common causes of serious test faults.
  • Mirror checks should happen every 5-8 seconds on open roads and before every speed change, direction change or manoeuvre.

Stopping distances: The Highway Code stopping distances — based on a 13.5-metre car in dry conditions — are the minimum legal reference points. In wet conditions, stopping distances at least double. In icy conditions, stopping distances can be ten times greater than dry distances. At 70mph on ice, a car travelling with 13.5 metres of following distance needs 750 metres to stop safely — over half a mile. These figures make a compelling case for reduced speed in adverse conditions.
