The Three Parts

The ADI Qualification — What's Involved

Part 1 — Theory Test

A computer-based test covering The Highway Code, driving theory, and hazard perception. There are 100 multiple-choice questions (you need 85/100) plus a hazard perception video test. Most candidates study for 3–6 months before sitting Part 1.

Key Tips
Use the official DVSA ADI Part 1 book
Practice with the official hazard perception software
The ADI Part 1 is harder than the standard theory test — allow enough prep time
Part 2 — Driving Ability

A 1-hour driving test assessed to the same standard as a fully qualified ADI instructor. You must demonstrate exemplary vehicle control, observation, and decision-making. Most people with a clean licence and strong driving history pass within a few months of preparation.

Key Tips
Drive at least 1–2 hours per week on varied roads in the run-up
Consider a few lessons with an ADI development coach
Common fails: speed limit violations, mirror checks, junctions
Part 3 — Instructional Ability

A 1-hour in-car assessment where you demonstrate your ability to teach driving. An examiner plays the role of a pupil at a pre-set level, and you are assessed on how well you coach them. This is the hardest part — most people need significant coaching before sitting Part 3.

Key Tips
Get dedicated Part 3 coaching from an approved trainer
Practise role-playing lessons with friends or family
Study the DVSA competency framework in detail
Career & Earnings

What Can You Earn as a Manchester ADI?

Qualified driving instructors in Manchester typically charge learners £32–£40 per lesson. Working 30–40 lessons per week (which most full-time instructors achieve), annual earnings range from £45,000 to £65,000 before vehicle and business costs. After costs, most Manchester ADIs take home £25,000–£45,000 per year.

The advantages go beyond money: you set your own hours, work in your local area, and make a real difference in people’s lives by helping them gain independence. Manchester has a consistently high demand for qualified instructors, particularly in south and east Manchester postcodes where test centre waiting times are longest.

Earn While You Train (PDI Licence)

Once you pass ADI Parts 1 and 2, you can apply for a Potential Driving Instructor (PDI) licence from DVSA. This allows you to charge for lessons for up to 6 months while you complete Part 3 training — so you can start generating income well before you are fully qualified.

PDI licences are renewable up to three times, giving you up to 18 months of paid teaching if needed. Most trainees find this removes much of the financial pressure of the qualification process.

ADI Licence Requirements
  • Full UK driving licence held for 3+ years
  • No more than 6 penalty points
  • No driving disqualification in the past 4 years
  • No conviction for dangerous or reckless driving
  • Fit and proper person check by DVSA

Local Road Knowledge and Driving Education

Building local road knowledge is one of the most underestimated aspects of driver training. Knowing which junctions require extra observation, where speed limits change, and where vulnerable road users are most likely to appear turns a competent learner into a genuinely safe driver for life. DriveSQ lessons are planned around the actual roads you will drive every day after passing.

Key Driving Knowledge for Greater Manchester

  • The DVSA's recommended following distance is a 2-second gap in dry conditions — double it in rain and triple it in snow or ice.
  • Speed limits are maximums, not targets — always drive at the speed appropriate for the road conditions, visibility and traffic density.
  • Highway Code Rule 170 requires you to give way to pedestrians when turning into a side road if they are already crossing.
  • Roundabout lane selection must be decided before the give-way line — changing lanes inside a roundabout is one of the most common causes of serious test faults.

Theory test insight: The DVSA theory test bank contains 982 questions across 14 topic categories. The hazard perception section requires identifying 14 clips with one developing hazard each, plus one clip containing two developing hazards. Candidates must respond as soon as the hazard starts to develop — the optimal window is within the first 1.75 seconds of hazard development to score maximum marks.
