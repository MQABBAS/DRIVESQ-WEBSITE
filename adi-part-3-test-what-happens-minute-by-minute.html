What happens on the ADI Part 3 test, minute by minute

Rules and fees checked on GOV.UK on 25 November 2026 · Updated 25 November 2026

The ADI Part 3 lasts about 45 minutes and you are in control of most of it. Knowing what happens, and when, removes a lot of the fear. This is the test as GOV.UK describes it, laid out as a clock.

The 45 minutes at a glance

TimeWhat happensRule from GOV.UK
0:00 to 3:00You and the pupil agree goals and discuss how risk will be managedNo more than 3 minutes
3:00 to about 43:00The pupil drives while you teachThe pupil drives for about 40 minutes
Any timeYou may stop safely to give feedbackAllowed
Last minutesYou give the pupil time to reflect on their own performanceNo more than 3 minutes

What you bring

  • A pupil. A partly trained learner, a fully trained learner or a full licence holder. A partly trained learner should be able to drive for 40 minutes without frequently stopping.
  • A suitable car. GOV.UK has separate car rules.
  • Your trainee licence. If you do not have one, bring your UK driving licence or passport.
Who cannot be your pupil: an ADI, or anyone who is preparing for the ADI Part 3 test. If you bring the wrong things, the test can be cancelled and you do not get a refund.

The first three minutes matter most

The opening discussion is short but sets up the whole lesson. In about three minutes you need to find out what the pupil wants to work on, agree a sensible plan for the lesson, and make sure they understand how responsibility for risk is shared between you. Trainees who rush this, or run it like a lecture, usually spend the next forty minutes teaching the wrong thing.

A useful way to think about it: you are not delivering a lesson you prepared at home. You are having a short conversation, and then you build a plan out of what the pupil says. See our lesson-planning guide for how to do that.

The 40-minute drive

The pupil drives and you teach. The examiner is looking for evidence that you meet the national standard for driver and rider training. Two rules are worth knowing in advance:

  • You cannot spend the whole lesson teaching a manoeuvre. A short recap of a manoeuvre the pupil has met before is fine.
  • If you need to give feedback, you may stop safely. Stopping is not a fault. Doing it badly or dangerously is.

The last three minutes

You give the pupil no more than three minutes to reflect on how it went. This is a coaching moment: the best versions ask the pupil what they did well and what they would change, before the instructor says a word.

What happens afterwards

The examiner fills in the Part 3 report form and you receive your grade and the completed report by email at the end of the test. Read the report carefully, because each of the 17 areas is scored and the report tells you where marks were lost. Our marking guide shows how those scores add up.

A practical countdown for test week

  • A week before: do two complete 45-minute lessons with a pupil who is not part of your normal practice, with someone watching.
  • The day before: check the car, your trainee licence (or licence and passport) and your pupil's availability and phone number.
  • On the day: arrive early, meet your pupil, and keep the opening conversation unhurried. You have three minutes. Use them well.

Questions people ask

How long is the ADI Part 3 test?

About 45 minutes. It begins with a discussion of goals and risk management of no more than 3 minutes, the pupil drives for about 40 minutes, and you give the pupil no more than 3 minutes to reflect at the end.

Who can I bring as a pupil for the ADI Part 3?

A partly trained learner, a fully trained learner or a full licence holder. They cannot be an ADI or someone preparing for the ADI Part 3 test.

Can I stop the car during the Part 3 to give feedback?

Yes. You may stop safely during the lesson to give immediate feedback.

When do I get my ADI Part 3 result?

You receive your grade and the completed report by email at the end of the test.

What happens if I forget my trainee licence?

If you do not have a trainee licence, bring your UK driving licence or passport. If you bring the wrong things, the test can be cancelled with no refund.
