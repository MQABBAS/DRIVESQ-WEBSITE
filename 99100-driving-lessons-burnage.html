Key Driving Knowledge for Greater Manchester

  • Speed limits are maximums, not targets — always drive at the speed appropriate for the road conditions, visibility and traffic density.
  • Highway Code Rule 170 requires you to give way to pedestrians when turning into a side road if they are already crossing.
  • Roundabout lane selection must be decided before the give-way line — changing lanes inside a roundabout is one of the most common causes of serious test faults.
  • Mirror checks should happen every 5-8 seconds on open roads and before every speed change, direction change or manoeuvre.

Hazard Perception Science

The hazard perception test requires candidates to identify developing hazards — situations that would cause a driver to take action, such as slowing, stopping, swerving or changing position. The test uses 14 video clips, each approximately one minute long, filmed from a driver's perspective in real traffic. One clip contains two developing hazards rather than one — this clip is not identified in advance. Scoring is based on response time: a response within the first second of hazard development scores five points, falling to four, three, two or one point as response time increases, with zero points for responses outside the scoring window. A score of at least 44 out of 75 is required to pass. Research has shown that real-world driving experience before the hazard perception test significantly improves scores — even non-driving passengers who focus on hazard identification during car journeys develop faster response patterns. Clicking rapidly and repeatedly (clutching) to beat the system triggers a fraud detection algorithm that eliminates the score for that clip entirely — a deliberate trap built into the test software.

Junctions and Emerging

  • Box junctions (yellow grid markings) must not be entered unless your exit is clear — the only exception is turning right when stopped by oncoming traffic or by a vehicle waiting to turn right.
  • Unmarked junctions (no give-way lines, no priority signs) are governed by the general rule of equal priority — treat them as a courtesy situation where common sense and eye contact determine who proceeds first.
  • Give-way lines at junctions require the vehicle to stop if necessary — unlike a stop line, a give-way allows you to proceed without stopping if the road is clearly free. However, many learners mistake 'can proceed without stopping' for 'should not slow significantly'.
  • At T-junctions, the MSM routine begins 100 metres before the junction — not at the junction itself. Early mirror checks and positioning give following drivers maximum warning.

DVSA Practical Test — What You Need to Know

The eyesight test at the start of a DVSA practical test requires the candidate to read a standard number plate from 20 metres. This equates to approximately 5 car lengths. Candidates who wear glasses or contact lenses must wear them during the test — failure to do so when the eyesight check result requires correction constitutes grounds for terminating the test. Candidates who fail the eyesight test do not receive a refund of their test fee.

After You Pass — What New Drivers Need to Know

The green novice driver plate (displayed voluntarily by new drivers) has no legal status in England, Scotland and Wales but is widely respected by other drivers. Northern Ireland requires new drivers to display R plates for two years after passing. The DVSA recommends new drivers consider using N-plates or green P-plates for at least three months to encourage following drivers to give additional space and patience. The plate also serves as a self-reminder — research shows drivers who display a novice plate in the first months after passing drive more carefully and have fewer incidents.
