Test Centre

Oldham Driving Test Centre

The DTC Failsworth students use — and the things no national driving school bothers to tell you about it.

Address: Bromley Street, Oldham OL8 4PQ — approximately 3 miles from Failsworth town centre via the A62
Test Duration: Approximately 38–40 minutes driving + pre-test briefing
Getting There by Bus: Routes 181 and 182 stop near the DTC on Bromley Street — check times in advance and allow at least 15 minutes buffer before your test slot
Documents: Provisional licence, theory pass certificate (within 2 years), glasses if prescribed for driving
Test Fee: £62 weekday / £75 weekend or bank holiday (DVSA 2025 rates)
The exit every candidate must know: The DTC exit onto Bromley Street gives onto a busy junction with a left-turn-only approach. This tight-radius exit clips the kerb if candidates do not position early in the lane before turning. Examiners observe this immediately after pull-away. DriveSQ students practise the Bromley Street exit approach in the lessons leading up to their test date.
Bay Parking Manoeuvre: Bay parking in the Oldham DTC car park is frequently tested. The bays are on a slight uphill gradient — the bay is on the right side of the car park. Vehicle creep during a reverse bay is a common fault on this gradient. Manual candidates must manage clutch control; automatic candidates must maintain firm brake pressure throughout the manoeuvre.
Parallel Parking Alternative: Parallel parking on Bromley Street itself is used as the manoeuvre alternative. The on-street position requires careful observation for buses on routes 181 and 182 which use this stretch — candidates should check mirrors and blind spot before opening the door after completing the manoeuvre.
First routes from the DTC: Most test routes from Oldham DTC head immediately into busy A-road traffic on the A62 or through the Oldham town junction network. Do not treat the departure from the DTC as a warm-up — the examiner's assessment is live from the moment you move off.
Real Test Routes

The 7 Failsworth Test Routes
That Examiners Actually Use

Mapped from real test experience. Our students practise every one of these before their test day.

1
HGV Traffic

Oldham Road A62 Southbound

The A62 runs southbound from Oldham toward Newton Heath through a designated HGV route. The 40mph dual carriageway section near Medlock Vale carries heavy lorry traffic throughout the day — lorries create blind spots and slow acceleration on merges. The slip-road merge from Medlock Vale onto the dual carriageway requires confident speed matching. Hesitation causes following vehicle pressure and examiner observation. DriveSQ students build A62 dual carriageway confidence over multiple lessons before test day.

📍 A62 Oldham Road southbound: Failsworth → Medlock Vale slip-road → Newton Heath border
2
Camera Zones

Broadway M35 — School Zones

Broadway is the residential spine road through Failsworth town, used by bus route 83. Two 20mph camera-enforced zones operate outside Failsworth School and St John's RC Primary during school hours. The zones are clearly signed but the 8:30am activation catches candidates who arrive early for lessons and assume the 30mph limit still applies. Candidates who have not specifically been told where the 20mph sections begin are caught every time. DriveSQ students know every camera point on Broadway before their first test day.

📍 Broadway M35: Failsworth town centre spine road, 20mph zones at Failsworth School and St John's RC Primary
3
Speed Humps

Hollins Road M40 — Moston

Hollins Road through Moston carries bus route 89 and has speed humps at irregular intervals along its length. Smooth approach at the correct speed — 15 to 20mph through each hump — is what examiners assess. Both approaching too fast and applying unnecessary heavy braking are noted as driving faults. Parked cars near the Moston Lane junction restrict the effective road width and require active mirror-signal-manoeuvre sequencing. DriveSQ introduces Hollins Road speed humps and the Moston Lane restriction from the early lessons.

📍 Hollins Road M40: Moston — speed humps throughout, bus route 89, Moston Lane junction width restriction
4
Mixed Speed Limits

Manchester Road A62 Northbound

A62 northbound into Failsworth passes through mixed 30mph and 40mph speed change zones at the Medlock Vale bridge. The speed limit change is clearly signed but candidates who miss the transition continue at 30mph into a 40mph zone — excessive caution at 30mph in a 40mph section is recorded as a fault by examiners, as is speeding. Cyclists use the advisory cycle lane on this section. Safe passing distance and mirror checks before passing cyclists are specifically assessed here.

📍 A62 northbound: Medlock Vale bridge speed change 30/40mph, advisory cycle lane, cyclists year-round
5
Junction Sequence

Lees Road OL8 — Limeside / Hathershaw

Lees Road through Limeside and Hathershaw has junction complexity at Hollins Road and a residential side-road Give Way sequence with multiple unmarked T-junctions where the priority is not immediately obvious. Many Give Way lines are faded — candidates must apply the "give way to the right" default rule correctly when line markings are unclear. This is a skill that has to be specifically taught, not assumed. DriveSQ students are taught the priority rule as a fallback before they encounter any of the faded Give Ways on the OL8 route.

📍 Lees Road OL8: Limeside → Hathershaw, multiple faded Give Way lines, Hollins Road junction
6
4-Lane Arterial

Rochdale Road A664 — Moston

Rochdale Road through Moston is a busy arterial road with a 4-lane section at Nuthurst Road junction. Correct lane selection for straight-on and right-turn manoeuvres at this junction is critical — many candidates take the wrong lane and either obstruct other vehicles or are forced into a lane change mid-junction. Multiple pedestrian crossings on this stretch are in regular use. DriveSQ students practise the Nuthurst Road junction specifically, including the correct lane for every direction, before their test.

📍 A664 Rochdale Road: 4-lane section at Nuthurst Road junction, multiple pedestrian crossings, busy arterial
7
Manoeuvre Zone

Oldham DTC Bay Parking

Reversing into the bay on the right side of the Oldham DTC car park — the most frequently tested manoeuvre at this centre. The slight uphill gradient means the car will creep forward on the slope during the manoeuvre if not held correctly. Manual candidates must use clutch control or apply the handbrake to prevent roll; automatic candidates must maintain consistent brake pressure. Parallel parking on Bromley Street outside the DTC is the alternative manoeuvre — candidates must observe for buses on routes 181 and 182 before and after. Our students practise the specific gradient and Bromley Street location, not a generic car park elsewhere.

📍 Oldham DTC car park: right-side bay on uphill gradient · parallel park alternative on Bromley Street

Local Knowledge

8 Failsworth Hazards Other
Schools Never Cover

The specific, local details that separate a DriveSQ Failsworth student from someone who learned without local knowledge.

Oldham Road A62 HGV Traffic

The A62 is a designated HGV route between Manchester and Oldham. Lorries create blind spots and slow merges. Speed on the 40mph dual carriageway section near Medlock Vale must be maintained confidently — hesitation causes following vehicle pressure and the examiner records a lack of progress fault. DriveSQ students are introduced to HGV traffic and dual carriageway speed maintenance on the A62 specifically, not on a quiet dual carriageway elsewhere that doesn't represent the test conditions.

Broadway M35 School Zones — Camera Enforced

Two 20mph camera-enforced zones operate outside Failsworth School and St John's RC Primary on Broadway during school hours. The zones are clearly signed but the 8:30am activation catches candidates who arrive early for lessons and assume the 30mph limit still applies. This is not an advisory 20mph — it is camera-enforced and the examiner will record a speeding fault if the candidate continues through at 30mph. Every DriveSQ student knows the exact start and end of both school zones on Broadway before their test.

Hollins Road Speed Humps

Hollins Road through Moston has speed humps at irregular intervals. The correct approach speed through the hump is 15 to 20mph — both too fast and unnecessary braking are noted by examiners as lack of smoothness faults. The irregular spacing means candidates cannot predict when the next hump appears, which rewards those who have specifically driven the road before. DriveSQ introduces Hollins Road humps in lesson 2 and explains the correct approach speed at each one.

Medlock Vale Pedestrian Bridge

The Medlock Vale bridge area on the A62 has a pedestrian crossing with irregular activation patterns. The crossing button is used frequently by walkers accessing the Medlock Valley Way footpath — including ramblers and dog walkers, especially at weekends and in the morning. The crossing can activate at any point during a test, including during the dual carriageway approach. Candidates who have only driven the A62 at speed and never encountered the pedestrian crossing activation are caught unprepared. DriveSQ covers the Medlock Vale crossing specifically.

Rochdale Road A664 4-Lane Section

The 4-lane section of Rochdale Road at Nuthurst Road junction in Moston requires correct lane selection for straight-on and right-turn manoeuvres. Many candidates take the wrong lane here — ending up in a straight-on lane when turning right, or in a right-turn lane when going straight. The result is either a lane obstruction fault or an incorrect turn that adds time and pressure to the rest of the test. Only local instructors with direct experience of this junction can walk a student through the exact lane selection for every direction.

Dean Lane Industrial Traffic

Dean Lane in Newton Heath (M11) has light industrial traffic from adjacent estates. Delivery vehicles double-park regularly, creating dynamic width restrictions that require anticipation not reaction. A candidate who has never driven Dean Lane before will encounter a double-parked lorry at speed with insufficient time to plan. DriveSQ students are taught to read Dean Lane ahead at a distance — identifying delivery vehicles before they become an emergency manoeuvre — as part of the Newton Heath route preparation.

Lees Road Priority Junctions

Lees Road OL8 has multiple unmarked T-junctions onto residential streets where the priority is not obvious. Give Way lines are sometimes faded — candidates who rely entirely on road markings to determine priority are caught when the markings are absent. The correct default is "give way to the right" when no markings are visible. This rule has to be specifically taught before a candidate encounters faded markings for the first time on test day. DriveSQ students know the rule and have applied it on Lees Road before they sit their test.

Failsworth Metrolink Stop — Tram Signals

Failsworth station on the Oldham/Rochdale Metrolink branch is at-grade on Oldham Road. Tram signal rules — including the flashing amber obligation — apply at the stop. Unlike inner-city stops, the Failsworth stop is on a 30mph road where cars approach at higher speed and tram signals appear more suddenly than in low-speed city centre environments. The frequency of trams at peak hours is high. Candidates who have not specifically been taught tram signal procedure will not know what to do when the amber flashing sequence begins mid-approach.
