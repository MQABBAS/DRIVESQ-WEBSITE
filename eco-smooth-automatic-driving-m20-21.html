Automatic Driving Lessons — Essential Knowledge

Automatic driving lessons remove the complexity of clutch and gear management, allowing learners to focus entirely on road observation, positioning and hazard response. The following evidence-based tips represent the most common areas where learner drivers in Greater Manchester gain or lose marks during training and testing.

  • An automatic vehicle with a manual gear-paddle option (tiptronic) allows engine braking in the same way as a manual — use the paddles on descents to reduce wear on your disc brakes.
  • The DSA practical test in an automatic car has identical assessment criteria to a manual test — all the same hazard perception, observation and road positioning skills are assessed.
  • Automatic licence restriction is permanent — if you pass in an automatic, you are restricted to automatics until you take and pass an additional manual practical test.
  • In an automatic car, creep control (releasing the brake gently) gives precise slow-speed control in car parks, tight manoeuvres and stop-start traffic — learn this technique in your first two lessons.
  • Sport mode in modern automatics holds gears longer and sharpens throttle response — use it when joining dual carriageways or motorways to ensure adequate acceleration, then return to Drive mode once at speed.

Highway Code update 2022: The Highway Code was substantially updated in January 2022 with a new hierarchy of road users placing pedestrians at the top and motorists at the bottom. Key changes include: pedestrians have priority at junctions when drivers are turning; cyclists may use the centre of a lane; and 'Dutch reach' door-opening technique is now recommended. All DriveSQ learners are taught the updated 2022 rules.
