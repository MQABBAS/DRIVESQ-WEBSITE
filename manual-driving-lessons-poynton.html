Road-by-Road Breakdown

Every Road Type Poynton Has to Offer

London Road A523 — Dual Carriageway Discipline

The A523 between Hazel Grove and Macclesfield clips the eastern edge of Poynton with a national speed limit section before reducing to 40mph through the village. We use the Waterloo Road junction — a right-turn across a fast carriageway — as a key observation exercise. You'll practise matching speed on entry, reading the central reservation for safe crossing opportunities, and signalling out of the merge lane at Poynton Roundabout. The A523 / Park Lane junction at the south end of the village introduces a filter-lane right turn that appears on many Stockport DVSA test routes, so mastering its timing is direct test preparation.

Park Lane & Poynton Village Centre

Park Lane forms the high street spine of Poynton, narrowing to a single effective lane between parked vehicles outside the Co-op and the pharmacy. You'll practise the essential give-way-to-oncoming discipline, reading pedestrian flow from the supermarket car park, and holding a smooth position around the bends near the war memorial. The Park Lane / Chester Road / London Road three-way priority junction at the south end requires accurate LADA (Look, Assess, Decide, Act) — the central island is small and the approach angle deceives many learners about who has priority. We break this down clearly in early lessons.

Dickens Lane & Woodford Road — Rural Transitions

Dickens Lane runs north from Poynton village toward Woodford in an S-curve through open farmland. The road narrows to 4.5m in places, with passing places informally used by locals rather than formally signed — requiring accurate judgement of vehicle width and confident use of the left half of the road. We extend lessons up to Woodford Road where the B5358 carries faster traffic from the A5102 roundabout near Bramhall. This junction, uncontrolled except by STOP markings, demands genuine gap selection at 50mph-equivalent traffic — an excellent confidence-builder before test day.

Coppice Road / Brook Lane — Beginner Foundation Estate

The residential grid south of Poynton village — Coppice Road, Brook Lane, Chapel Lane, and Shrigley Road — is where all DriveSQ beginners in SK12 start. These 20–30mph streets have light traffic, clear road markings, and short straight sections ideal for establishing clutch bite-point, mirror discipline, and smooth braking. Bay parking bays in the Poynton Leisure Centre car park off Yew Tree Lane give us a flat, spacious area for perpendicular, angled, and parallel park exercises with no time pressure. Once you're confident here, we move to the village centre and A523 work.
