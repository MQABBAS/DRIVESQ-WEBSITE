Night and All-Weather Driving — Essential Knowledge

British roads present their most challenging conditions after dark, in rain and in fog — building all-weather confidence before your test is an investment that pays every time you drive. The following evidence-based tips represent the most common areas where learner drivers in Greater Manchester gain or lose marks during training and testing.

  • Night driving is the most common context for driver fatigue incidents — never begin a long night journey after a full day's work; if you feel drowsy within 15 minutes of a motorway junction, pull in.
  • Dipped headlights must be used from sunset to sunrise and whenever visibility is reduced to below 100 metres — do not wait until it is fully dark to switch on your headlights.
  • In rain, the stopping distance is at least double the dry-weather figure — at 30mph allow a minimum 6-second gap instead of the 2-second dry weather gap.
  • Aquaplaning occurs when a tyre rides on a layer of water — if you feel the steering go light, ease off the accelerator gently and maintain steering direction until grip returns.
  • Fog lights (front and rear) should only be used when visibility drops below 100 metres — using fog lights in light rain or drizzle is illegal and can result in a fixed penalty notice.

Stopping distances: The Highway Code stopping distances — based on a 13.5-metre car in dry conditions — are the minimum legal reference points. In wet conditions, stopping distances at least double. In icy conditions, stopping distances can be ten times greater than dry distances. At 70mph on ice, a car travelling with 13.5 metres of following distance needs 750 metres to stop safely — over half a mile. These figures make a compelling case for reduced speed in adverse conditions.
