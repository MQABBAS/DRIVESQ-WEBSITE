Test Centre

Bury Driving Test Centre

The DTC Bury students use — and the things no driving school bothers to tell you about it.

Address: Angouleme Way, Bury BL9 0EL — in the Bury town centre retail area, directly behind the Millgate shopping centre
Test Duration: Approximately 38–40 minutes driving + pre-test briefing
Parking: The DTC car park is used for the bay parking manoeuvre — do not park in a test bay on arrival. Public parking is in the Bury Interchange multi-storey, a 3-minute walk. The Metrolink Bury Line terminates at Bury Interchange, 200 metres from the DTC — examiners are familiar with candidates arriving by tram.
Documents: Provisional licence, theory pass certificate (within 2 years), glasses if prescribed for driving
Test Fee: £62 weekday / £75 weekend (DVSA 2025 rates)
The car park slope: The DTC car park has a downward slope toward the rear of the car park where bay parking is conducted. Candidates must prevent roll-back — the slope is steeper than it appears from the driver's seat. Manual candidates must hold on the clutch bite or apply the handbrake. This is the detail most candidates are never told about before their test.
Bury Interchange: Bury Interchange (bus station and Metrolink terminus) is immediately adjacent to the DTC. The Metrolink Bury Line terminates here — trams, buses, cyclists, and pedestrians converge in a compact shared-use zone. Many test routes pass through the Interchange approach within the first few minutes.
Retail park access: Angouleme Way is a busy retail park access road serving the Millgate shopping centre. Candidates leaving the DTC car park immediately enter shared pedestrian and vehicle space. The examiner's assessment begins the moment you select gear — not when you exit the car park.
Pass rate: Bury DTC is around the national average. Strong local road knowledge — especially tram crossing rules, The Rock gyratory, and the DTC car park slope — is the key differentiator between prepared and unprepared candidates.
Real Test Routes

The 7 Bury Test Routes
That Examiners Actually Use

Mapped from real test experience. Our students practise every one of these before their test day.

1
Tram Crossings

Manchester Road A56 Southbound — Metrolink Section

The A56 dual carriageway leaving Bury town centre southbound toward Whitefield runs at a 40mph limit with Metrolink tram tracks running alongside. Tram stops at Besses o' th' Barn and Whitefield have flashing-amber signals — the same rules as all Metrolink crossings: flashing amber means you may pass carefully if the crossing is clear; you must not stop on tram tracks; do not overtake a tram at a stop where passengers are boarding or alighting. Examiners observe tram crossing behaviour on every route using the A56. This is the section most candidates from outside Bury are unprepared for.

📍 A56 southbound from Bury → Besses o' th' Barn Metrolink → Whitefield
2
Hill Starts

Walmersley Road B6222 / Jericho

North from Bury town centre up Walmersley Road, climbing steadily toward Walmersley village. Hill starts on the junctions approaching Jericho are a significant test of clutch control in manual vehicles. The road narrows at Woodhill Fold where parked cars force alternating priority — candidates must read the situation correctly and decide who has right of way without hesitation. This section rewards local knowledge: candidates unfamiliar with Woodhill Fold often either stop unnecessarily or push through when they should wait.

📍 Walmersley Road B6222 north from Bury → Jericho → Woodhill Fold narrow section
3
A-Road Speed

Ramsbottom Road A56 Northbound

A56 northbound toward Ramsbottom has a 40mph national speed limit stretch after the Bury North Metrolink stop. Candidates often drive too slowly here — undue hesitancy on a 40mph road is a fault as readily as speeding. The road passes Burrs Country Park where pedestrian crossing activity is unpredictable: walkers using the Irwell Valley trail frequently activate the crossing, and the timing can surprise candidates who are focused on maintaining speed. Anticipating the crossing well ahead is how DriveSQ students handle it.

📍 A56 northbound from Bury North → Burrs Country Park pedestrian crossing → Ramsbottom
4
Priority Trap

Bolton Road A58 / Walshaw Road

West from Bury town centre on the A58 toward Bolton, the Walshaw Road junction is a staggered crossroads that consistently misleads candidates into taking priority at the wrong point — those unfamiliar with the layout give way when they have priority, or proceed when they should give way. Additionally, the 30mph to 40mph speed limit change on the A58 past the Asda roundabout is a known trap: candidates in residential-driving mode miss the limit change and drive 30mph in a 40mph zone — an undue hesitancy fault.

📍 Bolton Road A58 west → Walshaw Road staggered crossroads → A58 past Asda roundabout
5
Rural / Residential

Brandlesholme Road / Tottington

Northeast from Bury through quiet residential roads then semi-rural roads toward Tottington village. The 30mph zones and blind bends on Brandlesholme Road after the golf course require a measured approach speed — candidates who have only driven wider suburban roads find the narrowing and bends stressful. Single-track sections require candidates to reverse for approaching vehicles, which is assessed as a decision-making exercise: the examiner observes whether you initiate the reverse promptly and execute it safely.

📍 Brandlesholme Road northeast → past golf course → Tottington village single-track
6
One-Way System

Bury Town Centre / The Rock Gyratory

The Rock shopping centre creates a large one-way gyratory around Bury town centre. Union Street, Princess Street, and Bolton Street form the one-way system that candidates unfamiliar with Bury town layout frequently navigate incorrectly — taking the wrong exit, missing the correct lane for a turn, or failing to observe pedestrianised zones at the shopping park boundary. This is a route where map knowledge and confident lane selection matter more than driving skill alone. DriveSQ students practise the gyratory until it is automatic.

📍 The Rock gyratory: Union Street → Princess Street → Bolton Street one-way system
7
Manoeuvre Zone

Angouleme Way Bay Parking — DTC Car Park

Reverse bay parking in the DTC car park on Angouleme Way is the most commonly assessed manoeuvre at Bury DTC. The downward slope toward the rear of the car park means candidates must prevent roll-back throughout the manoeuvre — the slope is steeper than it appears from the driver's seat, and many candidates are surprised by the creep when they release the brake. Our students practise this specific car park before their test so the slope and reference points are familiar, not a shock under examiner observation.

📍 Angouleme Way DTC car park · Downward slope toward rear bays · Roll-back prevention essential

Local Knowledge

8 Bury Hazards Other
Schools Never Cover

The specific, local details that separate a DriveSQ Bury student from someone who learned without local knowledge.

A56 Metrolink Tram Crossings — Flashing Amber

At Besses o' th' Barn and Whitefield tram stops on the A56, the signal includes a flashing-amber phase unique to Metrolink crossings. Flashing amber means the tram stop is active but you may proceed with caution if the crossing is clear of passengers boarding or alighting. Most candidates have never encountered this signal before their test and either stop unnecessarily (blocking the carriageway) or drive through without checking. Neither response is correct. DriveSQ teaches the full tram signal sequence from lesson one — because it appears on the A56 within minutes of leaving Bury DTC.

Bolton Road A58 Staggered Crossroads — Wrong Priority Trap

The Walshaw Road junction on the A58 is a staggered crossroads — the left-right offset means the priority arrangement is not immediately obvious. Candidates who read it as a simple crossroads frequently yield when they have priority, or proceed when they should give way. Either error in front of an examiner generates a serious fault. DriveSQ covers the A58 staggered crossroads explicitly, explaining who has priority at each approach before candidates drive it for the first time.

A58 Speed Limit Change — 30mph to 40mph Past Asda

The A58 Bolton Road changes from 30mph to 40mph after the Asda roundabout heading west. Candidates in residential-road mode miss the limit-change sign and continue at 30mph in a 40mph zone — generating undue hesitancy faults. This is one of the most common fault triggers on the Bury DTC Bolton Road route. Our students are told exactly where the limit changes before they drive the road, not when they're trying to manage a junction at the same time.

The Rock Gyratory — Bury Town Centre One-Way System

The Rock shopping centre creates a substantial one-way gyratory around Bury town centre. Union Street, Princess Street, and Bolton Street form a loop that candidates unfamiliar with Bury town layout frequently get wrong — taking the wrong exit, using the wrong lane, or driving into a pedestrianised zone boundary at the shopping park. There are no shortcuts here: the only preparation is driving the gyratory until the exits are automatic. DriveSQ students practise it repeatedly before test day.

Walmersley Road Hill Starts — Woodhill Fold Narrow Section

Walmersley Road climbs from Bury town centre toward Walmersley village with junctions approaching Jericho requiring uphill stops and restarts. The Woodhill Fold section narrows where parked cars reduce the road to single-file — give-way decisions must be made correctly without hesitation. Candidates who have only practised hill starts on flat test-centre car parks are consistently underprepared for the combination of a hill junction and a narrowing road at Woodhill Fold.

Brandlesholme Road Blind Bends After Golf Course

Brandlesholme Road northeast of Bury has a series of blind bends after the golf course on the approach to Tottington. The 30mph limit applies throughout, but the correct approach speed for the blind bends is lower than 30mph — candidates who drive at the limit through the bends risk being unable to stop within their vision distance if a vehicle appears from the opposite direction. DriveSQ covers the appropriate observation technique and speed management for this specific section.

Burrs Country Park Pedestrian Crossing — Unpredictable Timing

The pedestrian crossing near Burrs Country Park on the A56 northbound is activated frequently and often without the advance warning a regular crossing would give — walkers arriving from the Irwell Valley trail press the button and step out quickly. Candidates focused on maintaining 40mph on the A56 northbound are often caught by a red crossing signal they didn't anticipate. DriveSQ teaches candidates to treat the Burrs Country Park crossing as a high-activation hazard from well before it comes into view.

DTC Car Park Downward Slope — Reverse Bay Roll-Back Risk

The Bury DTC car park on Angouleme Way slopes downward toward the rear bays where reverse bay parking is assessed. A car in neutral will creep forward (i.e., back into the bay direction) if the brake is released mid-manoeuvre. Manual candidates must maintain clutch control or apply the handbrake throughout. Automatic candidates still need consistent brake pressure. The slope is steeper than it looks from the driver's seat — candidates who have only practised bay parking on flat surfaces are regularly surprised by it. This is the one detail about Bury DTC that almost no driving school tells candidates before test day.

Know Bury Before Your Test

The context that turns a technically capable driver into a locally aware one — and locally aware drivers pass.

🛒

Bury Market

One of the largest outdoor markets in the north of England. Wednesday and Saturday market days increase pedestrian and vehicle activity significantly in the town centre. The Rock gyratory and Bolton Street are heavily affected. Examiners avoid these peak times but candidates should know how the roads behave during busy periods — lesson scheduling around market days is something DriveSQ instructors plan for.

🚈

Metrolink Bury Line Terminus

The Bury line terminates at Bury Interchange. The tram route from Manchester Victoria runs alongside the A56 through Whitefield and Besses o' th' Barn. Tram crossings on the A56 use flashing-amber signals requiring give-way to trams, not stopping on tram tracks. This is the most important piece of local knowledge for any Bury DTC candidate — and it applies from the first five minutes of the test.

🌳

Burrs Country Park

A popular walking and cycling area off the A56 northbound. Pedestrian crossings near the park entrance are activated frequently; cyclists enter the road unexpectedly near the Irwell Valley trail junction. Examiners use this stretch because it requires genuine anticipation skills, not just reactive driving. Knowing the park is there — and that the crossing activates without much warning — changes how you approach that section of road.

🏢

The Rock Shopping Centre

Bury's main retail area creates a large one-way gyratory. Delivery vehicles, buses, and pedestrians converge near Union Street and Princess Street throughout the day. The pedestrianised zone boundary at Bury Shopping Park is clearly marked but easy to miss when navigating under test pressure. DriveSQ students drive the gyratory in lessons until the exits, lane choices, and pedestrianised boundaries are second nature.

🚂

East Lancashire Railway

The preserved steam railway operates weekend services from Bury to Rawtenstall. There is a level crossing on Bolton Street near Bury station. Examiners use this crossing on certain routes — candidates must treat it as a standard level crossing: check lights, stop if amber shows, do not cross until the road is clear. The East Lancashire Railway is not a scheduled service so crossing activation is unpredictable — a specific reason to know it is there before test day.

⚽

Bury FC — Gigg Lane

Football match days (primarily Saturdays, post-reinstatement) increase traffic on Wellington Road and Gigg Lane significantly. Not a test-day concern for most candidates as examiners avoid the area during matches, but relevant for lesson scheduling — avoid booking lessons in the Wellington Road / Gigg Lane area on match days. DriveSQ instructors know the fixture calendar and plan accordingly.

What are the Metrolink tram rules on Bury test routes?
The A56 Metrolink section uses flashing-amber signals at tram stops. Flashing amber means you may pass carefully if the crossing is clear. You must not stop on the tram tracks at any point.
