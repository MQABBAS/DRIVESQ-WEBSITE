The DVSA ADI Part 3 report form, box by box

Rules and fees checked on GOV.UK on 25 November 2026 · Updated 25 November 2026

When your ADI Part 3 ends, the examiner has filled in a two-page DVSA report form, and you get a copy by email. Most trainees read the total and stop. The rest of the form tells you exactly where marks were won and lost. Here is every part of it, in plain English, based on the DVSA's own form.

Source: the DVSA ADI Part 3 test report form (version dated September 2022). DVSA can change the form, so compare this with the current one.

Page 1: the lesson and the scoring

The header boxes

BoxWhat it is for

The lesson boxes

The examiner records the type of pupil and the theme of the lesson you taught:

  • Pupil type: partly trained, trained, full licence holder (new) or full licence holder (experienced). The form abbreviates the last two as FLH.
  • Lesson theme: junctions; town and city driving; interaction with other road users; dual carriageway and faster-moving roads; defensive driving; effective use of mirrors; rural roads; motorways; eco-safe driving; recap of a manoeuvre; commentary; recap of an emergency stop; independent driving; or other.

See our guide to choosing your pupil and lesson theme.

The 17 scored areas

Each area has four boxes: 0 (no evidence), 1 (demonstrated in a few elements), 2 (demonstrated in most elements), 3 (demonstrated in all elements). The areas fall into three sections, each with a total score:

SectionAreasWhat the examiner looks at
Lesson planning4The pupil's goals and needs, an agreed and suitable lesson structure, suitable practice areas, and adapting the plan
Risk management5Shared responsibility for risk, clear directions, awareness, timely intervention, feedback on safety-critical incidents
Teaching and learning strategies8Teaching style, pupil analysing problems, clarifying outcomes, accurate information, feedback, answering queries, non-discriminatory manner, end-of-lesson reflection

The form ends page 1 with a total overall score. Use the score calculator to add up your own practice lessons the same way.

Page 2: the review

Question on the formWhat a Yes means
Did the candidate score 7 or less on risk management?Automatic fail
Did the candidate behave in a way that put the examiner, the pupil or anyone else in immediate danger, so the lesson had to be stopped?Automatic fail
Was advice given to seek further development?The examiner advised you to get more training. If not, the form records a reason

The page also has space for the examiner's feedback to you. Read it twice: it is the most specific advice you will get.

How to read your own report in ten minutes

  1. Check the total against the bands: 0 to 30 fail, 31 to 42 Grade B, 43 to 51 Grade A.
  2. Check the risk management total. If it is 7 or less out of 15, that alone is a fail.
  3. List every area scored 0 or 1. Those are your gaps.
  4. Group the gaps by section. A cluster in one section is your training plan.
  5. Read the examiner's feedback and the development advice, and write your own plan.
  6. If you failed, see the retake rules before you book again.

Questions people ask

What is on the ADI Part 3 report form?

Your details and the test details, an insurance declaration, the pupil type and lesson theme, 17 areas scored 0 to 3 in three sections, and a review page with the automatic-fail questions and feedback.

How many marks is each area worth?

Each of the 17 areas is scored 0 to 3, so the maximum total is 51.

What does a score of 1 mean on the form?

The form describes the four scores as: 0 no evidence, 1 demonstrated in a few elements, 2 demonstrated in most elements and 3 demonstrated in all elements.

How many areas are in risk management?

Five, so the most you can score is 15. A score of 7 or less is an automatic fail.

Where do I get my completed report?

GOV.UK says you receive your grade and the completed report by email at the end of the test.
