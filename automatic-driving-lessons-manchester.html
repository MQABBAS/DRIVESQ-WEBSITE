Straight Talking

The Truth About Automatic Licences

🔵 Does an automatic licence restrict you?

Yes — and we'll be straight with you about it. If you pass your driving test in an automatic car, your licence will be restricted to automatic vehicles only. You will not be legally permitted to drive a manual car without taking a further practical test.

However, this is far less of a limitation than many people think. Here's the reality in 2026: the majority of new cars sold in the UK are now either automatic or electric (electric vehicles are, by design, fully automatic). Most car hire, fleet vehicles, and company cars now come in automatic. The landscape of driving has changed.

Many people — particularly city drivers in Manchester — drive exclusively in automatic cars their entire driving lives without ever needing a manual. If you genuinely want to drive manual vehicles eventually, we'll tell you to learn manual from the start. But for the vast majority of learners, an automatic licence is completely sufficient for a lifetime of confident, independent driving.

🟢 "Is learning automatic cheating?"

No — and this attitude is outdated and unhelpful. Passing your driving test in an automatic car requires exactly the same standard of observation, hazard awareness, road positioning, and decision-making as a manual test. The examiner does not give you an easier ride because your car shifts gears automatically.

This stigma originates from an era when automatic cars were far less common. Today, it simply does not hold up. Every licence — manual or automatic — represents the same legal standard of competence for safe road use. Driving in automatic is a legitimate, sensible choice for millions of drivers worldwide.

Manchester city centre features multi-lane junctions, busy tram crossings, one-way systems around Piccadilly and Deansgate, and constantly changing traffic signals. For a learner managing clutch, gears, mirrors, signals and road positioning simultaneously, it can feel overwhelming.

Common Questions

Automatic Driving Lessons — FAQs

Questions about automatic lessons? We've answered the most common ones honestly below.

Does DriveSQ charge more for automatic driving lessons? +

No — never. DriveSQ charges £35/hr for both manual and automatic lessons. Most driving schools add a £5–£10/hr surcharge for automatic cars. We've never done that. Our 5-lesson automatic pack is £162 and our 10-lesson pack is £330 — both the same as manual. No premium, no small print.

Can I drive a manual car after passing in automatic? +

No — if you pass your driving test in an automatic car, your licence will show an automatic restriction (category code 78). You would need to take a separate practical test in a manual car to have this restriction removed. However, many people find this perfectly fine. Most modern cars, especially electric vehicles, are automatic. If driving a manual is important to your plans, we'll recommend you learn in a manual from the start — we'll always be honest with you about this.

How many automatic lessons do I need? +

The DVSA's national average is around 45 hours for all learners, but many automatic learners progress faster because there is one less major skill to master (clutch control). Typically, motivated learners with no prior experience are test-ready in 20–35 hours of automatic lessons. Our instructors assess you honestly in your first lesson and give a realistic projection — we never drag lessons out.

Are automatic driving lessons harder than manual? +

No — automatic lessons are generally considered easier. You do not need to manage the clutch, biting point, or gear changes. This means less to coordinate and more mental capacity to focus on hazard awareness, observation, positioning and road reading — which are the real skills being assessed on test day. Many learners find they feel more confident and progress more quickly in automatic.

Can I do Pass Plus in an automatic car? +

Yes — Pass Plus is available in automatic cars with DriveSQ. The six modules (town driving, all-weather driving, out-of-town roads, dual carriageways, night driving, motorways) are identical whether you drive manual or automatic. You receive the same certificate, which some insurers recognise for discounts. WhatsApp us to enquire.

Is automatic easier to pass the driving test in? +

The driving test pass rate for automatic learners can be higher — largely because removing clutch and gear management eliminates a significant source of errors and nerves. You still need to demonstrate full competence in all areas: observation, road positioning, signalling, manoeuvres and hazard awareness. But many learners find their test performance is stronger in automatic because they are less stressed and can focus entirely on driving the road, not the car.
