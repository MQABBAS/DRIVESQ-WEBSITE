Test Centre

Manchester (Stretford) Driving Test Centre

The primary DTC Chorlton students use — and the specific details no other driving school bothers to tell you about it.

Primary Address: 210 Barton Road, Stretford M32 8JA — approximately 10 minutes from Chorlton M21
Alternative DTC: Withington Driving Test Centre, 89 Mauldeth Road West, Manchester M20 3ER — within 15 minutes of M21
Test Duration: Approximately 38–40 minutes driving + pre-test briefing
Getting There by Bus: Routes 85 and 86 stop on Barton Road — approximately 2 minutes' walk from the DTC entrance
Documents: Provisional licence, theory pass certificate (within 2 years), glasses if prescribed for driving
Test Fee: £62 weekday / £75 weekend or bank holiday (DVSA 2025 rates)
The bay parking detail every candidate must know: The Barton Road DTC bay parking area has a tight kerb-side step — the bay is angled with a slight downhill camber. Students who haven't specifically practised the angle consistently clip the kerb on the left-hand steer correction. This is one of the most common reasons for a manoeuvre fault at this centre. DriveSQ students visit the Barton Road car park during lessons so this is never a surprise on test day.
First route from the DTC: Most test routes head immediately onto Barton Road A5145 and into Chorlton Road — the speed limit changes from 30mph to 40mph near the M60 boundary within the first few minutes. The examiner's assessment begins the moment you move off.
Withington DTC alternative: Some Chorlton students — especially those in M20 Didsbury or M14 Fallowfield — may be offered a test at Withington DTC on Mauldeth Road West M20 3ER. DriveSQ covers routes from both centres. Mention your preference when booking.
Manchester Road Metrolink crossing: The Chorlton tram stop on Manchester Road is within 2 minutes of the DTC start point on most test routes. Tram signal rules (flashing amber = proceed with caution) must be known before test day — it is not a standard traffic light sequence.
Real Test Routes

The 7 Chorlton Test Routes
That Examiners Actually Use

Mapped from real test experience. Our students practise every one of these before their test day.

1
Speed Change

Barton Road A5145 Southbound to Sale Moor

A frequent route out of Barton Road DTC heading south toward Sale Moor. The critical hazard is the speed limit change from 30mph to 40mph at the M60 boundary — candidates who don't notice the signs continue at 30mph on a 40mph road (a potential driving fault for unnecessary slow speed) or accelerate too aggressively when the limit ends. Bus lane awareness on Barton Road is also assessed: lanes have restricted operational hours and candidates who enter without checking the signage receive a serious fault. DriveSQ students know exactly where the speed limit changes and where bus lane hours are posted before sitting their test.

📍 Barton Road A5145: Stretford DTC → M60 boundary speed change → Sale Moor · bus lane awareness throughout
2
Pedestrian Zone

Chorlton Road B5217 through Chorlton Village

Chorlton Road runs through the heart of Chorlton village, passing pedestrian crossings at St Clement's Road and a series of traffic calming humps. The crossings generate a high frequency of pedestrian activity — particularly during school hours and weekend evenings. Traffic calming humps must be approached at reduced speed (not braked over harshly), and candidates must give way to pedestrians already crossing at all crossings. Examiners use this route specifically during higher-traffic periods. DriveSQ students practise Chorlton Road under real traffic conditions from their early lessons.

📍 Chorlton Road B5217: Chorlton village → St Clement's Road pedestrian crossings → traffic calming humps throughout
3
Bus Corridor

Wilbraham Road A6010 — Bus Lanes & School Zones

Wilbraham Road is one of South Manchester's busiest bus corridors — routes 50 and 50A create a continuous moving bus presence. Car drivers must not enter bus lanes during operational hours (7–10am and 4–7pm). The road also passes through school zones near Chorlton High School on Nell Lane, where 20mph camera-enforced limits are active 8–9am and 3–4pm. Cycle lanes add a third element: cyclists filter through queuing traffic and merge unexpectedly. Candidates who haven't been told the operational hours for these bus lanes are likely to enter one during a peak-hours test — an immediate serious fault.

📍 Wilbraham Road A6010: Chorlton → bus lanes (7–10am, 4–7pm operational) → Chorlton High School zone → cycle lanes throughout
4
Dual Carriageway

Princess Road A5103 — 40mph Dual Carriageway

Princess Road is the main arterial route into South Manchester from the M60. At 40mph with HGV traffic from Trafford Park and significant commuter volumes, it is the section of the Chorlton test that students are most underprepared for. Merging from Merseybank Avenue requires confident mirror-signal-manoeuvre at speed — examiners observe lane position, following distance, and merge timing carefully. Candidates who have only practised 30mph residential roads find this section overwhelming. DriveSQ Chorlton students specifically practise A5103 merging from lesson 5 onward — not as an afterthought at the end of their programme.

📍 Princess Road A5103: 40mph dual carriageway · Merseybank Avenue merge · HGV and commuter traffic · M60 to Chorlton section
5
Priority Section

Palatine Road B5167 through Didsbury

Palatine Road between M21 and M20 has restricted-width sections caused by parked cars on both sides. These sections require confident priority negotiation — understanding who has right of way and communicating clearly with oncoming drivers. The hazard is that oncoming vehicles in Didsbury frequently do not yield even when they should, requiring the candidate to hold position and assert priority. Candidates who panic, hesitate, or give way inappropriately accumulate multiple driving faults in a single section. DriveSQ students practise the three specific priority-negotiation pinch points on Palatine Road before their test.

📍 Palatine Road B5167: M21 border → restricted-width parked car sections → Didsbury village → M20 border · priority negotiation required
6
Residential Zone

Hardy Lane / Edge Lane — 20mph Residential

The Hardy Lane and Edge Lane residential route runs through a 20mph zone with a series of Give Way signs at unmarked junctions. In a 20mph zone, many candidates still travel at 25–28mph without realising — this generates driving faults. The Give Way signs at unmarked junctions require full stops and effective observation before emerging, even when the junction appears clear. Examiners use residential routes specifically to assess low-speed control and junction observation. DriveSQ students know every Give Way junction on this route and are practised at 20mph zone speed discipline before their test.

📍 Hardy Lane / Edge Lane: residential 20mph zone · Give Way at unmarked junctions · low-speed observation assessed throughout
7
Manoeuvre Zone

Barton Road DTC Bay Parking

Reversing into angled bays in the Barton Road DTC car park — the most frequently tested manoeuvre at this centre. The bay parking area has a tight kerb-side step and a slight downhill camber, creating two separate challenges: the angle of the bay requires a specific left-steer correction to avoid clipping the kerb, and the slope means the car will creep if not held. Manual candidates must control the clutch or apply the handbrake; automatic candidates must maintain consistent brake pressure. The examiner watches from the moment you select reverse — there is no warm-up phase. Our students practise this exact car park, not a flat multi-storey that doesn't reflect the real test.

📍 Barton Road DTC car park · angled bay · tight kerb-side step · downhill camber · left-steer correction required

Local Knowledge

8 Chorlton Hazards Other
Schools Never Cover

The specific, local details that separate a DriveSQ Chorlton student from someone who learned without local knowledge.

Beech Road Saturday Market — Pedestrian Overflow

Beech Road hosts a popular Saturday market generating extreme pedestrian density between 9am and 2pm. Pedestrians spill into the road from the market with no formal crossing, no marshalling, and no predictable movement patterns. Examiners use Beech Road on Saturday tests specifically because of its complexity — it is an efficient way to observe hazard perception and low-speed pedestrian management. Candidates who have never driven Beech Road on a Saturday are visibly unprepared. DriveSQ Chorlton students practise Saturday morning Beech Road as a standard part of their programme — not as an optional extra.

Princess Road A5103 Dual Carriageway — HGV Merging

The A5103 is a 40mph commuter route with HGV access serving Trafford Park and the M60. Merging from Merseybank Avenue onto the A5103 requires confident mirror-signal-manoeuvre at speed — the gap must be judged at 40mph, not the 30mph most learners are used to. Candidates who are not comfortable at 40mph either merge too slowly (causing the following vehicle to brake), or abort the merge entirely (a serious fault for indecision). DriveSQ Chorlton students practise this exact merge point from lesson 5 onward, so the A5103 is routine by test day.

Wilbraham Road Bus Lanes — Operational Hours

Routes 50 and 50A run a continuous bus corridor on Wilbraham Road. Car drivers must not enter bus lanes during operational hours — 7:00–10:00am and 4:00–7:00pm. These hours directly overlap with popular morning and early-evening test slots. A candidate who enters the bus lane during operational hours receives an immediate serious fault. The rule is simple, but many students simply haven't been told the hours. DriveSQ covers Wilbraham Road bus lane hours in the second lesson — not the week before the test.

Palatine Road Parked Cars — Priority Negotiation

Between M21 and M20, Palatine Road narrows significantly due to parked cars. Multiple sections require priority negotiation — knowing who has right of way and asserting it clearly. Oncoming vehicles in this area frequently do not yield even when the candidate has priority, requiring the candidate to hold position confidently rather than giving way unnecessarily. Repeated unnecessary giving-way in a priority-negotiation section generates driving faults. The specific pinch points on Palatine Road require local knowledge to anticipate; a candidate who encounters them cold on test day is at a significant disadvantage.

School Zones on Mauldeth Road — Camera Enforced

Chorlton High School and several primary schools generate 20mph camera-enforced zones on Mauldeth Road, active 8:00–9:00am and 3:00–4:00pm. These times align exactly with popular test slot windows. A candidate travelling at 25mph through a camera-monitored 20mph zone receives a serious speeding fault on the test form — even if the examiner says nothing at the time. Knowing where the 20mph zone begins and ends (not just reacting when you see a sign) is the only reliable protection. DriveSQ students know the zone boundaries on Mauldeth Road before they sit their test.

Southern Cemetery Pedestrians — Barlow Moor Road

The Southern Cemetery on Barlow Moor Road attracts consistent heavy foot traffic and cyclists, particularly on weekends and in good weather. Pedestrians cross Barlow Moor Road between cemetery entrances and exit unexpectedly from gaps in the perimeter wall. The road is also part of a test route — examiners include the Southern Cemetery section specifically because of the irregular pedestrian behaviour. Candidates who have not driven Barlow Moor Road past the cemetery are typically unprepared for how unpredictable the pedestrian movement is at this location.

Chorlton Metrolink Stop — Manchester Road Tram Rules

The Chorlton tram stop on Manchester Road creates a temporary stopping zone when trams arrive and depart. Tram overtaking rules apply at the stop — car drivers must not overtake a stationary tram. The tram signal on Manchester Road is not a standard traffic light: a flashing amber means proceed with caution, and candidates who treat it as a green light and drive through without checking for trams are at immediate risk of a serious fault. DriveSQ Chorlton students are taught the full tram signal procedure from lesson one — because the Manchester Road crossing is within 2 minutes of the DTC start point on multiple test routes.

Barlow Moor Road Cyclists — Door Zone Awareness

Barlow Moor Road carries a designated cycle route and acts as a through-route for commuter cyclists. Cyclists filter past queuing traffic at junctions and approach at significant speed relative to stationary vehicles. When parked cars line Barlow Moor Road — which is continuous — the door zone extends into the cycle lane. Candidates who position too close to parked cars risk a cyclist door-zone collision and receive an immediate serious fault for a road position that creates danger. DriveSQ builds the specific 1–1.5 metre passing distance technique on Barlow Moor Road from early lessons.
