Know the roads

5 Tricky Spots on the Stockport Test Routes

Every examiner at Dialstone Lane uses a variation of the same core routes. DriveSQ instructors have identified these five sections as the ones that most commonly lead to faults.

1

Dialstone Lane — Leaving the Test Centre

The test begins the moment you pull out of the test centre car park onto Dialstone Lane. The road is residential but busy with parked vehicles on both sides and the exit has a slight uphill gradient that catches manual learners. Turning right out of the centre means crossing oncoming traffic with limited sightlines due to the curve of Dialstone Lane to the left. Many learners rush this first move feeling the pressure of the test beginning — which is exactly when they need to be most composed.

Instructor tip: Take a breath before pulling out. Check right, check left, check right again. A parked car 20 metres to your right can obscure a fast-moving cyclist or motorbike. Leave more space than you think you need on the uphill pull away to avoid stalling in a manual car.
2

A6 Buxton Road — Lane Discipline and Speed Changes

The A6 Buxton Road is the spine of Stockport's test routes and arguably the most demanding road on any Stockport driving test. It runs through Hazel Grove and into Stockport town centre carrying two lanes in each direction in many sections. The speed limit changes from 30 mph to 40 mph and back multiple times, and the transitions aren't always marked by prominent signs. Bus stops with buses pulling out, pedestrian crossings with short warning distances, and right turn lanes that appear suddenly all combine to create a high-concentration section.

Instructor tip: Watch for speed limit signs every 100–150 metres on the A6. Default to 30 mph when uncertain. On two-lane sections, stay in the left lane unless overtaking or turning right — examiners note any unnecessary lane changes.
3

Wellington Road South — Dual Carriageway Merge and Shaw Heath Junction

Wellington Road South is a dual carriageway in places that then narrows approaching Stockport town centre. The junction with Shaw Heath and St Petersgate is a complex multi-directional crossroads used on most test routes. Learners who have only practised on residential roads often underestimate the speed of traffic on Wellington Road South when joining from a side road. The Shaw Heath junction requires early positioning to be in the right lane for your intended exit before you reach the lights.

Instructor tip: Before reaching Wellington Road South, identify your intended route and position in the correct lane 200 metres before the junction. Don't try to change lanes once you can see the lights — it's too late. On the dual carriageway sections, match the speed of traffic promptly after joining from slip roads.
4

Bramhall Lane — Residential Transition and School Area

Bramhall Lane connects Stockport centre to Davenport and Bramhall and forms part of many test routes. The transition from the busier town end — where two lanes of traffic occasionally interact with bus stops and cyclists — to the quieter residential section near Davenport requires consistent speed management. There are school zones near the Davenport area where the 20 mph limit applies at relevant times, and parked vehicles on both sides narrow the road through the residential section without warning.

Instructor tip: Bramhall Lane's speed limit changes require active monitoring — it's not consistently 30 mph throughout. Watch for 20 mph school signs and observe the road markings on the road surface. When parked cars narrow the lane, look well ahead and identify a passing place before the road closes in around you.
5

Offerton Lane — Roundabout at Turncroft Lane

The junction of Offerton Lane and Turncroft Lane involves a small roundabout regularly used when returning to the test centre. The roundabout is tight and the approach from Offerton Lane gives limited visibility of vehicles already on the roundabout from the right. Learners often give way when it's actually their right of way (causing hesitation faults) or, conversely, pull onto the roundabout without properly checking the vehicle to the right on the blind approach. Exiting correctly also requires signalling left as you pass the exit before yours — which learners sometimes forget under pressure.

Instructor tip: Approach the Offerton Lane roundabout at a steady speed — don't brake hard at the last second. Look right early to assess what's already on the roundabout. Give way to traffic on the roundabout, but don't hesitate if the roundabout is clear. Left signal as you approach your exit.

What you'll be asked to do

Manoeuvres at Stockport Test Centre

One manoeuvre is chosen randomly by the examiner. All four are practised regularly at Dialstone Lane.

🅿️

Parallel Park

Usually on Dialstone Lane itself or a nearby residential street. Smooth control, good observations, end up parallel to the kerb within 2 car lengths of the target vehicle.

⬛

Reverse Bay Park

In the test centre car park — reverse into a marked bay. Choose a bay with clear space either side. Maintain all-round observation throughout. End within the lines.

⬜

Forward Bay Park

Drive forward into a bay then reverse out. Take extra care when reversing — look both ways for moving vehicles before and throughout the reverse.

➡️

Pull Up on Right

Park on the right side of the road (facing oncoming traffic), reverse 2 car lengths, then safely rejoin traffic. Observe behind and in front before pulling away from the right side.

Emergency Stop — One in Three Tests

Approximately one third of tests at Stockport include an emergency stop. The examiner will warn you before the test begins that they may ask for one. When they raise their hand and say "stop", brake firmly and hold — don't swerve or look in the mirror first. The examiner checks the road is clear before giving the signal.

Instructor advice

3 Golden Tips for the Stockport Test

1. Book a Mid-Morning Test

The A6 and Wellington Road South are considerably busier during school runs (8–9am) and afternoon peak (3:30–5:30pm). A 10am or 11am test slot gives you lighter traffic on the busiest sections, less aggressive merging from other drivers, and bus stops that aren't blocked by queuing traffic. Most Stockport test centre slots in the 10am–12pm window are noticeably calmer than early morning slots.

2. Complete a Full Mock Test

Your DriveSQ instructor will conduct a complete mock test in the weeks before your actual test — 40 minutes on the Stockport routes, marked exactly as an examiner would mark it. The mock test exposes habits you don't know you have. In our experience, most learners identify one or two recurring issues during the mock that they then have two or three lessons to fix before the real test. This is the single most effective preparation step.

3. Warm-Up Lesson on Test Morning

Book a 30–40 minute lesson on the morning of your test. Your instructor collects you from home, drives you through the key Stockport test roads including Dialstone Lane, the A6 approach and whichever roundabouts or junctions your last lesson identified as needing more repetition, then drops you at the test centre already warmed up and confident. This is included with DriveSQ intensive packages and available as an add-on for block bookings.
