Real Test Routes

The 7 Altrincham Test Routes
That Examiners Actually Use

Mapped from real test experience. Our students practise every one of these before their test day.

1
Dual Carriageway

A56 Manchester Road Northbound

The A56 Manchester Road is Altrincham's primary dual carriageway, running northbound at 50mph through Broadheath and Sale. Lane discipline is the key test element here — candidates must hold their lane at 50mph, not drift toward the central reservation, and plan lane changes well in advance for junctions. Many learners who have only practised on 30mph residential roads find the speed and lane commitment at 50mph challenging under test conditions. This route typically forms the first 10 minutes of a test before turning back into residential roads.

📍 A56 Manchester Road: Altrincham → Broadheath → Sale direction, returning via Washway Road or School Road
2
Speed Cushions

B5165 Hale Road — Speed Cushions

Hale Road runs from Altrincham town centre south through Hale village. Speed cushions near Hale village require the correct approach: straddle them at slow speed with tyres either side of the raised section, or approach square-on and take them centrally. Candidates who don't know the cushion positions swerve to avoid them — generating a steering fault — or take them at speed and lose control momentarily. The road also narrows near Hale village centre, where parked cars reduce lane width and create natural pinch points that the examiner uses to test observation and planning.

📍 B5165 Hale Road south from Altrincham → Hale village → Ashley Road junction
3
Staggered Phase

A560 Stockport Road — Timperley Crossroads

The Timperley crossroads on Stockport Road A560 has a staggered pedestrian phase that runs independently of the main traffic signal. When the traffic light turns green, a separate pedestrian phase may still be active — and candidates who move on the green light without checking the pedestrian signal pick up a serious fault for failure to give way. This is one of the most consistently cited failure points at Altrincham DTC. The examiner watches this junction closely because so many candidates get it wrong. DriveSQ Altrincham students practise the Timperley crossroads from their first lesson on the A560.

📍 A560 Stockport Road, Timperley crossroads — pedestrian phase independent of vehicle signal
4
Village Lanes

WA14 Bowdon / Dunham Road — 20mph Lanes

Bowdon village lanes on and around Dunham Road are 20mph with parked cars on both sides creating a narrow effective carriageway. Door-zone hazards are significant here — residents open car doors without looking, and the narrow road leaves little margin for evasive action. Candidates must maintain a safe distance from parked cars (at least a car-door width) without crossing the centre line into oncoming traffic, while holding a consistent 20mph rather than creeping below it. The tight geometry of Bowdon also includes several blind bends where speed must be reduced to a speed at which the car can stop in the visible distance.

📍 Bowdon village lanes, Dunham Road, Church Brow — 20mph zone, residential
5
Bus Stop Layby

A538 Wilmslow Road Hale — Ashley Road

The A538 Wilmslow Road through Hale has a bus stop layby near the Ashley Road junction that causes positioning problems for candidates. Buses pulling into the layby partially obstruct the nearside lane — candidates must decide whether to wait, merge, or continue, all while managing the approach to the Ashley Road junction ahead. The correct response depends on the specific situation: a bus already stationary at the stop is different from one still pulling in. DriveSQ Altrincham instructors specifically practise the decision-making sequence at this stretch so it's automatic on test day.

📍 A538 Wilmslow Road, Hale — bus stop layby near Ashley Road junction
6
Multi-Lane Roundabout

M63 / A560 Altrincham Ring Road — Broadheath Roundabout

The Broadheath roundabout on the A560 ring road is a multi-lane junction where correct lane selection must be made 100 metres before entry — not on approach. Candidates who don't read the road markings in advance frequently end up in the wrong lane and either exit incorrectly or make a last-second lane change under pressure, both of which generate faults. The roundabout also handles A56 dual carriageway traffic, which means high-speed vehicles are entering from the right as you approach. Our students practise Broadheath from multiple approach roads before test day.

📍 Broadheath roundabout, A560 / A56 junction — multi-lane, read markings 100m in advance
7
Manoeuvre Zone

Moss Lane Bay Park — Angled Bays

The DTC car park on Moss Lane uses angled bays for the bay parking manoeuvre. Angled bays have different reference points from the perpendicular bays candidates often practise in supermarket car parks — the steering sequence begins earlier and the final position is judged against the bay lines rather than the car directly alongside. The school entrance directly opposite means pedestrians cross the car park during the manoeuvre — the examiner expects candidates to pause, observe, and wait if necessary, not continue the manoeuvre while pedestrians are in the path. Every DriveSQ Altrincham student practises this specific car park before test day.

📍 Moss Lane DTC car park — angled bays, school entrance opposite, pedestrian-heavy

Local Knowledge

8 Altrincham Hazards Other
Schools Never Cover

The specific, local details that separate a DriveSQ Altrincham student from someone who learned without local knowledge.

Moss Lane DTC Exit — School Entrance Opposite

The test centre exit onto Moss Lane sits directly opposite a school entrance. This creates a compound hazard: crossing traffic from the right, school-run pedestrians stepping out from the left, and potentially vehicles turning into the school from both directions simultaneously. The examiner begins the recorded assessment from the moment you select gear in the car park — this is not a warm-up. DriveSQ Altrincham students practise the Moss Lane exit on every pre-test lesson so the observation sequence is automatic, not improvised on test day.

A56 Dual Carriageway — 50mph Lane Discipline

The A56 Manchester Road runs at 50mph between Altrincham and Broadheath. Candidates who have only practised on 30mph residential roads find the commitment to lane position at 50mph difficult — there is no margin for gradual drift or late lane changes at this speed. The examiner observes lane discipline over a sustained stretch, not just at junctions. The most common fault here is drifting toward the central reservation while simultaneously checking mirrors. Our instructors take all Altrincham students onto the A56 from early in their training, not as a late addition before test day.

Bowdon Village Lanes — 20mph, Door-Zone Hazards

Bowdon village lanes are 20mph with parked cars on both sides leaving a narrow effective road width. The door-zone hazard is significant: a resident door opening without warning can catch a candidate who is too close to parked vehicles. The correct position is a full car-door's width from parked cars, which means being closer to the centre line — creating a different hazard from oncoming vehicles on narrow lanes. This balance — safe from doors but still within lane — requires specific practise in Bowdon, not general advice about passing parked cars.

Timperley Crossroads Staggered Phase

The Timperley crossroads on A560 Stockport Road has a pedestrian phase that operates independently of the vehicle signals. When the light turns green for vehicles, pedestrians who pressed the button a few seconds earlier may still be crossing on their green man. Moving on a green vehicle signal without first checking the pedestrian phase is a serious fault — and it happens frequently at this junction because candidates don't expect the independent phase. This is taught from the first lesson at DriveSQ for all Altrincham students; it should not be a surprise on test day.

Hale Road Speed Cushions — Straddle or Centre

Speed cushions on Hale Road near Hale village require a specific approach: either straddle the cushion with tyres each side of the raised section, or approach square-on and take it centrally at low speed. Many candidates, not having been taught the correct technique, swerve around the cushion as if it were a pothole — generating a failure to maintain proper course fault. Others hit the cushions at too high a speed, which is both uncomfortable and, if severe enough, a loss of vehicle control fault. DriveSQ covers cushion technique explicitly on the first Hale Road lesson.

Ashley Road Bus Layby — Positioning Decision

The bus stop layby on Wilmslow Road A538 near Ashley Road requires a real-time decision: is the bus stationary and passengers still boarding, in which case wait; or is the bus pulling away, in which case proceed? Candidates who have not been taught the decision framework try to force a rule where none exists — either waiting too long (undue hesitancy fault) or pulling past a bus that is still discharging passengers. The examiner wants to see active observation and a rational decision, not a frozen response. We practise this specific stretch and walk through the decision framework before driving it.

Broadheath Roundabout — Read Markings 100m in Advance

The Broadheath roundabout is one of the most technically demanding junctions on any Altrincham test route. Multi-lane entry from the A56 and A560 means lane selection must be made 100 metres before the junction — not on approach. Candidates who leave lane selection to the last moment either take the wrong exit or perform a late lane change that cuts across a vehicle already committed to a lane. The roundabout also feeds into and from the A56 dual carriageway, so vehicles entering from the right may be at 50mph. DriveSQ students approach Broadheath from every entry road before test day.

Airport Perimeter Roads M23 — 40/50mph Limit Changes

Routes into the M23 Wythenshawe area on some test itineraries pass close to Manchester Airport perimeter roads near Simonsway. Speed limit changes between 40mph and 50mph occur near airport approach roads — Simonsway runs at 40mph where candidates expect 30mph, and the airport perimeter road (accessible from Ringway Road) runs at 50mph. Driving too slowly on 40mph and 50mph sections generates undue hesitancy faults. DriveSQ covers the specific limit changes on these roads as part of Wythenshawe-area lesson routes.
