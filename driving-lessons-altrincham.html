Altrincham Driving Test Centre

The DTC Altrincham students use — and the specific details no driving school bothers to tell you about it.

Address: Moss Lane, Altrincham WA14 2TL
Test Duration: Approximately 38–40 minutes driving + pre-test briefing
Transport: Altrincham Metrolink terminus (Metrolink + Northern Rail interchange) is 7 minutes' walk via Stamford New Road
Documents: Provisional licence, theory pass certificate (within 2 years), glasses if prescribed for driving
Test Fee: £62 weekday / £75 weekend (DVSA 2025 rates)
What nobody tells you: The test centre exit onto Moss Lane is directly opposite a school entrance. Crossing traffic and pedestrians combine at this point — vehicles turning in to the school, parents on foot, and children near the road. Real Test Routes

The 7 Altrincham Test Routes
That Examiners Actually Use

Mapped from real test experience. Our students practise every one of these before their test day.

1
Dual Carriageway

A56 Manchester Road — Northbound Dual Carriageway

The dual-carriageway section of the A56 between Altrincham and Sale carries a 50mph speed limit and tests lane discipline comprehensively. Candidates must maintain appropriate speed in the correct lane without undertaking vehicles in the outer lane. The road is wide and fast — candidates who have spent most of their practice on residential streets find the pace intimidating. Examiners specifically watch for understaking, failure to maintain 50mph when safe, and erratic lane changes. Our students drive this route at full speed every lesson.

📍 A56 Manchester Road northbound: Altrincham → Sale · 50mph dual carriageway section
2
Residential

B5165 Hale Road — Speed Cushions, Hale Village

The B5165 Hale Road is a residential 30mph route through Hale village with speed cushions installed near Hale. Candidates must straddle or take each cushion correctly — straddling too far left causes wheel contact on the kerb side, too far right puts the car out of position. Speed awareness must be maintained throughout; candidates who slow dramatically before each cushion attract undue hesitancy observations. The road also narrows at parked car sections near the village — positioning and oncoming vehicle judgement are both tested here.

📍 B5165 Hale Road: residential 30mph · speed cushions near Hale village · parked car sections
3
Staggered Lights

A560 Stockport Road — Timperley Crossroads

The A560 Timperley crossroads has a light-controlled junction with a staggered pedestrian phase that is independent of the main traffic signal cycle. The pedestrian green man can activate while vehicles still have a green light — and many candidates, seeing pedestrians start to cross, become confused and stop when they have a valid green. Equally, the phase can end before candidates expect it, leaving them hesitant in a junction. Understanding the signal sequence here is a genuine differentiator. Our students know this junction's sequence before they ever drive through it.

📍 A560 Stockport Road: Timperley crossroads light-controlled junction · staggered pedestrian phase
4
Narrow Lanes

WA14 Bowdon — Dunham Road Village Lanes

The 20mph lanes through Bowdon village are among the most technically demanding on any Altrincham test route. Tight roads with parked cars on both sides reduce effective width to barely one vehicle in places — the door-zone hazard from parked cars on the left is constant. Candidates must position far enough from parked cars to avoid doors opening into their path while staying clear of oncoming traffic. The narrow width and low speed limit mean slow progress that must still be steady and confident — hesitancy in this area generates observations. Range Rovers and large SUVs are common and use the full width.

📍 Bowdon village: Dunham Road · 20mph tight lanes · parked cars both sides · door-zone hazards
5
Bus Stop Hazard

A538 Wilmslow Road Hale — Ashley Road Junction

The busy shopping-area junction at Ashley Road on the A538 Wilmslow Road is a positioning challenge. A bus stop layby on the left-hand side of the A538 causes candidates to drift left anticipating buses pulling in, or to follow the natural line of the road too far left when vehicles are in the layby. Position must be maintained in the left lane through the junction without being distracted by layby activity. Buses pulling out of the layby are also a hazard — candidates must assess whether the bus is emerging or stationary before committing to lane position.

📍 A538 Wilmslow Road: Ashley Road junction · bus stop layby left · shopping-area pedestrian activity
6
Multi-Lane Roundabout

M63 / A560 Altrincham Ring Road — Broadheath

The roundabout exits at Broadheath on the Altrincham ring road are multi-lane and require reading road markings well in advance — at least 100 metres before the roundabout entry. Lane choice is determined by road markings and destination, and the markings change at each approach arm. Candidates who leave lane selection until they are at the give-way line often make incorrect choices or change lanes unsafely on the roundabout. The volume of traffic here also increases decision pressure. Every DriveSQ Altrincham student practises this roundabout from all approach directions.

📍 M63/A560 Broadheath roundabout: multi-lane · read road markings 100m in advance · high traffic volume
7
Manoeuvre Zone

Moss Lane Bay Park — DTC Angled Bays

Reversing into angled bays at Altrincham DTC on Moss Lane is the most commonly tested manoeuvre. The school entrance directly opposite means significant pedestrian and vehicle movement during morning test slots — the manoeuvre cannot be completed in isolation; observation throughout is essential. The examiner watches whether you check for movement at the school entrance during each reference point, not just at the start of the manoeuvre. Our students practise these specific bays with school-entrance observation built in from the first time they attempt it.

📍 Moss Lane DTC car park · angled reverse bays · school entrance opposite creates active observation requirement

Local Knowledge

8 Altrincham Hazards Other
Schools Never Cover

The specific, local details that separate a DriveSQ Altrincham student from someone who learned without local knowledge.

Moss Lane DTC Exit — School Entrance Opposite

The first thing an examiner observes at Altrincham DTC is how you emerge onto Moss Lane. The school entrance directly opposite the test centre creates a combination of crossing traffic, turning vehicles entering the school, pedestrians including children, and parents stopping on the road. This is not a gentle warm-up — it is an immediate, complex emerging manoeuvre. Candidates who have not specifically practised this exit often rush it or give inadequate observation. Every DriveSQ Altrincham student drives this exit multiple times before test day.

A56 Dual Carriageway — 50mph, Lane Discipline, Undertaking

The A56 between Altrincham and Sale is 50mph dual carriageway. Undertaking (passing a vehicle on its left when it could move right to let you pass) is a serious fault. The dual carriageway also tests whether you maintain 50mph when traffic conditions allow — candidates who sit at 40mph on a clear road generate undue hesitancy observations. Lane selection for the A56 from Altrincham town centre must also be planned — joining in the wrong lane requires an immediate lane change at speed, which many candidates mishandle.

Bowdon Village Lanes — 20mph, Narrow Width, Door Zones

Bowdon village lanes are 20mph and often narrowed by parked cars on both sides to barely one-vehicle width. The door-zone hazard is real — a parked car door opening gives no time to stop at 20mph unless you are positioned appropriately. Position must be at least 1 metre (a car-door width) from parked cars. In a narrow lane this means threading a gap that feels tight. Candidates who hug the left to give space to oncoming traffic place themselves in the door zone. DriveSQ teaches the exact positioning reference points for these lanes.

Timperley Crossroads Staggered Phase — Independent Pedestrian Signal

At the A560 Timperley crossroads, the pedestrian phase does not always coincide with the red light for vehicles. The green pedestrian man can appear while the vehicle signal is still green, or the pedestrian phase can end before the vehicle red phase. Candidates who move on vehicle green without checking for pedestrians who have a simultaneous green man risk a serious fault. Candidates who stop for pedestrians when both pedestrians and vehicles have green cause confusion and potential rear-end collision. DriveSQ teaches the signal sequence at this junction specifically.

Hale Road Speed Cushions — Straddle or Take, Maintain Awareness

Speed cushions on Hale Road require a deliberate positioning choice: straddle (wheels either side of the raised section) or take (one or both wheels over the top). Straddling is only possible in vehicles with sufficient track width — most cars can straddle WA15 cushions but the wheel position must be exact. Misjudging the straddle puts a front wheel over the cushion, causing a jolt. The key fault here is excessive speed reduction — braking to 10mph before a cushion when 25-28mph straddle is comfortable. Know the cushion locations before your test so they are not surprises.

Ashley Road Bus Layby — Positioning and Emerging Vehicles

The bus stop layby on the A538 Wilmslow Road near Ashley Road junction creates a specific positioning problem. The road curves slightly toward the layby — candidates following the natural road line drift left into the layby zone. Buses pulling out of the layby need careful assessment: is the bus stationary with its engine running (going to pull out), or moving (already pulling out)? Candidates who don't plan their position in advance of the layby and make last-minute adjustments at the junction often find themselves mispositioned when they need to turn or go straight at Ashley Road.

Broadheath Roundabout Multi-Lane — Read Markings 100m Ahead

The Broadheath roundabout on the Altrincham ring road requires lane commitment before the give-way line. At 100m from the roundabout entry, the road markings direct you to the correct lane — if you read them at 10m it's too late to change safely. Candidates who have not specifically practised this roundabout from all approach arms frequently choose the wrong lane and either make an unsafe change on the roundabout or exit at the wrong junction. We practise entry from the A56, A560, and Broadheath approach specifically.

Airport Perimeter Roads — M23 Area, 40/50mph Limit Changes

Roads in the M23 Wythenshawe area near Manchester Airport have speed limit changes from 30mph to 40mph and 50mph as you approach airport perimeter and Simonsway. Candidates who stay at 30mph on a 40mph section generate undue hesitancy. The M56 slip roads are accessible from some routes — examiners do not take learners onto the motorway but the slip road approach roads require 60mph awareness near their entries. Know the limit changes on Simonsway and Brownley Road before your test — average-speed camera awareness is also relevant in this area.

Know Altrincham Before Your Test

The context that turns a technically capable driver into a locally aware one — and locally aware drivers pass.

🚊

Altrincham Metrolink Terminus

Altrincham is the southern terminus of the Metrolink Altrincham Line and also served by Northern Rail. The interchange is extremely busy on weekday mornings — trams, trains, buses, taxis, and pedestrians all converge around Stamford New Road. Test-day mornings require early arrival; traffic around the interchange backs up from 7:30am. Some test routes pass through the Stamford New Road area — know the interchange layout before your test day.

🏡

Hale & Bowdon Affluent Area

Hale and Bowdon are among the most affluent areas in the North West. Narrow lanes in Bowdon village are frequently shared with large SUVs — Range Rovers, Defenders, and Volvo XC90s that use the full lane width. Reversing out of driveways onto 20mph lanes is common. Candidates must be prepared for wide vehicles in tight roads and stay patient when gates open and vehicles reverse. Never rush a narrow lane in WA14 — the examiner will note composure under pressure.

✈️

Manchester Airport Proximity

Manchester Airport's perimeter is reached via M23 and WA15 postcodes. Airport-related traffic — taxis, minibuses, freight vehicles — is heavier than typical suburban roads. The M56 slip roads are accessible near some test routes in the WA15 area. Speed limits change as you approach the airport perimeter. Candidates covering M23 and Wythenshawe routes need to know the limit changes on Simonsway and the Ringway Road approach.

🛒

Altrincham Market & Old Market Place

Altrincham's Old Market Place and the Covered Market are among the busiest pedestrian areas in South Manchester. Loading zones adjacent to the market are frequently misused as stopping places — vehicles stopping suddenly, delivery lorries reversing, and pedestrians stepping off the pavement without looking. Market days (Tuesday, Friday, Saturday) increase activity significantly. Candidates must know which areas are pedestrian-only and where the loading zone boundaries are.

🏫

Grammar Schools in WA14

The Altrincham Grammar Schools (boys' and girls') are located in WA14 close to Hale Road. Between 8:15am and 8:45am, Hale Road carries significantly elevated school-run traffic — parked cars on both sides, parents performing U-turns, and a high volume of young pedestrians crossing. Test appointments starting between 8:00am and 9:00am will encounter this window. Know where the schools are on Hale Road and expect to encounter the school-run congestion pattern.

🌳

Dunham Massey National Trust

Dunham Massey deer park is a National Trust property on the WA14/WA16 border. On Sunday mornings, especially in summer, the approach roads carry slow-moving tourist traffic — vehicles pulling over to consult maps, pedestrians walking along the road toward the estate, and cyclists. Test routes near Dunham Road in WA14 will occasionally encounter this pattern. Patience and hazard perception at slow-moving tourist traffic is specifically observed by examiners on these routes.
