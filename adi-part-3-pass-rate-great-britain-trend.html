ADI Part 3 pass rates across Great Britain: the trend since 2018

Updated 25 November 2026 · Source: DVSA ADI part 3 test statistics (DRT521C, published 3 September 2026, data to June 2026)

More people than ever are taking the ADI Part 3, and a smaller share are passing. In 2025/26 there were 13,606 Part 3 tests in Great Britain, almost double the 6,933 in 2018/19, and the pass rate fell from 37.1% to 27.8%.

The numbers: tests +96.2% since 2018/19, pass rate -9.2 percentage points. Passes still rose from 2,569 to 3,784.

What the trend suggests, and what it does not

DVSA does not say why the pass rate fell. What the figures show is a clear step down from about 34.2% in 2023/24 to 28.9% in 2024/25, at the same time as tests rose from 8,609 to 11,749. Possible reasons are more first-time candidates, different preparation, or changes in how tests are recorded: before December 2022 DVSA did not record every Part 3 test. Without the underlying reasons, treat any explanation as a guess.

Men and women

In 2025/26 DVSA published 10,400 tests taken by men (pass rate 27.0%) and 3,209 by women (30.4%). Women made up 24% of tests and passed at a rate 3.3 points higher.

What this means for you

  • A fail is common. Roughly seven in ten attempts do not pass, so a fail says little about your ability to teach.
  • Prepare for the marking sheet. The pass mark is 31 out of 51, with a minimum standard in risk management. See how it is marked.
  • Budget for a possible retake. Each attempt costs £111, and the rules after a third fail are strict. See the retake rules.

Questions people ask

What is the ADI Part 3 pass rate in Great Britain?

27.8% in 2025/26 (3,784 passes from 13,606 tests), according to DVSA table DRT521C.

Has the ADI Part 3 pass rate fallen?

Yes. It was 37.1% in 2018/19, 34.2% in 2023/24 and 27.8% in 2025/26, while the number of tests roughly doubled.

Do women pass the ADI Part 3 more often than men?

In 2025/26 DVSA's figures show a pass rate of 30.4% for women and 27.0% for men.

How many ADI Part 3 tests are taken a year?

13,606 in 2025/26, up from 6,933 in 2018/19.

Why did the pass rate fall?

DVSA does not give a reason in the data. Treat explanations with caution. One known factor for older years is that not every Part 3 test was recorded before December 2022.

Source and method. All figures are from the DVSA tables DRT522B ("ADI part 3 test pass rate by gender: test centres", updated 25 June 2026, data to March 2026) and DRT521C ("ADI part 3 tests conducted, passed and pass rates", Great Britain, updated 3 September 2026), published on GOV.UK. Years are financial years (April to March); 2020/21 is left out because the pandemic disrupted testing. DVSA notes that ADI Part 3 data is recorded by hand, so accuracy can be affected. It also withholds counts where they are small enough to identify individuals (shown here as 'withheld'), and a centre appears only if more than one examiner carries out these tests. Small numbers move pass rates a lot: one extra pass or fail can shift a centre's rate by several points. Treat one year at one centre with care. DVSA also notes that before December 2022 not all ADI Part 3 tests were recorded, so older pass rates carry extra uncertainty. Differences, rankings and comparisons on this page are calculated by DriveSQ from the DVSA figures and are not DVSA headline figures. A centre's pass rate says nothing about how any individual test was marked.
