Failed ADI Part 3? Retakes, the third-attempt rule and what to do next

Rules and fees checked on GOV.UK on 25 November 2026 · Updated 25 November 2026

Failing the ADI Part 3 is common and it is not the end. But the rules about retakes are strict, and the third fail has serious consequences. Here is exactly what GOV.UK says, and how to plan so you never get near the third attempt.

What happens after each result

ResultWhat happens next
PassApply for your first ADI badge within 12 months of passing, or you must pass all three qualifying tests again
First failYou can retake. Book your next attempt within 2 years of passing your ADI Part 1
Second failYou can retake, under the same 2-year limit
Third failYou must stop giving paid lessons immediately

The third fail

If you fail the Part 3 for the third time, all of these apply:

  • You must stop giving paid-for lessons immediately, and it is illegal to give paid-for lessons after that.
  • Your trainee licence becomes invalid.
  • You must retake and pass Part 1 and Part 2 again.
  • You must wait 2 years from your original Part 1 pass before you can retake Part 1.
The takeaway: every attempt is precious. A third fail costs you at least two years, and that is why the right time to book is when you are consistently above the pass mark, not when you hope to scrape through.

Extra training after a fail

If you chose the extra-training option on your trainee licence (rather than the supervision option), you must do 5 hours of extra training before you retake the Part 3. See the trainee licence guide for the two options.

A way to plan your attempts

This is our own planning approach, not a DVSA rule. Treat each attempt as a project:

  1. Read the report line by line. Each of the 17 areas is scored. Highlight everything scored 0 or 1.
  2. Group the low scores by category. If most are in one category, that is where your next training goes.
  3. Set an evidence target. For example, three practice lessons in a row, scored by someone watching, with a total comfortably over 31 and risk management well above 7.
  4. Keep the 2-year clock in view. Write down the date two years after your Part 1 pass, and work backwards.
  5. Only then book. Booking early to "have a go" uses up an attempt.

Money matters

Each attempt costs £111 in test fees, plus the cost of the extra training or practice you need, and the time you cannot earn from paid lessons if your licence is affected. A retake is never just £111. This is the strongest argument for being ready before you book.

If you think the result was wrong

The result cannot be changed, but GOV.UK describes two routes: you can make a complaint to the DVSA, and you can appeal to a court within 6 months in England and Wales (21 days in Scotland). The DVSA may offer a refund or a free retest in some cases. If you are considering this, read the official page and act within the time limits.

What to do this week if you have just failed

  • Take a breath. Most people who qualify do not pass first time.
  • Get the report and write the 17 scores down in a table.
  • Find your lowest category and book targeted training for it.
  • Message us if you would like to ask about training for your weakest category.

Questions people ask

How many times can I fail the ADI Part 3?

You can retake after a first or second fail. After a third fail you must stop giving paid lessons straight away, and retake and pass Parts 1 and 2.

What happens if I fail Part 3 three times?

Your trainee licence becomes invalid, it is illegal to give paid-for lessons, you must retake and pass Parts 1 and 2, and you must wait 2 years from your original Part 1 pass before retaking Part 1.

How long do I have to retake the ADI Part 3?

You must book your next attempt within 2 years of passing your ADI Part 1.

Do I need extra training after failing?

If you chose the extra-training option on your trainee licence, you must do 5 hours of extra training before you retake.

Can I appeal an ADI Part 3 result?

The result cannot be changed, but you can complain to the DVSA and you can appeal to a court within 6 months in England and Wales or 21 days in Scotland.
