What Is an Intensive Driving Course?

An intensive driving course — sometimes called a crash course — concentrates your lessons into a short period, typically one to four weeks. Instead of one lesson per week over several months, you take multiple lessons per week or even daily sessions. This immersive approach helps many learners retain skills faster and reach test standard sooner. DriveSQ intensive courses are fully personalised — we assess your starting level and build a programme that gets you test-ready efficiently.

Who Are Intensive Courses Best For?

  • Complete beginners who want to pass quickly for work or university
  • Learners who have had some lessons and want to finish their training fast
  • People relocating to Manchester who need to drive for their new job
  • Students with holiday time who want to use the break productively
  • Anyone who has failed a test and wants focused preparation for a retake
  • Licence holders from abroad who need to convert to a UK licence

Intensive Driving Courses — Essential Knowledge

An intensive course compresses months of lessons into days or weeks — it suits learners who have limited time, need to pass quickly, or learn better with daily repetition. The following evidence-based tips represent the most common areas where learner drivers in Greater Manchester gain or lose marks during training and testing.

  • Intensive courses require significant mental stamina — driving for 6-8 hours per day is taxing and learners should ensure they sleep at least 8 hours between days of intensive training.
  • Book your DVSA test before starting your intensive course — test slots fill quickly and a confirmed test date creates a clear target that structures the course effectively.
  • Drink water consistently throughout intensive days — dehydration reduces cognitive performance by up to 20% and significantly affects reaction time and concentration.
  • Theory test must be passed before taking the practical test — if your theory certificate is less than two years old, you can proceed straight to the practical component of an intensive course.
  • Intensive courses work best for learners who have some prior experience — complete beginners typically need at least some spreading-out of learning to allow overnight consolidation.

This means signal phases can be shorter or longer than expected — learners should always approach signals prepared to stop rather than accelerating in anticipation of a green phase.

Pedestrian Priority in 2022

The 2022 Highway Code update fundamentally changed the relationship between drivers and pedestrians at junctions. Under the updated rules, when a driver is turning into a side road, pedestrians who are already crossing that road or intending to cross have priority. This is a significant change from the previous convention where drivers completing turns had right of way. Rule H2 of the updated Highway Code states that drivers must give way to pedestrians waiting to cross at a junction. Rule H3 requires cyclists and horse riders to give way to pedestrians on shared use paths. The updated hierarchy of road users places pedestrians at the top, followed by cyclists, motorcyclists, cars, vans and finally HGVs. This hierarchy reflects the relative vulnerability of road users in collisions — a pedestrian struck at 20mph has a 97% survival probability, rising to only 20% at 40mph. The practical implications for learners are significant: at every junction and roundabout approach, the first check must now be for pedestrians crossing or about to cross, before assessing vehicle traffic.

Lane Discipline Essentials

  • Hatched areas (diagonal white lines inside a solid white border) must not be entered except in an emergency — hatching inside a broken white border may be entered if safe and necessary.
  • Bus lanes that operate during off-peak hours may be used by general traffic outside their hours of operation — always check the hours-of-operation sign before entering a bus lane.
  • The left lane on any multi-lane road (including dual carriageways) is the correct lane for normal driving — the centre and right lanes are overtaking lanes only.
  • If you are in the right lane of a three-lane motorway and a vehicle behind signals to overtake, you should return to the middle lane and then the left lane as soon as it is safe to do so.

DVSA Practical Test — What You Need to Know

The DVSA practical driving test has a duration of approximately 40-50 minutes for most candidates. Within this time the examiner will assess: a 'show me' safety question while driving, approximately 20 minutes of independent driving following a sat-nav or road signs, at least one manoeuvre, and the possibility (one in three tests) of an emergency stop exercise. The examiner may ask you to pull over and restart on several occasions — this is normal and does not indicate impending failure.

After You Pass — What New Drivers Need to Know

Vehicle maintenance responsibility transfers entirely to the driver after passing — and many new drivers are unaware of what this involves. Key monthly checks: tyre pressure (including spare), tyre tread depth (legal minimum 1.6mm across the central 75% of the tread width and around the entire circumference), oil level, coolant level, windscreen washer fluid, and all lights. Annual MOT from year three, six-monthly visual tyre inspection, and a full service to manufacturer's schedule. Driving a vehicle with a tyre tread below 1.6mm carries a £2,500 fine and three penalty points per tyre — four defective tyres could mean 12 points and licence revocation for a newly qualified driver.
