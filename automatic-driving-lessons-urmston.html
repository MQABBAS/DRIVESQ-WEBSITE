Local Road Knowledge

Roads We Cover in Urmston

Metrolink & Town Centre

Urmston Town Centre & Golden Way — Metrolink Crossing, Bus Stops & Signal Junctions

Urmston town centre sits at the heart of the M41 road network and the Urmston Metrolink tram stop on the Altrincham line is located right on Flixton Road, the main road through the town. The at-grade tram crossing at Urmston station requires the same scanning routine as all other Metrolink crossings — check the signal board for both tram directions, position behind the white stop line and wait until the track is completely clear before crossing, even if the road signals are showing green. Beyond the tram crossing, Flixton Road continues into Urmston's pedestrianised zone with its busy bus stops, delivery vehicles and pedestrians crossing between shops. The Golden Way junction — where Flixton Road meets Crofts Bank Road and Stretford Road — is a four-arm signal junction that occasionally features on Stretford DVSA test circuits as an independent driving waypoint. We practise all approach directions to this junction until candidates can select the correct lane and signal sequence from memory.

Residential Practice

Davyhulme Road & Moorfield Road — Manoeuvres, Parked Cars & School Zones

Davyhulme Road connects Urmston to the village of Davyhulme and passes near Urmston Grammar School — a significant hazard point during school arrival and departure times. We schedule lessons to include the school zone deliberately, building awareness of lollipop persons, children crossing at unexpected points and the parked cars that accumulate near the school gates. The residential streets off Moorfield Road in Davyhulme are used for three-point turn practice — Tewkesbury Road and Kingsway South are quiet enough for beginners but have the realistic feature of parked vehicles on both sides. Bay parking is practised at the Davyhulme Park car park off Davyhulme Road, where the marked bays are wide and the site is quiet on weekday mornings. Parallel parking is introduced on Princes Road and Westbourne Road, which have enough parked vehicles to make the exercise representative of real test conditions.

Dual Carriageway

A57(M) Trafford Way & A56 Manchester Road — Speed Management & Lane Discipline

The A57(M) Trafford Way is a 50 mph dual carriageway that forms the eastern boundary of the Urmston area before connecting to the Stretford Gyratory and the city centre. It is a short but demanding section — two lanes in each direction, high traffic volumes and a merge from the A56 slip road that requires confident speed judgment. The A56 Manchester Road approaches this junction from the south, carrying traffic from Sale and the Altrincham direction. Candidates who lack confidence on dual carriageways often show hesitation at this merge, which is a fault the examiner will note. We tackle the A57(M) in both directions during mid-course lessons, introducing it initially in light traffic early in the morning before progressing to peak-hour runs as confidence grows. By test day the merge feels straightforward because it has been practised repeatedly rather than introduced for the first time on test day.

Test Route Preparation

Lacy Street & Stretford DVSA — Full Mock Test Circuit via Urmston and Flixton

Stretford DVSA on Lacy Street M32 is the test centre for Urmston pupils. The test centre is approximately ten minutes from Urmston town centre along Stretford Road, and the approach road itself is part of the test — candidates leave Lacy Street, join the local road network and return via a prescribed circuit that typically includes sections of Urmston or Flixton. The Flixton circuit from Stretford DVSA heads west along Stretford Road, crosses into Davyhulme Road and loops through Flixton before returning via Irlam Road. The Urmston circuit extends further into the residential streets of Moorfield Road and then back via the Golden Way junction. We run complete mock tests from Lacy Street replicating both circuits so candidates know every junction, crossing and potential hazard before their test date. By the time the real test arrives, every road on the circuit has been driven at least twice — usually three or four times — in mock test conditions.
