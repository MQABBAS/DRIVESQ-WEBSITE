The trainee driving instructor licence (pink badge) explained

Rules and fees checked on GOV.UK on 25 November 2026 · Updated 25 November 2026

A trainee driving instructor licence, often called the pink badge, lets you give paid lessons while you work towards the ADI Part 3. It lasts six months and comes with conditions. Here is how it works.

Who can get one

GOV.UK says you can apply once you have passed your ADI Part 2 test, and you must meet all of these:

  • You passed ADI Part 1 in the last 2 years.
  • You passed ADI Part 2.
  • You have had at least 40 hours of training from a qualified ADI, with at least 10 of those hours in a car. This is recorded on form ADI 21T.
  • You are eligible to take the ADI Part 3.

The two options you must choose between

When you apply, you choose one of two options. GOV.UK says you cannot change your mind afterwards, so read both carefully.

Which one is right? There is no universal answer. Supervision suits people who have a sponsoring ADI who can sit in on lessons regularly. Extra training suits people who want a structured block before the test. Ask us if you want to talk it through.

Moving schools

GOV.UK says you must apply for a new trainee licence if you leave one driving school and join another, so think before you move mid-licence.

When the licence runs out

A trainee licence lasts six months, so you want your Part 3 date planned inside that window. If you are not ready, do not book early just to beat the date. Read our guide on retakes and the third-attempt rule, because the rules there decide how much room you have.

A sample six-month plan

This is our own illustration, not a DVSA schedule:

MonthFocus
1Settle in with your sponsor or training programme; teach a small number of pupils; get watched early
2Practise opening conversations: goals and risk agreed in about three minutes
3Score yourself on all 17 areas after every lesson; find your weakest category
4Targeted training for that category; two full 45-minute practice lessons observed
5Book your Part 3 only if your observed scores are comfortably above the pass mark
6Take the test; if you need a retake, check the 2-year Part 1 clock and your licence

Questions people ask

How much is a trainee driving instructor licence?

£140, checked on GOV.UK on 25 November 2026.

How long does a trainee licence last?

6 months.

Who can apply for a trainee driving instructor licence?

You must have passed ADI Part 2 and passed ADI Part 1 in the last 2 years. You also need at least 40 hours of training from a qualified ADI, with at least 10 hours in a car, and be eligible to take the Part 3.

What are the two options on a trainee licence?

Option 1 is supervision by your sponsoring ADI for 20% of all lessons. Option 2 is at least 20 hours of extra training, completed within 3 months of getting the licence and before you book the Part 3. You cannot change option once you have chosen.

What if I move to another driving school?

You must apply for a new trainee licence if you leave a driving school and join a new one.
