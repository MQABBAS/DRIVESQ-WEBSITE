Test Centre

Eccles Driving Test Centre

The DTC Salford students use — and the things no driving school bothers to tell you about it.

Address: Liverpool Road, Eccles, Salford M30 0BH — on the A57 Liverpool Road dual carriageway, west of Salford city centre
Test Duration: Approximately 38 minutes driving + pre-test briefing
Parking: Street parking on Liverpool Road and side streets. Arrive 15 minutes early. The test car park is directly behind the building; examiners start bay parking manoeuvres here.
Documents: Provisional licence, theory pass certificate (within 2 years), glasses if prescribed for driving
Test Fee: £62 weekday / £75 weekend (DVSA 2025 rates)
What nobody tells you: The DTC sits on the A57 Liverpool Road — the same road used in test routes. The test car park is directly behind the building. The examiner's observation begins the moment you select gear. The car park has a slight camber toward the rear wall — candidates reversing too close to parked cars fail here on the bay parking manoeuvre.
The A57 exit: Leaving the DTC means joining Liverpool Road A57 immediately. A short slip road requires merging at 50mph — there is no gentle warm-up. This is the most technically demanding start to any Salford test route, and the first examiner trap many unprepared candidates encounter.
Typical first move from DTC: Most routes exit westbound onto the A57 or eastbound toward Salford Crescent — both require immediate confidence on a busy dual carriageway. The test starts the moment you pull away from the car park.
Pass rate: Eccles DTC is around the national average. Strong local knowledge of the A57 merge, Eccles gyratory, and Langworthy priority junctions is the key differentiator between prepared and unprepared candidates.
Real Test Routes

The 7 Salford Test Routes
That Examiners Actually Use

Mapped from real test experience. Our students practise every one of these before their test day.

1
Examiner Trap

Liverpool Road A57 Westbound Dual Carriageway

The test exits directly from the DTC onto the A57. Candidates must merge at 50mph from a short slip road — there is no long acceleration lane. The speed limit drops from 50mph to 30mph at the Barton Bridge approach without advance warning signs, creating a known examiner trap. Candidates who are still focused on the merge miss the limit change and continue at 50mph past the change point — a serious fault. DriveSQ students practise this specific stretch until the speed change is automatic, not something they think about under pressure.

📍 DTC exit → Liverpool Road A57 westbound → Barton Bridge speed limit change → return loop
2
Lane Discipline

Regent Road A57 Eastbound toward Salford Crescent

One of the busiest roads on any Salford test. The 3-lane section narrows to 2 lanes before the Ordsall Lane junction — candidates who fail to merge correctly are caught out. Lane discipline and signal timing are critical. The Salford Crescent railway bridge creates a blind crest on the approach — candidates who approach too fast cannot see the queue or red light on the far side. Bus lane enforcement cameras at the Salford Crescent junction catch candidates who stray into the bus lane during peak hours.

📍 Regent Road A57 eastbound → Ordsall Lane junction → Salford Crescent bridge crest → city-centre approach
3
Faded Markings

Eccles New Road A57 / Langworthy Road Residential Loop

Left at Langworthy Road into the residential grid. Parked cars on both sides of narrow streets. A 20mph zone with road humps operates throughout. Priority junctions on Seedley Road have faded give-way markings — examiners specifically watch lane position here because candidates who don't spot the faded lines treat it as an unmarked junction and fail to give way correctly. DriveSQ instructors cover every Seedley Road junction in this loop before the test, because the road markings cannot be relied upon to guide you.

📍 Eccles New Road → Langworthy Road → residential grid → Seedley Road priority junctions → return
4
Roundabout

Ordsall Lane / Trafford Road Roundabout

A 4-arm roundabout near MediaCityUK with heavy turning movements from Salford Quays traffic. Cyclists and trams (Metrolink Eccles Line) cross on the approach — candidates must check for trams before entering the roundabout zone. Examiners frequently use this route toward Old Trafford. The volume and variety of road users — HGVs from the quays, cyclists, trams, and pedestrians — makes this one of the most demanding roundabouts on any Salford test route. Knowing which lane to enter from each approach is essential before the day.

📍 Ordsall Lane → Trafford Road roundabout → Metrolink crossing approaches → MediaCityUK exit
5
Residential

Weaste Lane / Weaste Cemetery Loop

Narrow residential streets in a 20mph zone. Weaste Cemetery creates a pinch point where the road narrows to effectively single-file — vehicles approaching from opposite directions must give way. There is no formal passing place or give-way marking at the cemetery stretch, so right of way is determined by position and common sense. Candidates unfamiliar with the road often fail to anticipate the narrowing and meet opposing traffic at the pinch point without a safe gap. Every DriveSQ Salford student covers this specific pinch point in preparation.

📍 Weaste Lane → Weaste Cemetery pinch point → residential 20mph loop → return via Eccles New Road
6
One-Way System

Eccles Town Centre / Church Street Gyratory

A one-way system through Eccles town centre with multiple turn-offs where candidates take the wrong exit. The gyratory changes priority at the Eccles Cross junction — candidates who approach as if it is a standard roundabout take the wrong lane. Multiple candidates from other schools have failed here specifically because they have not been briefed on the gyratory layout. DriveSQ walks every Salford student through the Eccles gyratory turn-by-turn before they drive it, and then practises it repeatedly until the correct exits are memorised, not worked out under examiner pressure.

📍 Eccles New Road → Church Street one-way gyratory → Eccles Cross junction → town centre exit
7
Manoeuvre Zone

Liverpool Road Bay Parking (DTC Car Park)

Reverse bay parking in the DTC car park off Liverpool Road is the most commonly tested manoeuvre at Eccles. The car park has a slight camber toward the rear wall — in a manual car, the handbrake must be applied during the manoeuvre to prevent rolling. Automatic candidates still need to manage brake pressure against the camber. The reference points for straight bay parking on a cambered surface differ from a flat town-centre car park, and half the candidates on any given test day struggle with the roll. DriveSQ students practise this car park specifically — not a generic bay-parking skill, but this specific car park's camber.

📍 Eccles DTC car park off Liverpool Road · Parallel park on Liverpool Road side streets · Pull-up-right on A57 or Langworthy Road

Local Knowledge

8 Salford Hazards Other
Schools Never Cover

The specific, local details that separate a DriveSQ Salford student from someone who learned without local knowledge.

Liverpool Road A57 Speed Limit Drop — 50mph to 30mph, No Warning

The speed limit drops from 50mph to 30mph at the Barton Bridge approach on the A57 westbound without advance warning signs. Candidates exiting the DTC are already focused on the merge from the short slip road — and then immediately face a speed limit change that is signed but easy to miss at merge speed. Examiners specifically watch for candidates who continue at 50mph past the 30mph change point. DriveSQ teaches the exact location of this change from the first lesson, so it is never a surprise on test day.

Salford Crescent Bridge Blind Crest on Regent Road

The Salford Crescent railway bridge creates a genuine blind crest on Regent Road A57 eastbound. Traffic slows or stops on the far side for the Salford Crescent junction and bus lane enforcement cameras — but this is invisible to an approaching driver until they are over the crest. Candidates who approach the bridge at 40mph without reading the road risk tailgating or emergency braking on the far side. The correct technique is to reduce speed before the crest so you can stop safely for anything on the far side — exactly what examiners observe.

Metrolink Crossing near Trafford Road / Ordsall Lane

The Eccles Metrolink Line crosses near Trafford Road on the approach to the Ordsall Lane roundabout. Flashing amber signals apply at the tram crossing — the same rules as any Metrolink crossing apply: you may proceed if the crossing is clear, but you must not stop on the tram tracks. Candidates focused on the roundabout approach often pay insufficient attention to the tram signal phase. This is not a theoretical hazard — the Eccles Line runs frequently and trams do appear during test hours.

Eccles Gyratory Wrong-Exit Trap (Church Street One-Way)

The Church Street gyratory in Eccles town centre is a one-way system where multiple exits look similar to each other. At the Eccles Cross junction the priority changes — candidates who approach as if it were a standard roundabout are in the wrong lane and take the wrong exit. This is one of the most common reasons for test failures at Eccles DTC among students from other schools. DriveSQ covers every Eccles gyratory exit by turn number and road name before any student attempts the test.

Langworthy Road 20mph Zone / Faded Give-Way Markings

The Langworthy Road residential grid operates a 20mph zone with road humps. Priority junctions on Seedley Road have give-way markings that are severely faded — not just worn, but in places virtually invisible on the road surface. Candidates who rely on road markings to identify give-way junctions fail to give way at these points. Examiners specifically watch lane position and give-way behaviour on Seedley Road because they know the markings are faded. DriveSQ students are briefed on every faded junction in this loop before the test.

Weaste Cemetery Pinch Point — Narrow Road, Opposing Traffic

Weaste Cemetery narrows the road on Weaste Lane to effectively single-file. Vehicles approaching from opposite directions must give way — but there is no formal passing place or give-way sign at this point. The right of way is determined by position relative to the nearest passing space. Candidates unfamiliar with the pinch point arrive at speed and meet opposing traffic with no safe gap. The correct approach is to anticipate the narrowing by looking ahead and slowing before the pinch, not reacting to it when already committed. Our Salford instructors cover this specific point in every programme.

MediaCityUK / Salford Quays Pedestrian Crossings — Unexpected Puffin Crossings

The MediaCityUK and Salford Quays development has introduced multiple Puffin crossings mid-road in locations where candidates do not expect them — between junctions, not at standard corners. Unlike a pelican crossing, a Puffin crossing does not flash amber after the red phase — you must wait for green. Candidates unfamiliar with Puffin crossings sometimes pull away on the pedestrian red phase thinking the crossing has finished. DriveSQ specifically covers every Puffin and pelican crossing near the Quays on the Salford test routes.

A57 Dual Carriageway Merge at Short Slip Road (DTC Exit)

The slip road from Eccles DTC onto the A57 is shorter than most motorway or dual carriageway slip roads. Candidates must reach 50mph merge speed in a short distance while simultaneously checking mirrors, blind spot, and the busy carriageway traffic. Candidates who are still accelerating when they reach the end of the slip road either have to stop (dangerous on a dual carriageway) or merge at a dangerously low speed. DriveSQ practises this specific slip road multiple times in the lessons before the test — not just dual carriageway driving in general, but this exact slip road.

Major commuter route from Eccles into Salford city centre. Peak hour congestion 7:30–9am and 4:30–6:30pm creates very different driving conditions from off-peak. Salford Quays Development

Eccles Town Centre

Tuesday and Friday market days increase pedestrian activity and parking pressure around Church Street gyratory. Knowing the gyratory layout cold, independent of traffic conditions, is the only reliable preparation.

M60 Junction 12 (Eccles Interchange)

The A57 connects directly to M60 J12 at the Eccles Interchange. The roundabout at the interchange is not on standard test routes, but learners crossing it on lessons as part of broader route practice must understand the A57 approach lane discipline — lanes on the A57 leading to J12 are used differently from a standard town roundabout approach, and getting into the wrong lane here on a lesson can create a dangerous situation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Real questions from Salford students, answered honestly.

Where is Eccles Driving Test Centre?
Eccles Driving Test Centre is at Liverpool Road, Eccles, Salford M30 0BH. It sits on the A57 Liverpool Road dual carriageway, west of Salford city centre. Arrive via the A57 from either direction; the test centre entrance is on the south side of Liverpool Road.
What areas does Eccles DTC serve?
Eccles DTC serves M5, M6, M7, M27, M28, M30, and surrounding Salford and Eccles postcodes. Students from Worsley, Swinton, Pendlebury, and parts of Stretford also test here.
What is the hardest part of the Salford/Eccles test route?
The Liverpool Road A57 exit from the DTC, where a short slip road requires merging at 50mph; the Eccles gyratory one-way system at Church Street; and the Langworthy Road residential grid with faded priority markings on Seedley Road. All three are covered in every DriveSQ Salford lesson programme.
Does DriveSQ cover Ordsall, Langworthy, and Weaste?
Yes. We collect from all M5, M6, M7, M27, M28 and M30 postcodes — Salford, Eccles, Ordsall, Weaste, Langworthy, Seedley, Pendleton, Lower Broughton, Monton, Winton, Swinton, Pendlebury, Worsley, Boothstown, Walkden. No travel surcharge within these areas.
How many lessons to pass in Salford?
DVSA average is 45 hours. DriveSQ Salford students typically pass in 30–40 hours. Salford's mix of dual carriageway, one-way systems, and residential 20mph zones means a broad programme is essential — don't just practise on quiet streets and expect to be ready for the A57 at 50mph and the Eccles gyratory.
Is there a bus lane on the Salford/Eccles route?
Yes. Regent Road A57 inbound has a bus lane during peak hours (7–9am, 4–6pm). A57 Liverpool Road also has peak-hour bus lane enforcement near the city-centre approach. Our instructors brief you on exactly when and where these apply so you never enter a bus lane accidentally on test day.
Are there speed cameras on the Salford test routes?
Yes. Average-speed cameras operate on Liverpool Road A57 between Eccles and Salford Crescent. Mobile enforcement sites appear on Regent Road and Eccles New Road. Driving at 50mph confidently on the dual section (not creeping at 35mph) is specifically what examiners look for — undue hesitancy at appropriate speed limits generates faults.
What manoeuvres are tested at Eccles DTC?
One of: reverse bay park (most common — DTC car park, slight camber toward the rear wall), parallel park (Liverpool Road side streets), or pull up on the right (A57 or Langworthy Road). Bay parking is most frequent. The slope in the DTC car park is the detail most candidates miss — manual drivers must hold the handbrake mid-manoeuvre to prevent rolling, and reference points differ from a flat car park.
Is automatic or manual better for Salford?
Both work. The Eccles gyratory and Langworthy residential driving is easier in automatic — no clutch to manage during slow-speed one-way system navigation. Liverpool Road at 50mph is identical in both. If gear changes under pressure is your stress point, automatic removes it entirely. A separate manual test is needed later if you want to drive manuals.
