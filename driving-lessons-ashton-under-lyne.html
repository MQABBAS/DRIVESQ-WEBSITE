Test Centre

Ashton-under-Lyne Driving Test Centre

The DTC Ashton students use — and the things no other driving school bothers to tell you about it.

Address: Henry Square, Ashton-under-Lyne OL6 7SR — in Ashton town centre, one-way entry from Warrington Street
Test Duration: Approximately 38–40 minutes driving + pre-test briefing
Metrolink: Ashton-under-Lyne Metrolink terminus is a 3-minute walk on Lord Sheldon Way — candidates arriving by tram should allow extra time for the crossing
Documents: Provisional licence, theory pass certificate (within 2 years), glasses if prescribed for driving
Test Fee: £62 weekday / £75 weekend (DVSA 2025 rates)
What nobody tells you: Henry Square is one-way entering from Warrington Street. The exit to Wellington Road requires a right turn across oncoming traffic — examiners test this manoeuvre on nearly every test. Gap selection here is critical: traffic on Wellington Road moves fast and the turn is blind from the holding position. Most candidates who fail here have never specifically practised this exit in a lesson.
The Metrolink Crossing: Lord Sheldon Way carries both pedestrian and tram traffic between Henry Square and the Metrolink terminus. The tram has priority at its crossing signal — candidates must await the correct signal before proceeding. This crossing may appear on the test route within the first few minutes of leaving the DTC.
First move from the DTC: Most Ashton test routes exit Henry Square onto Wellington Road — the right turn across traffic is the very first manoeuvre examiners observe. The test starts the moment you engage gear in the car park. Don't treat the exit as a warm-up.
Pass rate: Ashton DTC is broadly around the national average. Candidates with specific knowledge of the Henry Square exit, Guide Bridge filter light, and Stamford Street bus lane consistently outperform those without local route preparation.
Real Test Routes

The 7 Ashton Test Routes
That Examiners Actually Use

Mapped from real test experience at Henry Square. Our students practise every one of these before their test day.

1
Filter Arrow

A635 Ashton Old Road / Droylsden

The A635 Ashton Old Road runs west from Ashton through Droylsden toward Manchester — a long 40mph stretch with traffic lights at Guide Bridge. Candidates must read the far-side filter arrow correctly at these lights. The filter arrow is positioned on the opposite side of the junction and governs a turning movement; candidates who miss it either move on a red phase or stop on a green one. Both generate serious faults. DriveSQ specifically covers Guide Bridge filter arrow behaviour in lessons before any candidate tests on this route.

📍 A635 Ashton Old Road westbound → Guide Bridge junction → Droylsden → Manchester Road
2
Camera Enforced

A6017 Stamford Street — Town Centre One-Way

Stamford Street is a town-centre one-way road with bus lanes during peak hours. The Lord Sheldon Way junction is camera-enforced — entering the bus lane outside permitted hours or in error generates a fine in real life, and on the test an examiner would record it as a serious fault for failing to observe lane restrictions. Candidates must know the bus-lane operational hours, verify signage before entering, and maintain correct lane position throughout Stamford Street's one-way section past the Metrolink crossing.

📍 Stamford Street → Lord Sheldon Way junction (camera) → Wellington Road → Henry Square
3
Narrow Road

B6167 Katherine Street / Park Parade

Katherine Street and Park Parade run near Ashton Market — a narrow section frequently used by delivery vehicles. Parked delivery vans and loading bay activity require candidates to read priority correctly: which vehicle has right of way where the road narrows, when to hold back, and when to proceed. The market is busiest on Monday, Friday, and Saturday — test slots on these days encounter more delivery activity. Candidates unfamiliar with the route are often surprised by the parked vehicles and either push through unsafely or stop unnecessarily long.

📍 Katherine Street → Park Parade → Ashton Market approach → Old Street
4
Hill Starts

A627 Mossley Road Northbound

Mossley Road climbs past Mossley station on a notable gradient. Hill starts are tested on the approach to the traffic lights on this climb — candidates in manual cars must manage clutch control on the slope to avoid rolling back into the vehicle behind. The lights at the Mossley station approach are short-phased and sometimes catch candidates mid-hill-start. Automatic candidates still need correct brake management. DriveSQ covers this specific section of Mossley Road because the gradient is steeper than it looks from level ground.

📍 A627 Mossley Road northbound from Ashton → gradient climb → Mossley station approach
5
Dual Carriageway

A57 Hyde Road / Dukinfield

The A57 Hyde Road includes a dual-carriageway merge from a slip road where traffic is travelling at 60mph. Lane discipline and a full mirror-signal-manoeuvre sequence on entry are essential. Candidates who hesitate on the slip road either merge too slowly (forcing 60mph traffic to brake) or merge too fast (cutting across lanes). The examiner observes mirror use, signal timing, and gap selection on this merge — it is one of the most technically demanding elements of the Ashton test routes, particularly for candidates who have only practised on residential roads.

📍 A57 Hyde Road westbound → dual carriageway section → Dukinfield border → return
6
20mph Zone

B6174 Penny Meadow to Broadoak Road

Penny Meadow and Broadoak Road pass through a residential 20mph zone with frequent pedestrians and a school crossing patrol. The 20mph limit is mandatory — driving at 30mph here (the same as adjacent roads) is a speeding fault. School crossing patrols operate during school hours and have full authority to stop traffic. Candidates must observe patrollers in advance, stop before the crossing when indicated, and not move until the patroller has cleared and given the all-clear. DriveSQ covers every school zone and its operational hours on the Ashton test routes.

📍 Penny Meadow → Broadoak Road → 20mph residential zone → school crossing patrol
7
Manoeuvre Zone

Henry Square Bay Park — Wellington Road Exit

Bay parking at Henry Square is a commonly tested manoeuvre at Ashton DTC. The exit onto Wellington Road after bay parking is itself a test element — it is a right turn across oncoming traffic from a one-way system. Examiners watch carefully for gap-selection and emerging safely from the square. Candidates must identify a safe gap in both directions, signal correctly, and commit to the turn confidently. Waiting too long generates an undue hesitancy fault; moving into an unsafe gap generates a dangerous fault. DriveSQ students practise this exit until it is completely routine.

📍 Henry Square DTC car park · Bay park manoeuvre · Right-turn exit onto Wellington Road

Local Knowledge

8 Ashton Hazards Other
Schools Never Cover

The specific, local details that separate a DriveSQ Ashton student from someone who learned without local road knowledge.

Henry Square One-Way Exit — Right Turn onto Wellington Road

Henry Square is one-way, entering from Warrington Street. The exit to Wellington Road is a right turn across oncoming traffic — and it is tested on nearly every Ashton test. Traffic on Wellington Road approaches at speed, and the gap must be correct before committing. Too many candidates either go too early (dangerous fault) or wait so long the examiner records undue hesitancy. DriveSQ students practise this specific exit until gap-selection is instinctive, because it is often the very first manoeuvre on the test.

A635 Guide Bridge Traffic Lights — Far-Side Filter Arrow

The A635 Guide Bridge traffic lights include a far-side filter arrow that governs a turning movement. The filter arrow is positioned on the opposite side of the junction — easy to miss when candidates are looking at the near-side lights. Acting on the wrong signal phase (going on red or stopping on a green filter) is a serious fault. This is one of the most commonly misread junctions on the Ashton test route and one of the first things DriveSQ covers when a student is preparing for test at Henry Square.

Stamford Street Bus-Lane Camera — Lord Sheldon Way Junction

The bus lane on Stamford Street is camera-enforced at the Lord Sheldon Way junction. The bus lane operates during peak hours — outside these hours it is available to all traffic, but candidates must check the operational times sign before entering. Driving in the bus lane during restricted hours is a serious fault on the test (and a penalty notice in everyday driving). DriveSQ teaches candidates the exact hours and the sign location so there is no uncertainty on test day.

Mossley Road Gradient — Hill Starts at Red Light on Steep Approach

A627 Mossley Road northbound climbs past Mossley station on a gradient that is steeper than it looks. Traffic lights on the approach stop candidates mid-slope — manual drivers must control the clutch precisely to avoid rollback. The lights are short-phased, so candidates who aren't prepared find themselves rushing the hill start under pressure. DriveSQ specifically drills hill start technique on this section of Mossley Road so candidates feel confident rather than tense when the light turns green.

A57 Dual Carriageway Slip — Mirror-Signal-Manoeuvre on Merge at 60mph

The A57 Hyde Road dual-carriageway merge requires full mirror-signal-manoeuvre discipline at 60mph traffic speeds. Candidates who have only practised on residential and town-centre roads often freeze on the slip road, either over-braking (causing a hazard behind) or merging into an unsafe gap (causing a hazard ahead). DriveSQ builds up to this merge gradually — slip-road approaches on quieter dual carriageways first — so candidates have the reference points and muscle memory needed when the A57 appears on their test route.

Penny Meadow 20mph Zone — School Patrols and Frequent Pedestrians

The 20mph zone on Penny Meadow and Broadoak Road covers a residential area with active school crossing patrols. Candidates who carry their 30mph instincts into this zone risk a speeding fault. School crossing patrols have full authority to stop traffic — candidates must stop before the crossing and not move until the patroller has cleared and signalled. DriveSQ teaches the exact boundaries of every 20mph zone on the Ashton routes and the patrol locations during school hours.

Katherine Street Delivery Bays — Double-Parked Vehicles, Priority Varies

Katherine Street near Ashton Market has active delivery bays that are frequently occupied — on market days (Monday, Friday, Saturday) delivery vehicles are often double-parked beyond the bays. Where the road narrows, priority varies based on position relative to passing points and which vehicle is closest to an obstruction on its own side. Candidates who don't know the road try to guess right of way under pressure. DriveSQ covers priority-reading specifically in this area, because market-day conditions make it unpredictable for the unprepared.

Metrolink Crossing Lord Sheldon Way — Tram Has Priority, Await Signal

The Metrolink tram crossing on Lord Sheldon Way between Henry Square and the Ashton terminus operates with a signal system that gives the tram priority. Candidates must await the correct signal before crossing — the tram does not stop for road traffic. This crossing is close to the DTC and may appear on the test route within the opening minutes. Candidates who treat it like a standard traffic light, or who move on an incorrect phase, risk a serious or dangerous fault. DriveSQ covers the tram crossing signal procedure in early lessons.

Know Ashton-under-Lyne Before Your Test

The local context that turns a technically capable driver into a locally aware one — and locally aware drivers pass.

🚊

Ashton Metrolink Terminus

Ashton-under-Lyne is the terminus of the Metrolink Ashton Line — trams run every 12 minutes. Pedestrian crossings on Lord Sheldon Way are active throughout the day and the tram has priority at its crossing signal. This is one of the first things candidates encounter when leaving Henry Square DTC on many test routes.

🏙️

Ashton Market

Ashton Market on Katherine Street and Park Parade is busiest on Monday, Friday, and Saturday. Loading bays and delivery traffic make the roads around the market narrower and less predictable on these days. Test slots on market days encounter more parked delivery vehicles and greater priority-judgement demands near the market area.

🏭

Guide Bridge Station Area

Guide Bridge has a level crossing and an active freight rail line. The crossing gates may close during a test, requiring candidates to stop correctly at the crossing markings. Freight trains use the line regularly — candidates who have not encountered the crossing before may be caught out by its operation during a test route on the A635.

🏫

Tameside Schools — 20mph Zones

20mph zones are widespread across OL6 and SK16 during school hours (roughly 8–9am and 3–4pm). Camera enforcement applies in many of these zones. DriveSQ instructors know every school zone boundary and operational hour on the Ashton test routes — so candidates are never surprised by a limit change on test day.

🌊

River Tame Crossings

The River Tame crosses through Dukinfield and Stalybridge via narrow bridges that appear on SK16 and SK15 test routes. These crossings restrict road width significantly — candidates must read approaching traffic, judge whether to wait or proceed, and maintain a safe position through the narrow section. Knowing the crossings exist before driving them is a significant advantage.

🛒

Snipe Retail Park — A635 Congestion

Snipe Retail Park on the A635 generates significant congestion on Saturday afternoons as turning traffic tries to enter and exit. Candidates on a Saturday test may encounter queuing traffic, mid-road turning vehicles, and pedestrians crossing the A635 outside designated crossings. DriveSQ prepares students for this specific section of the A635 under congested conditions, not just free-flowing traffic.
