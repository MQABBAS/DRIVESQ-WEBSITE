Choosing your pupil and lesson theme for the ADI Part 3

Rules and fees checked on GOV.UK on 25 November 2026 · Updated 25 November 2026

You choose your pupil, and the lesson theme comes from your conversation with them. Both choices change how easy it is to show the 17 areas. The DVSA form lists the pupil types and the lesson themes the examiner records, which gives us a clear menu to plan from.

What GOV.UK says about your pupil

  • You can bring a partly trained learner, a fully trained learner or a full licence holder.
  • A partly trained learner should be able to drive for 40 minutes without frequently stopping.
  • Your pupil cannot be an ADI, or someone who is preparing for the ADI Part 3 test.

The DVSA form records four pupil types: partly trained, trained, full licence holder (new) and full licence holder (experienced).

The lesson themes on the form

The form lists these themes for the examiner to tick: junctions; town and city driving; interaction with other road users; dual carriageway and faster-moving roads; defensive driving; effective use of mirrors; rural roads; motorways; eco-safe driving; recap of a manoeuvre; commentary; recap of an emergency stop; independent driving; and other. You are not marked on which theme you pick. You are marked on how you plan and teach it.

Matching pupil, theme and goal

These are our own suggestions, not DVSA rules:

PupilThemes that tend to give you room to show planning and coachingWatch out for
Partly trained learnerJunctions; town and city driving; effective use of mirrors; interaction with other road usersA pupil who needs constant instructions leaves less space for coaching questions
Trained learnerIndependent driving; defensive driving; rural roads; recap of a manoeuvreToo little to teach if they are already test-ready. Agree a clear goal for the lesson
Full licence holder (new)Dual carriageway and faster-moving roads; eco-safe driving; commentaryThey may want to skip the risk conversation. Have it anyway
Full licence holder (experienced)Defensive driving; commentary; interaction with other road usersExperienced drivers can resist coaching. Be ready to give a reason for each question

Five questions to ask before you pick

  1. Will they give me real goals? The first area is about identifying the pupil's learning goals and needs. A pupil who knows what they want to work on helps you.
  2. Can they drive for 40 minutes? The test has the pupil driving for about 40 minutes.
  3. Do they handle a stranger watching? Nervous pupils can be hard to coach in the moment. Practise with them first.
  4. Are they reliable on the day? Confirm the date and arrival time more than once. A pupil who does not turn up is a problem that costs you the test.
  5. Are they allowed? Check they are not an ADI and are not preparing for the Part 3.

Practise the pairing, not just the theme

Before test day, run at least two full lessons with the same pupil on a similar theme, with someone who knows the marking watching. Score them on the score calculator. If risk management or lesson planning scores are low, change the plan, not just the pupil.

Questions people ask

Who can I bring as a pupil for the ADI Part 3?

A partly trained learner, a fully trained learner or a full licence holder. They cannot be an ADI or someone preparing for the ADI Part 3.

Does the lesson theme affect my score?

The DVSA form records the theme but you are marked on the 17 areas of competence, not on which theme you chose.

What themes does the DVSA form list?

Junctions, town and city driving, interaction with other road users, dual carriageway and faster-moving roads, defensive driving, effective use of mirrors, rural roads, motorways, eco-safe driving, recap of a manoeuvre, commentary, recap of an emergency stop, independent driving and other.

How long does the pupil drive?

For about 40 minutes of the roughly 45 minute test.

Can I spend the whole lesson on a manoeuvre?

No. GOV.UK says you cannot spend the whole lesson teaching a manoeuvre, although a short recap is allowed.
