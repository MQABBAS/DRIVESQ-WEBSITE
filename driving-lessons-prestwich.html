Test Centre

Bury Driving Test Centre

The DTC Prestwich students use — and the things no driving school bothers to tell you about it.

Address: Angouleme Way, Bury BL9 0EL — in the Bury town centre retail area, off the A58
Test Duration: Approximately 38–40 minutes driving + pre-test briefing
Parking: The DTC car park on Angouleme Way is used for the bay parking manoeuvre — do not park in a test bay by accident. Public parking is available at the Bury Interchange multi-storey, a 3-minute walk. Arrive 15 minutes early.
Documents: Provisional licence, theory pass certificate (within 2 years), glasses if prescribed for driving
Test Fee: £62 weekday / £75 weekend (DVSA 2025 rates)
What nobody tells you: Angouleme Way is a busy retail park access road. The DTC exit goes directly into shared pedestrian/vehicle space near the Bury Interchange. Many candidates rush out of the car park and immediately fail to observe pedestrians stepping out between parked cars. The examiner starts recording from the moment you select gear in the car park.
The Bury Interchange: Bury Interchange (bus station and Metrolink terminus) is immediately adjacent to the DTC. Some test routes pass through the interchange approach — trams, buses, cyclists, and pedestrians all share the same zone. This is the most complex start to any Greater Manchester test route.
Typical first move from Prestwich: Most routes head south down Bury New Road A56 toward Prestwich — which means Metrolink crossings appear within the first 5 minutes of the test. Don't treat the drive from DTC to Prestwich as a warm-up — it is the test.
Pass rate: Bury DTC is around the national average. Strong local road knowledge — especially tram crossing rules — is the key differentiator between prepared and unprepared candidates.
Real Test Routes

The 7 Prestwich Test Routes
That Examiners Actually Use

Mapped from real test experience. Our students practise every one of these before their test day.

1
Tram Crossings

Bury New Road A56 — Metrolink Section

The A56 Bury New Road runs from Bury through Whitefield, Besses o' th' Barn, Prestwich, and into Manchester. The Metrolink Bury Line runs alongside and crosses the A56 at multiple points with on-street tram stops. At Prestwich tram stop, Besses o' th' Barn stop, and Whitefield stop, trams cross or run beside car lanes with light-signal controls. Most candidates from outside Prestwich have never driven on a tram-road before their test. The signals work differently from standard traffic lights — steady amber means stop, flashing amber means proceed with caution if the tram stop is clear. Getting this wrong in front of an examiner is an immediate serious fault.

📍 A56 Bury New Road: Whitefield → Besses o' th' Barn → Prestwich → Broughton Park
2
Complex Junction

Simister Lane / M60 Junction Roundabout

Simister Lane runs east from Prestwich to the M60 motorway junction 17/18 roundabout at Simister. This roundabout handles motorway slip road traffic alongside local traffic — it is one of the most complex roundabouts on any Bury DTC test route. Lane discipline is critical: the correct lane for Simister village is different from the lane for the M60 northbound, and candidates who enter on the wrong side frequently exit onto the motorway slip road — a serious fault. The approach from Simister Lane also has a 40mph section that changes to 30mph before the roundabout; many candidates don't notice the change.

📍 Simister Lane east → M60 J17/18 roundabout → return via Bury New Road
3
Speed Cameras

Whitefield Bypass and Church Lane

The Whitefield bypass (A665/A56 junction area) is a 40mph dual carriageway section that many candidates expect to be 30mph, having driven Church Lane and the old village route at 30mph. The speed limit change is signed but easy to miss when navigating simultaneously. Average-speed cameras on the bypass enforce 40mph — driving at 30mph on this section generates a 'undue hesitancy' fault. Church Lane itself is 30mph with speed humps near St John's CE Primary School, and a pelican crossing that is triggered frequently by pedestrians heading to Whitefield town centre.

📍 A56 northbound → Whitefield bypass 40mph → Church Lane 30mph → Bury New Road return
4
Residential

Scholes Lane and Prestwich Village

Scholes Lane connects Prestwich village to the A56, passing through a conservation area with stone walls close to the road edge on one side and parked cars on the other. The lane narrows at the St Mary's Churchyard bend — single-file with no obvious give-way line. Candidates unfamiliar with the road either stop too long waiting for right of way or push through when they should wait. The approach to Prestwich village from Scholes Lane involves a sharp 90-degree left turn that catches candidates who are travelling slightly too fast. Our students walk this junction before driving it.

📍 Scholes Lane → Prestwich village → St Mary's Road → Bury New Road A56
5
A-Road

Radcliffe Road A665 toward Bury

The A665 Radcliffe Road runs north from Bury town centre through Unsworth toward Radcliffe. It is 40mph in the open section past Unsworth and features a series of offset crossroads where traffic from minor roads gives way. The challenge here is maintaining 40mph consistently while scanning for emerging vehicles at side junctions — candidates in slow-speed residential mode often find the A665 intimidating. The junction at Bury Road/Bolton Road near Unsworth involves a dual give-way that many candidates treat as a simple crossroads. Mobile camera enforcement is used on this road periodically.

📍 A665 Radcliffe Road → Unsworth → Hollins → return via Manchester Road
6
Residential

Butterstile Lane — Kersal and Broughton Park

Butterstile Lane runs south from Prestwich toward Kersal and Broughton Park — an area of wider tree-lined roads that look open but contain several pedestrian-heavy crossings, speed tables, and school zones (Prestwich Arts College, St Mary's RC Primary). The roads around Broughton Park have a large Orthodox Jewish community with significant pedestrian activity on Friday evenings (Shabbat) and Saturdays — test times on Friday afternoons coincide with this. Candidates must give way to pedestrians at zebra crossings and observe children near school zones. Not knowing this area means being surprised by it on test day.

📍 Butterstile Lane → Kersal Road → Hilton Lane → Broughton Park approach
7
Manoeuvre Zone

Angouleme Way Bay Parking

Bay parking is the most commonly tested manoeuvre at Bury DTC — the DTC car park on Angouleme Way is used. Reverse bay park is most frequent. The car park has a slight downward slope toward the bays, meaning a car in neutral will creep forward — manual candidates must hold the clutch or apply the handbrake during the manoeuvre. Automatic candidates still need to manage brake pressure. The bays are adjacent to the examiner office windows, which creates pressure. Our students practise this car park specifically — reference points here are different from a flat town-centre car park.

📍 Angouleme Way DTC car park · Residential bays on nearby streets for parallel park

Local Knowledge

8 Prestwich Hazards Other
Schools Never Cover

The specific, local details that separate a DriveSQ Prestwich student from someone who learned without local knowledge.

Metrolink Tram Signals — Flashing Amber

At Prestwich, Besses o' th' Barn, and Whitefield tram stops, the signal sequence includes a flashing amber phase that is unique to tram crossings. Flashing amber means the tram stop is active but you may proceed with caution if the stop is clear of passengers boarding/alighting. Most learners have never seen this signal before and treat it as a red light (stopping unnecessarily) or ignore it entirely (driving through when they shouldn't). Neither is correct. DriveSQ teaches the full tram signal sequence from the first lesson, because it appears on the test within minutes of leaving Bury DTC.

The Simister Roundabout M60 Trap

The M60 junction 17/18 roundabout at Simister handles high-speed motorway traffic merging with the local Simister Lane approach. Candidates who haven't specifically practised the correct lane for each exit frequently end up on the M60 slip road — a serious fault as the examiner cannot safely turn you around on a motorway. The correct lane for returning toward Prestwich is the left lane throughout the roundabout, exiting at the second exit. This is counter-intuitive when the right lane looks like it should lead back the way you came.

Whitefield Bypass Speed Limit Change

The old Bury New Road through Whitefield village is 30mph. The Whitefield bypass (the more direct A56 route north) is 40mph. Candidates who have been driving 30mph through Whitefield village and then join the bypass without noticing the limit change get 'undue hesitancy' faults for driving too slowly. Average-speed cameras are present. The limit-change sign is at the bypass entry point but easy to miss during a lane-change manoeuvre. Know exactly where the limit changes before your test.

Scholes Lane Pinch Point — St Mary's Churchyard

Scholes Lane narrows at the St Mary's Church boundary wall — old stone walls sit right at the road edge with no footpath buffer. When a vehicle is coming the other way, the effective width of the road requires one vehicle to wait. There is no formal give-way line here — the right of way is determined by who is closer to a passing point. Candidates who don't know this point exist attempt to push through and either clip the wall or cause a dangerous situation. Every DriveSQ Prestwich student practises this specific spot.

Broughton Park Friday Afternoon Pedestrian Activity

The Broughton Park area (M7, south of Prestwich) has a large Orthodox Jewish community. Friday afternoons from approximately 3pm see significantly increased pedestrian activity as residents head home before Shabbat. Test appointments between 2:30pm and 4:30pm on Fridays will encounter more pedestrians at zebra crossings, more children near schools, and more parked cars as people stop to collect groceries. This is a real, local hazard that affects afternoon Friday tests in Prestwich's southern area — and one that generic driving schools never mention.

Bury New Road A56 — Tram Track Lane Position

On sections where the Metrolink runs alongside the A56, the tram tracks are embedded in the road surface. Car tyres can catch in the groove alongside tram tracks, causing the car to pull. Some candidates, noticing this, swerve away from the tracks — which takes them out of their lane. The correct position is to straddle the tram track at a shallow angle (not parallel to it) and maintain consistent road position. This is a specific technique that our Prestwich instructors teach explicitly on the A56.

Bury Interchange Start — Pedestrians and Buses

Bury Interchange (bus station and Metrolink terminus) is immediately adjacent to Bury DTC. The first 200 metres of many test routes pass through shared pedestrian/vehicle access roads where buses, trams, taxis, cyclists, and pedestrians all share space without conventional road markings. Candidates fresh from residential road practice are often overwhelmed by the volume of users in this small area. We specifically practise the Interchange approach during lessons so it's routine, not a shock, on test day.

Longfield Centre Car Park Exits

Longfield Centre, Prestwich's local shopping area, has three vehicle exits onto Longfield Road and Bury New Road. Delivery vehicles, parking manoeuvres, and pedestrians crossing between shops make this stretch of road unpredictable during daytime test hours. The examiner observes whether you slow appropriately at car park exits without being asked — anticipating vehicles emerging is tested here. Most driving schools don't specifically teach this — they just say "watch for emerging vehicles." Our Prestwich students know exactly which car park exits to anticipate and where pedestrians typically appear.

Metrolink Bury Line

The Bury Metrolink runs the full length of the Prestwich test route. Prestwich station, Besses o' th' Barn station, and Whitefield station all have on-street tram sections that affect car lanes. This is the single most important local knowledge for any Prestwich student — no other area has tram crossings appearing this frequently on the test route.

⛪

St Mary's Parish Church, Prestwich

St Mary's Church on Church Lane sits at the junction of Scholes Lane — creating a traffic bottleneck on Sunday mornings and during funerals. The adjacent walled churchyard narrows Scholes Lane at a critical point on the test route. Knowing the church is there helps you anticipate the road narrowing before you see it.

🏫

Prestwich Arts College & School Zones

Prestwich Arts College on Heys Road (M25) and several primary schools on the Butterstile Lane / Broughton Park route generate school-time traffic (8–9am, 3–4pm). Camera-enforced 20mph school zones apply — these are not optional, and the cameras are active. Our instructors know every school zone on the test route and its operational hours.

🛣️

M60 Junction 17 — Simister

The M60 junction at Simister is one of the most technically demanding junctions on any Bury DTC test route. Motorway traffic joins the local roundabout at speed. Knowing the roundabout exits cold — not working them out under pressure — is what separates candidates who handle it cleanly from those who make errors.

🚌

Bus Routes on Test Roads

Routes 97, 98, 135, X90, and several other services use Bury New Road A56 through Prestwich and Whitefield. Buses pulling in at stops on the A56 test whether you observe correctly and decide whether to wait behind or overtake safely. The A56 is the busiest bus corridor in the test route — knowing where the stops are lets you anticipate rather than react.

🏘️

Broughton Park Community

Broughton Park (M7) borders Prestwich to the south. The area has a significant Orthodox Jewish community — pedestrian activity patterns are different from typical suburban areas. Friday afternoons and Saturday mornings see high pedestrian volumes. A DriveSQ instructor who knows the area will tell you this before your first lesson — not when you're surprised by it on test day.

Frequently Asked Questions

Real questions from Prestwich students, answered honestly.

Which test centre do Prestwich students use?
Prestwich students test at Bury Driving Test Centre, Angouleme Way, Bury BL9 0EL — approximately 4 miles from Prestwich via Bury New Road A56. Bury is the primary test centre for all M25 and M45 postcodes.
What are the actual test routes from Bury DTC?
The main routes covering Prestwich include: (1) Bury New Road A56 southbound — Metrolink tram crossings at Whitefield, Besses o' th' Barn, and Prestwich stops, (2) Simister Lane / M60 J17-18 roundabout, (3) Whitefield bypass (40mph) and Church Lane (30mph), (4) Scholes Lane and Prestwich village, (5) Radcliffe Road A665 toward Unsworth, (6) Butterstile Lane / Kersal / Broughton Park, (7) Angouleme Way bay parking manoeuvre. Examiners combine 2–3 routes per test.
What are the tram signal rules on the Bury New Road?
At Metrolink tram stops on Bury New Road, the light sequence is: red (stop), steady amber (prepare to stop), flashing amber (proceed with caution if the stop is clear). You must not stop on tram tracks. You must not overtake a tram at a stop where passengers may be boarding or alighting. These rules are tested implicitly at every tram crossing during the test. Most candidates who fail here have not specifically been taught the flashing amber signal rule.

How many lessons do I need in Prestwich?
The DVSA average is 45 hours for a complete beginner. DriveSQ Prestwich students typically pass in 30–40 hours with weekly lessons. The Metrolink tram route, Simister roundabout, and Bury Interchange are the areas that add time for candidates from non-tram areas — allow extra lessons specifically on these if you've never driven in a tram town before.
What manoeuvre is most common at Bury DTC?
Bay parking (reverse into bay) at the Angouleme Way DTC car park is most common. The car park has a slight downward slope — the car will creep forward if not held by the clutch or brake. Parallel parking is tested on Angouleme Way. The 'pull up on the right and reverse' manoeuvre is used on wider roads like Radcliffe Road. We practise all three, with extra attention on the car park slope detail that most instructors don't mention.
Is there a bus lane on the Bury New Road?
The A56 Bury New Road has bus-lane provisions in sections between Prestwich and Manchester during peak hours. Our Prestwich instructors cover the exact sections and operational hours during lessons. As a general rule: if there is a bus lane sign with operational hours, check the time before entering — never assume a lane is freely available during busy hours without checking.

Is automatic better than manual for Prestwich?
Both work well on Prestwich test routes. The Bury New Road A56 is straightforward in either. Scholes Lane slow-speed work and Bury Interchange navigation are easier in automatic when you're not also managing a clutch. If tram signal reading and simultaneous gear changes feel like too much to handle at once, automatic removes the gear element entirely. You can take a separate manual test later if needed.
