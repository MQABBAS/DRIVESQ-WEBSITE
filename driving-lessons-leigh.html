Test Centre

Leigh Driving Test Centre

The DTC Leigh students use — and the things no driving school bothers to tell you about it.

Address: Bradshawgate, Leigh WN7 4NE — in Leigh town centre on the main one-way system
Test Duration: Approximately 38–40 minutes driving + pre-test briefing
Parking: Street parking on Bradshawgate is limited. Arrive 15 minutes early — use the Spinning Gate shopping centre car park nearby for instructor drop-off. Do not block loading bays on Bradshawgate during the pick-up window.
Documents: Provisional licence, theory pass certificate (within 2 years), glasses if prescribed for driving
Test Fee: £62 weekday / £75 weekend (DVSA 2025 rates)
Public Transport: Buses 2/2A stop on Bradshawgate — approximately 2 minutes walk from the DTC
What nobody tells you: Bradshawgate is a one-way street. The DTC exit leads to a right-turn onto King Street. Candidates who ignore the one-way markings and attempt a left turn — out of habit or nerves — receive a serious fault immediately. Examiners note this more than almost any other error at Leigh DTC. Every DriveSQ Leigh student practises the exit multiple times before test day.
Town centre start: Many routes begin by navigating Leigh town centre — Chapel Street loading zones, pedestrianised boundary sections, and bus-only roads are all adjacent to the test route. The examiner starts assessing from the moment the car moves. Don't treat the first 200 metres as a warm-up.
Route variety: Leigh DTC uses routes that mix town centre complexity (Chapel Street, King Street), dual carriageway confidence (Atherleigh Way), and high-speed A-road work (East Lancs Road). Candidates prepared only for residential roads will be caught out.
Pass rate: Around the national average. Local road knowledge — especially the one-way system exit and A579 speed changes — is the most reliable differentiator.
Real Test Routes

The 7 Leigh Test Routes
That Examiners Actually Use

Mapped from real test experience. Our students practise every one of these before their test day.

1
Concealed Junction

A572 St Helens Road Westbound

40mph with a concealed entrance at the junction with Atherleigh Way. Candidates must read priority correctly on the approach — the junction is on a slight bend which limits sightlines, and the give-way arrangement is not obvious until you're almost at the white line. Candidates who miss the junction type fail to position correctly and are caught giving way when they have priority, or proceeding when they should yield. DriveSQ students drive this approach from multiple directions before test day.

📍 A572 St Helens Road westbound → Atherleigh Way junction → Leigh town return
2
Independent Signal Phase

B5215 Leigh Road / Atherton — Bag Lane Junction

30mph residential route with light-controlled crossroads at the Bag Lane junction. The pedestrian phase here is independent — it can go red for through traffic while pedestrians are still crossing, separately from the main signal cycle. Candidates who study the main phase and decide to move when it turns green, without checking for an active pedestrian phase, frequently collect a serious fault. This is Leigh's most technically nuanced signal junction — our students practise it until the independent phase is automatic to check.

📍 B5215 Leigh Road → Bag Lane junction → Atherton residential network
3
Speed Sequence

A579 Warrington Road — Abram

Multiple 30→40→30mph speed limit changes in quick succession through the Abram section. Candidates who miss limit signs collect speeding minors; those who brake hard when they notice they've overrun receive hesitancy faults — there's no clean outcome from not knowing the sequence. These changes happen across roughly a mile of road with no obvious landmarks to trigger a limit check. The only reliable solution is to know where every change is before you drive it. Our students are briefed on every limit sign location on this road.

📍 A579 Warrington Road → Abram → Platt Bridge approach → return route
4
Dual Carriageway

A572 Atherleigh Way Dual Carriageway

40mph northern bypass section. The challenge is the slip road entry from the A579 — candidates must execute mirror-signal-manoeuvre at speed while matching traffic flow on the dual carriageway. Candidates who slow at the slip-road entry and merge at 25mph when carriageway traffic is doing 40mph create a dangerous situation. DriveSQ teaches the full MSMM sequence for this specific slip, including the merge point and where to check for space before committing to join.

📍 A579 slip → A572 Atherleigh Way northbound → Leigh town approach
5
High-Speed Merge

B5215 East Lancs Road Slip / M60 Feeder

Merging from a short slip road onto a 70mph national speed limit road. The slip is short — candidates have limited distance to match speed and find a gap. Candidates who arrive at the end of the slip at 40mph, with no gap available and nowhere to go, panic. The correct approach involves reading the main carriageway from the start of the slip road, selecting a gap, and building to matching speed before reaching the merge point. DriveSQ specifically practises the approach reading technique, not just the merge itself.

📍 B5215 → East Lancs Road A580 slip → join 70mph national speed limit → next exit
6
Town Centre Nav

Leigh Town Centre A578 / Chapel Street

Pedestrianised zone boundaries with active loading restrictions. Bus-only sections run adjacent to the test route — route reading is essential to avoid bus-only roads. Loading restrictions on Chapel Street operate 8am–6pm on weekdays. An examiner who sees a candidate drive into a bus-only section or park in a loading bay will issue a serious fault. Knowing the exact boundary of the pedestrianised zone and where bus-only begins is non-negotiable. DriveSQ students walk this area before driving it.

📍 A578 King Street → Chapel Street boundary → Spinning Gate → return route
7
Manoeuvre Zone

Bradshawgate Bay Park

Angled bays on a slight camber adjacent to the DTC. The exit from these bays requires a right-turn across a one-way street — candidates who don't check the one-way markings before committing to the exit receive a fault. The camber means the car will roll toward the footpath side if brake pressure drops during the manoeuvre — examiners specifically observe footpath clearance. Our students practise this bay park with attention to the camber reference points and the one-way exit check that generic schools skip.

Local Knowledge

8 Leigh Hazards Other
Schools Never Cover

The specific, local details that separate a DriveSQ Leigh student from someone who learned without local knowledge.

Bradshawgate DTC Exit — One-Way Right-Turn

The DTC on Bradshawgate sits on a one-way street. When you leave the test, the only legal exit is a right-turn onto King Street. Candidates who don't check the one-way markings — and attempt to turn left out of habit or because it feels like the natural direction — receive a serious fault before they've driven 20 metres. This is Leigh DTC's most notorious start-of-test error. Every DriveSQ Leigh student practises this exit specifically, multiple times, before test day. The examiner is watching for this check from the moment you indicate to pull away.

Atherleigh Way Dual Carriageway Slip Entry

The A572 Atherleigh Way is 40mph. Entering from the A579 slip requires full mirror-signal-manoeuvre at speed while reading the dual carriageway for a gap. The slip is short — candidates who reach the merge point too slowly cannot safely join fast-moving traffic without causing other drivers to brake. The correct technique is to start reading the carriageway from the slip road entry, target a specific gap, and build speed to match before the merge point. Generic instructors who haven't specifically taught this slip get caught out — and so do their students.

A579 Warrington Road Speed Sequence — Three Changes

The A579 through Abram has three speed limit changes: 30mph into the village, 40mph through the open section, then 30mph again before Platt Bridge. These changes appear across roughly a mile of road that looks broadly similar throughout. Candidates who don't know the signs are coming carry 40mph into a 30mph zone and collect speeding minors — or they see the sign late and brake sharply, collecting a hesitancy fault. Knowing where each sign is located is the only reliable prevention. DriveSQ students are briefed on every limit sign location on this road.

Bag Lane Pedestrian Phase — Independent Signal

The Bag Lane crossroads on the B5215 has an independent pedestrian phase that can run separately from the main traffic signal cycle. Candidates who watch the main phase and judge it's their turn to go, without checking whether a pedestrian phase is simultaneously active, will drive through a red pedestrian signal. This is a serious fault. The independent phase isn't long, but it can appear while through-traffic is on green. Checking for active pedestrian phases before moving at any signal-controlled junction is a fundamental DriveSQ teaching point — here it is tested with real consequences.

East Lancs Road Slip Road — Short Merge onto 70mph

The East Lancs Road A580 slip road from the Leigh area is short. Candidates have a limited distance to match the 70mph national speed limit traffic on the main carriageway before the merge point. Those who arrive at the merge point still doing 40–50mph, with traffic behind them on the slip and a full carriageway ahead, have no good options. The preparation technique — reading traffic from the slip entry, targeting a specific gap, building speed toward match-speed — is taught explicitly by DriveSQ. Candidates who have only been told "build your speed" are not prepared for the length and pace of this specific slip.

Chapel Street Loading Zone — Active Restrictions

Chapel Street loading restrictions operate 8am–6pm on weekdays. Adjacent bus-only sections mean the area around Leigh town centre is an active restrictions minefield for candidates without local knowledge. A single wrong turn — into a bus lane, into a loading zone during enforcement hours, or stopping in a no-stopping area — is an immediate serious fault. DriveSQ students walk this area before they drive it. The pedestrianised zone boundary is signed, but the bus-only sections are only clear if you know the road layout. We map it before every test.

Leigh Sports Village Pedestrians — Match Days

Leigh Sports Village hosts Leigh Centurions Rugby League on St Helens Road. On match days — predominantly weekend evenings and some midweek — the volume of pedestrians walking along and crossing St Helens Road increases significantly. Pedestrians cross mid-road between parked cars and at informal crossing points that aren't zebra crossings. Test appointments that coincide with pre-match arrivals or post-match departures encounter this hazard. DriveSQ instructs students on this specifically — not all test times will have match-day pedestrians, but knowing the risk is there changes how you scan on St Helens Road.

Mosley Common Level Crossing — Amber = Stop

The B5211 Mosley Common Road has a level crossing in the M29 area of Tyldesley. The rule at level crossings is: amber means stop unless you are so close to the line that stopping would be dangerous. A flashing white light means proceed. Candidates who treat an amber crossing signal as a "speed up and clear it" situation — the car driver's instinct at traffic lights — risk both a serious fault and genuine danger. Candidates who stop unnecessarily at a white light when it's clear also fail. DriveSQ teaches the exact level crossing signal rules and practises the Mosley Common crossing specifically.

Leigh Sports Village

Leigh Town Centre — Chapel Street

Chapel Street loading zones and pedestrianised sections are active restrictions zones with bus-only sections immediately adjacent. East Lancs Road A580

One of the busiest A-roads in the North West. Slip roads from the Leigh test area require full MSMM technique at 70mph national speed limit. The A580 carries high volumes of HGV traffic, particularly during business hours. Candidates who have never merged onto this road before their test are at a significant disadvantage. We specifically practice A580 joins, not just general dual carriageway work.

🏭

Astley Green Colliery

A heritage site on the Astley area fringe, accessed via narrow lanes on Astley Road. Some test routes pass near this area where road widths reduce and passing places are limited. The colliery site attracts visitors, and the access road is used by both site traffic and local residents. Candidates who haven't driven these narrow sections before their test are surprised by how quickly oncoming vehicles appear.

🏫

Leigh Schools — Morning Rush

Schools serving Hindley and Tyldesley (M29/WN7) generate significant congestion between 8:15–8:45am. Test appointments during this window encounter queues on residential roads that are normally clear, as well as pedestrians and cyclists at informal crossing points near school gates. The morning rush adds 5–10 minutes to journey times on affected roads and changes the scan pattern required. DriveSQ students are briefed on which roads are affected by which schools.

⛽

Leigh Guided Busway

A bus-only corridor running on the old rail line through Leigh — it is for buses only and is not an option for car traffic under any circumstances. Sections of the busway run adjacent to or near road junctions used on test routes. Candidates confused about the busway boundary occasionally attempt to use it. DriveSQ maps the busway boundary during lessons so students know exactly where it starts and stops.
