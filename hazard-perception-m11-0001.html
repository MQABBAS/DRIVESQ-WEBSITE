FAQs — Hazard Perception in M11

What is hazard perception on the driving test?

Hazard perception is part of the DVSA theory test — 14 video clips filmed from the driver's view, each containing at least one developing hazard. You click when you spot a hazard developing. The earlier you click (within the scoring window), the higher your score. Pass mark is 44/75.

What is a developing hazard in driving?

A developing hazard is a situation that will require you to take action — slow down, stop, or change direction. Examples include a pedestrian stepping toward the road, a car pulling out of a side street, or a cyclist moving into your lane. You score points for clicking as the hazard develops, not when first visible.

How do I improve my hazard perception score?

Scan ahead continuously and look for clues that a hazard is developing — pedestrians near the kerb, parked cars with passengers visible, vehicles at Give Way lines. Click at the moment the hazard requires action, not when first spotted. Practise official DVSA clips daily for 2–3 weeks before your theory test.

Does DriveSQ teach hazard perception in M11?

Yes. DriveSQ links hazard perception training to real driving in M11 — your instructor explains developing hazards as you encounter them on the road. This real-world context makes hazard perception clips much easier to read because you've seen the same situations in real life.
