Legal basics — every lesson

Law = legal requirement · DVSA = DVSA standard / guidance · DriveSQ = our professional standard

  • LawNo hand-held phone. You MUST NOT use a hand-held phone or similar device for any purpose while supervising a learner driver (Highway Code rule 149). Put it on Do Not Disturb before the lesson.
  • LawProvisional licence. A learner must hold a valid provisional licence for the category they're driving. Check it before their first drive and if anything changes.
  • LawEyesight. A driver must be able to read a number plate from 20 metres (with glasses or contact lenses if they use them). Check this before the first lesson — and stop if they can't.
  • LawL plates must be displayed on the front and back of the car so they can be seen easily.
  • LawSeat belts must be worn where fitted (Highway Code rule 99).
  • LawInsurance, tax and MOT must be valid for the car and cover the learner.
  • DVSAADI badge (or trainee licence) on display in the car during paid lessons; dual controls checked before each lesson.
  • DVSAFit to teach. Don't teach tired — tiredness increases the risk of a collision (Highway Code rule 91). Plan breaks on long days.
Never: teach under the influence of alcohol or drugs, leave a learner without proper supervision, or let a learner drive on a motorway unless it's with an approved instructor in a car fitted with dual controls.

The shape of a quality lesson (2 hours)

DVSA expects the pupil to reflect at the end — on a standards check they're given about 3 minutes to do this.

10 minRecap & agree goals. Check the portal notes, ask how their practice went, and agree one or two clear goals with them — what they want to improve too.
80 minPractise the goal. Explain or demonstrate → guided practice → independent practice. Short, safe stops for feedback when needed.
15 minApply it. A slightly harder route or independent driving (sat nav or road signs) to test the new skill.
15 minReflect & record. Pull over safely. Ask them first: what went well, what was hard, what next. Add your view, set the next goal, then log the lesson in the portal. Before they go home, ask for an honest Google review.

1 · Lesson planning

DVSA: did you identify the pupil's goals and needs, use a suitable structure and practice area, and adapt the plan when needed?

Know the learner
  • Read last lesson's notes in the portal
  • Ask what they've practised and how they feel
  • Note anything that affects learning (nerves, confidence, needs)
Set the right goal
  • Specific: "approach and emerge at T-junctions", not "general driving"
  • Achievable for their level today
  • Agreed together, not imposed
Choose the area
  • Quiet roads for new skills, busier roads to apply them
  • Avoid routes beyond their ability — it isn't safe or effective
Adapt
  • If it's too easy or too hard, change the plan and say why
  • Weather, traffic or confidence may mean a different route

2 · Risk management

DVSA: did the pupil understand how responsibility for risk is shared, were directions clear and in good time, were you aware of everything, was any intervention timely, and did you give feedback on safety-critical moments?

On a standards check, scoring 7 or less on risk management — or behaving in a way that puts anyone in immediate danger — is an automatic fail.
  • Agree who does what at the start: "I'll talk you through it; if I need to use my dual controls I'll tell you why afterwards."
  • Directions early and clear: "At the end of the road, turn left" — given in good time, never at the last second.
  • Read the road for them: scan well ahead, check mirrors and the learner's feet, hands and eyes.
  • Intervene early and in proportion: a verbal prompt first, then dual controls only when needed.
  • Debrief every intervention: what happened, why, and what they'll do next time — a learning point, not a telling-off.

3 · Teaching & learning strategies

DVSA: style suited to the pupil, pupil encouraged to analyse and take responsibility, accurate information, timely feedback, questions answered, a non-discriminatory manner, and reflection at the end.

Coach, don't just instruct
  • Ask before you tell: "What did you notice at that junction?"
  • Let them work out the fix for safe mistakes
Match their style
  • Some learn by seeing (demo, diagram), some by doing, some by talking it through
  • Nervous learners: smaller steps, more encouragement
Feedback that works
  • Specific: what went well, one thing to change, how to change it
  • Timely — stop safely if a point can't wait
Accurate information
  • Teach to the Highway Code and DVSA guidance
  • Not sure? Say so, check, and follow it up next lesson
Build independence
  • Full talk-through → prompts → independent driving
  • Practice ideas between lessons and Student Portal mock tests
Respect everyone
  • A non-discriminatory manner throughout (and it's the law under the Equality Act 2010)
  • Patient, encouraging, professional language

Professional conduct

  • On time, every time. Running late? Tell the student before the lesson starts.
  • Clean, safe car with L plates, dual controls checked, and your badge on display.
  • Professional communication only — keep messages about lessons, and keep boundaries clear, especially with under-18s.
  • Protect personal data — student details are for arranging lessons only; never share them (UK GDPR).
  • Payments only as agreed with DriveSQ and the student; give clear receipts.
  • Honest progress. Don't put a learner in for a test before their mock tests show they're ready.
  • Update DriveSQ — mark new referrals Booked / Not booked yet / Can't reach / Not going ahead, and log every lesson in the portal.

Before they go home — ask for a Google review

Reviews are one of the main reasons new learners choose DriveSQ. Your portal reminds you when a lesson has just finished.

  • When: at the end of the lesson, once you've parked and the engine is off — never while they're driving. Straight after a pass is a great moment too.
  • How: hand them the DriveSQ Google review tag to tap with their phone (or scan its QR code). No tag with you? Tap Ask now in your portal to show the QR code on your screen, or send the link on WhatsApp so they can do it at home.
  • What to say: “Thanks for today! If you're happy with your lessons, could you leave us an honest Google review? It really helps other learners find us.”
  • Ask everyone — not only learners you think are happy. Google doesn't allow businesses to pick and choose who they ask.
  • Never offer money, discounts or free lessons for a review, and never write, edit or post a review for a learner (or on their phone). Fake and incentivised reviews are banned by Google and are unlawful under UK consumer law.
