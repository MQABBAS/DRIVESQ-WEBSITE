Test Centre

Manchester (Longford Park) Driving Test Centre

The DTC Urmston students use — and the things no driving school bothers to tell you about it.

Address: Longford Park, Stretford M32 8PR — approximately 3 miles from Urmston via Chester Road A56, accessed via Edge Lane
Test Duration: Approximately 38–40 minutes driving + pre-test briefing
Getting There by Bus: 263 and X57 services stop on Chester Road A56 near Longford Park entrance, approximately 3 minutes' walk from the DTC
Documents: Provisional licence, theory pass certificate (within 2 years), glasses if prescribed for driving
Test Fee: £62 weekday / £75 weekend or bank holiday (DVSA 2025 rates)
The exit every Urmston candidate must know: The DTC exit onto Edge Lane is left-turn only. Examiners specifically watch whether candidates position correctly in the exit bay before turning. Cutting the corner causes an immediate fault. This is one of the first manoeuvres of the test — DriveSQ students practise it in every lesson that ends at the DTC.
Longford Park Setting: The DTC sits within Longford Park, a public park with pedestrian, jogger, and cyclist activity near the entrance. Pedestrians cross the approach road without warning. The examiner's assessment begins from the moment the car leaves the bay — treat the park approach as live test territory.
Bay Parking Manoeuvre: Bay parking in the Longford Park DTC car park is frequently tested. The bays are set on a downhill gradient — vehicle creep and camber affect staying within the lines. Correcting for camber is a specific skill that must be practised here, not in a flat car park elsewhere.
First routes from the DTC: Most Urmston test routes head immediately onto Edge Lane and Chester Road A56 — the Metrolink tram crossing appears within the first few minutes. Do not treat the departure from the DTC as a warm-up; it is the test.
Real Test Routes

The 7 Urmston Test Routes
That Examiners Actually Use

Mapped from real test experience. Our students practise every one of these before their test day.

1
Width Restriction

Urmston Lane / Crofts Bank Road

The spine road through Urmston town centre serves the 263 bus route and is lined with parked cars on both sides for much of its length, creating a single-file corridor through the busiest section. Priority negotiation with oncoming vehicles requires clear, confident decision-making — hesitation or pulling too wide are among the most common faults recorded on this route. The Give Way at Station Road junction requires early recognition of priority markings. Candidates who have never specifically practised Crofts Bank Road are frequently caught out by the combination of bus stops, parked cars, and the Station Road junction in quick succession.

📍 Urmston Lane → Crofts Bank Road: Urmston town centre spine road → Station Road Give Way junction → bus route 263 stops
2
School Zones

Flixton Road through Flixton Village

A 30mph residential route through Flixton village passing Flixton Girls School and St Michael's RC Primary. Two 20mph camera-enforced zones are active during school run times — the cameras are mounted on poles rather than overhead gantries, making them easy to miss if a candidate hasn't specifically been shown where they are. Pedestrian crossings at Church Road add an observation demand. Candidates who don't know the exact location of each school zone and camera point on this route will drive through at 30mph in a 20mph zone — an immediate serious fault.

📍 Flixton Road: Urmston → Flixton village → Flixton Girls School → St Michael's RC Primary → Church Road pedestrian crossing
3
Hidden Hazard

A57 Warburton Lane toward Irlam

A 40mph rural-feel road with a concealed dip near the Carrington turn that reduces forward visibility. The approach is at 40mph — not seeing the dip in advance causes late braking reactions that generate a driving fault for poor hazard awareness. Farm access roads join Warburton Lane requiring a slow hazard approach each time. This route has the feel of a quiet country road but demands continuous hazard observation. DriveSQ instructors flag the Carrington dip specifically on every lesson using this route so that candidates already know it is coming when it appears on test day.

📍 A57 Warburton Lane: Urmston → Carrington (hidden dip) → Irlam → farm access junctions throughout
4
Tram Crossing

Chester Road A56 through Stretford

The A56 Chester Road carries Urmston test routes into Stretford, where the Metrolink tram crossing at the Stretford stop is encountered within the first part of the test. The tram signal sequence uses a flashing amber that means proceed only if the stop is clear — candidates who don't understand the flashing amber freeze mid-road as the tram enters, an immediate serious fault. A 40mph section follows the tram crossing. Old Trafford match day congestion on the M16 approach can affect this route — examiners avoid the worst streets but some congestion is unavoidable.

📍 Chester Road A56: Stretford Metrolink stop (flashing amber) → 40mph section → Old Trafford M16 approach
5
Dual Carriageway

M17 Trafford Way Dual Carriageway

A 50mph section near the Trafford Centre — the highest-speed section on any Longford Park DTC route. Heavy HGV traffic uses M17 throughout the day, approaching at 50mph alongside a learner car. The Trafford Centre slip road approach requires early mirror checks, confident lane selection, and no hesitation at the entry. Mandatory lane discipline is formally assessed throughout. Candidates who have never driven at 50mph near heavy goods vehicles generate fault after fault on this section. DriveSQ builds dual carriageway confidence specifically in every Urmston lesson programme, not as an afterthought.

📍 M17 Trafford Way: 50mph dual carriageway → Trafford Centre slip road approach → HGV-heavy throughout
6
Residential Zone

Woodsend Road toward Davyhulme

A 30mph residential route through Davyhulme on bus route 263, with regular speed humps and a school zone near St Mary's RC Primary. Speed humps require smooth speed management — hard braking on approach is a fault. The school zone on Woodsend Road is a known test candidate catch point; the 20mph restriction during school hours is camera-enforced. DriveSQ students are shown the exact position of each speed hump and each school zone sign on Woodsend Road before their test, so there are no surprises on the day.

📍 Woodsend Road: Urmston → Davyhulme → St Mary's RC Primary school zone → speed humps throughout → bus route 263
7
Manoeuvre Zone

Longford Park DTC Bay Parking

Reversing into bays in the Longford Park DTC car park — frequently tested at this centre. The bays sit on a downhill gradient with road camber that requires active correction to stay within the lines. Vehicle creep during the manoeuvre is a common fault for candidates who haven't practised this specific slope. Manual candidates must hold the biting point or apply the handbrake; automatic candidates must maintain firm brake pressure throughout. The examiner observes from the moment reverse is selected. Reference points developed in a flat car park do not transfer directly to a sloped, cambered surface — only practising the real thing at Longford Park prepares a candidate for this.

📍 Longford Park DTC car park · downhill gradient · road camber · bay on right · examiner observes throughout

The specific, local details that separate a DriveSQ Urmston student from someone who learned without local knowledge.

Trafford Way M17 Dual Carriageway — 50mph HGV Traffic

The 50mph section near Trafford Centre is the highest-speed section of any Longford Park DTC route. HGVs travelling at 50mph alongside a learner car require early mirror checks, confident lane discipline, and no hesitation at entry. Many Urmston students have only driven on 30mph residential roads before their test — the step up to 50mph with heavy goods vehicles alongside is significant. DriveSQ builds dual carriageway confidence specifically over multiple sessions, not as a single end-of-programme lesson. Candidates who haven't specifically trained at 50mph alongside HGVs generate lane discipline faults throughout this section.

Chester Road A56 Metrolink Crossing — Flashing Amber

The Stretford Metrolink stop at Chester Road is encountered within 5 minutes of most Urmston test routes. The tram signal uses a flashing amber phase: flashing amber at a stop means proceed only if the stop is clear. Candidates who don't understand the flashing amber freeze mid-road as the tram enters — an immediate serious fault. DriveSQ teaches the full tram signal procedure from lesson one, because this crossing appears on nearly every Longford Park DTC route that Urmston students will drive on test day. Getting this wrong is one of the most common reasons Urmston candidates fail.

Flixton Road School Zones — Camera Poles, Not Gantries

Flixton Girls School and St Michael's RC Primary on Flixton Road generate two 20mph camera-enforced zones active 8–9am and 3–4pm. The cameras are mounted on poles rather than overhead gantries — easy to miss for candidates who are looking at road height rather than pavement level. Candidates who haven't been specifically shown the camera positions on this route continue at 30mph through a 20mph zone during a test that falls in a school run window. DriveSQ students know the exact start and end of every school zone on Flixton Road before they ever sit a test.

Old Trafford Match Day Congestion — M16 Approach

When Manchester United or Lancashire County Cricket Club play at home, M16 and Chester Road A56 experience standstill traffic within 2 hours of kick-off. Test times within these windows require extended route awareness — examiners avoid the worst streets but some test routes are unavoidable. A test booked on a match day morning or afternoon will encounter this congestion. DriveSQ instructors advise Urmston students on upcoming fixture dates near their test booking, and where possible help them choose test slots that avoid the worst match-day congestion patterns.

Crofts Bank Road Parked Car Filtering — Priority Negotiation

Crofts Bank Road through Urmston town centre has parked cars on both sides creating a single-file corridor. Priority negotiation with oncoming vehicles requires clear decision-making — hesitation or pulling too wide are among the most commonly recorded faults on this route. The rules are specific: if a parked car is on your side of the road, you give way to oncoming traffic. Candidates who have not practised this corridor under instruction often freeze when an oncoming vehicle appears, or pull wide into the oncoming lane prematurely. DriveSQ teaches priority negotiation on Crofts Bank Road specifically, not on a generic quiet street.

Warburton Lane Hidden Dip — Carrington Turn

A57 Warburton Lane has a concealed dip near the Carrington turn that reduces forward visibility. The approach is 40mph — not seeing the dip in advance causes late braking reactions. The rule is to treat any reduction in forward visibility as a potential hazard: maintain following distance and be prepared to stop. Candidates who haven't driven this road before are caught entirely off-guard when the dip appears. DriveSQ instructors flag the Carrington dip specifically every time they use this route with a student — so that on test day, the candidate already knows it is there and maintains the correct approach speed.

Davyhulme Roundabout — Multi-Arm Irregular Priority

The roundabout at the junction of Woodsend Road, Urmston Lane, and Stretford Road is multi-arm with irregular entry priorities. The correct lane for exiting onto Woodsend Road is not the one that feels natural to candidates approaching from Urmston Lane for the first time. Incorrect lane selection at this roundabout is a common fault — candidates who arrive in the wrong lane either take the wrong exit or make an unsafe lane change on the roundabout itself. DriveSQ students practise this specific roundabout and its lane requirements before their test.

Longford Park Pedestrian Crossings — No Lights, No Markings

Longford Park has two uncontrolled pedestrian crossing points on the DTC access road. Pedestrians — walkers, joggers, families — cross without waiting for vehicles and without any formal crossing infrastructure. There are no traffic lights, no zebra stripes, no pelican signals. Candidates must observe and yield without a formal signal prompt. The examiner's assessment begins the moment the car moves off from the bay — yielding to pedestrians on the park access road before the test officially starts on public roads is already being assessed. Candidates who treat the park road as a pre-test warm-up are caught out immediately.

Irlam Crossroads — Flashing Amber Arrow

Flixton Green near St Michael's Church attracts lunchtime and weekend pedestrian activity from the village pub and café. On test day the examiner notes whether a candidate spotted pedestrians preparing to cross early, or only braked when they were already in the road. DriveSQ students are taught to read pedestrian behaviour on Flixton Road before they reach the Green.
