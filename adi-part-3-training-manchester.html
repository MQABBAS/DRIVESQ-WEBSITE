ADI Part 3 training in Manchester

Rules and fees checked on GOV.UK on 25 November 2026 · Updated 25 November 2026

The ADI Part 3 is the last of the three tests you pass to become an Approved Driving Instructor, and for most people it is the hardest. It is not a driving test. A DVSA examiner watches you teach a real pupil for about 45 minutes and scores how well you plan, manage risk and teach. DriveSQ trains instructors for exactly this test in Greater Manchester.

In one line: you can book Part 3 once you have passed Part 2; it costs £111; you get a maximum of three attempts; and you score 31 or more out of 51 to pass.

What the ADI Part 3 actually tests

Parts 1 and 2 test what you know and how you drive. Part 3 tests something different: whether you can help someone else learn. The examiner sits in the back and scores 17 areas of competence, each from 0 to 3, grouped into three categories:

  • Lesson planning: finding out what the pupil needs and agreeing a sensible plan.
  • Risk management: keeping the lesson safe and making sure the pupil understands who is responsible for what.
  • Teaching and learning strategies: how you explain, question, give feedback and adapt as the lesson goes.

Most trainees hold a trainee driving instructor licence (the pink badge) while they prepare, so that they can teach paying pupils under the rules and gain real practice before the test.

How the road to your badge fits together

  1. Pass Part 1 (theory and hazard perception). It is valid for two years for your Part 3 attempts.
  2. Pass Part 2 (your own advanced driving).
  3. Get a trainee licence if you want to teach before qualifying: £140, lasts six months, needs at least 40 hours of training from a qualified ADI.
  4. Prepare for Part 3 with training, practice lessons and feedback.
  5. Take Part 3 and, on a pass, apply for your first ADI badge.

What the official fees add up to

If you pass every stage at the first attempt, the DVSA's own fees come to £743. That excludes training, books and revision materials, and the cost of a suitable car.

Each retake costs the full fee again. A failed Part 3 attempt adds £111, so a plan that gets you ready before you book is the cheapest plan.

Are you ready? A quick check

  • You can plan a 45-minute lesson for a pupil you have just met, in about three minutes, in your head.
  • You can name the risk in a lesson before it happens and say who is responsible for what.
  • Your pupil does most of the talking about what they are finding hard. You are not lecturing.
  • You adjust the lesson when it is not working, instead of carrying on with your plan.
  • A watcher who knows the marking has seen you score comfortably above the pass mark more than once.

If you tick fewer than three of these, book more preparation first. A fail means a gap, a fee and a wait.

Questions people ask

What is the ADI Part 3 test?

It is the last of the three tests to qualify as an Approved Driving Instructor. A DVSA examiner observes you giving a lesson of about 45 minutes to a pupil you bring, and marks 17 areas of competence.

How much does the ADI Part 3 cost?

The DVSA test fee is £111, checked on GOV.UK on 25 November 2026. If you fail and retake, you pay the full fee again. Training with DriveSQ is separate: message us for the current price.

How many times can I take the ADI Part 3?

You can retake after a first or second fail, but if you fail a third time you must stop giving paid lessons straight away and retake and pass Parts 1 and 2 before trying again.

What score do I need to pass the ADI Part 3?

Out of 51 marks, 31 to 42 is Grade B, 43 to 51 is Grade A, and 0 to 30 is a fail. A score of 7 or less in the risk management category is also a fail.
