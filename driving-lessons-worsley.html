Test Centre

Eccles Driving Test Centre

The DTC Worsley students use — and the things no driving school bothers to tell you about it.

Address: Liverpool Road, Eccles M30 0BH — on the A57 Liverpool Road arterial road through Eccles town centre
Test Duration: Approximately 38–40 minutes driving + pre-test briefing
Parking: The DTC car park is used for the angled bay parking manoeuvre. Do not park in a test bay by accident. Buses 67 and 100 stop on Liverpool Road, 1 minute walk. Arrive 15 minutes early.
Documents: Provisional licence, theory pass certificate (within 2 years), glasses if prescribed for driving
Test Fee: £62 weekday / £75 weekend (DVSA 2025 rates)
What nobody tells you: The DTC car park exit onto Liverpool Road is a right-turn only in the morning peak — examiners test gap selection very carefully as A57 Liverpool Road carries fast-moving traffic. Candidates who have not specifically practised this exit hesitate or select an unsafe gap. The examiner is watching from the moment you select gear in the car park.
A57 Liverpool Road: Liverpool Road is a busy 40mph arterial corridor with multiple bus stops, layby pull-outs, and junctions with side roads. Bus movements back into the live lane are a key test of hazard anticipation — candidates who haven't driven this road before are frequently caught out.
Public Transport: Buses 67 and 100 stop on Liverpool Road, 1 minute walk from the DTC. Eccles town centre is a 5-minute walk if you need to arrive by tram from Salford or central Manchester.
Pass rate: Eccles DTC is broadly in line with the national average. Strong local knowledge of the Liverpool Road exit and East Lancs Road slip is the key differentiator between prepared and unprepared candidates.
Real Test Routes

The 7 Worsley Test Routes
That Examiners Actually Use

Mapped from real test experience. Our students practise every one of these before their test day.

1
Bus Stops / Fast Road

A57 Liverpool Road Eccles

Liverpool Road is a fast 40mph arterial with multiple bus stops that have layby pull-outs. Candidates must anticipate buses re-entering the live lane — a bus indicating to pull out has priority once it is moving, but many learners fail to read the signals early enough. The road also features junctions with minor side roads where emerging traffic gives way to Liverpool Road — but the speed of approaching vehicles catches out candidates who aren't scanning far ahead. Most test routes begin or end on this road, so hesitation and positioning faults accumulate here at a higher rate than anywhere else in Eccles.

📍 A57 Liverpool Road · Eccles town centre · DTC exit and approach
2
Concealed Junction

A572 Barton Road / Winton

The A572 Barton Road is a 30mph residential route with a concealed side road at Winton village that catches many candidates out. The major road (A572) has no give-way signs for the minor road junction — meaning traffic can emerge from the side road without warning. Candidates travelling on the A572 must anticipate this junction even though no signs remind them of it. The road through Winton also has slightly substandard visibility at the bend approaching the junction, which reduces reaction time. DriveSQ students are briefed on this hazard by name before they encounter it.

📍 A572 Barton Road → Winton village → concealed side road junction
3
Heritage / Narrow

B5211 Worsley Road

The B5211 Worsley Road runs at 40mph through Worsley village — home of the Worsley Navigable Levels and the Bridgewater Canal junction. Near the heritage area, lanes narrow significantly and tourist traffic creates unpredictable slow-downs, especially at weekends and bank holidays. The road surface changes near the canal bridge, and parked cars on the heritage side reduce effective road width. Candidates who push through at 40mph in the narrowed section regularly receive positioning faults. Knowing exactly where the lane narrows — and slowing proactively before you can see the obstruction — is what DriveSQ teaches here.

📍 B5211 Worsley Road → Worsley village → Bridgewater Canal heritage area
4
Dual Carriageway

A580 East Lancs Road / Boothstown

The A580 East Lancs Road is one of the northwest's busiest A-roads, running at a national speed limit of 70mph on the dual carriageway sections near Boothstown. Candidates must match traffic speed on the slip road entry and execute the full MSMM (Mirror–Signal–Manoeuvre–Mirror) sequence before joining. This is the most technically demanding section of any Worsley test route — candidates who have only practised 30mph residential roads are overwhelmed at the slip. Many fail here not from a poor manoeuvre but from underspeed on the slip road, causing the examiner to apply the dual control. Every DriveSQ Worsley student practises the A580 entry before test day.

📍 A580 East Lancs Road → Boothstown → 70mph dual carriageway slip entry
5
Pedestrian Phase

A576 Walkden Road / Walkden

Walkden Road through Walkden town centre is 30mph and features a pedestrian phase at the shopping precinct that is independent of the vehicle signal cycle. This means the green light for vehicles may appear, but the pedestrian crossing phase is still active — candidates who move off on the vehicle green without checking the pedestrian phase have failed before they've moved 10 metres. The Tesco Extra on the precinct approach also creates heavy AM peak traffic, with vehicles turning across the main road. Our students practise the specific sequence at this junction until it is automatic.

📍 A576 Walkden Road → Walkden precinct → independent pedestrian phase
6
20mph Village

M30 Monton Road / Monton

Monton Road through Monton village is 20mph with tight lanes, parked cars on both sides, and alternating priority. The pub car park exits onto Monton Road unpredictably — vehicles reversing or exiting without checking properly are common on weekend afternoon test slots. Candidates must identify the alternating priority correctly (yellow give-way diamonds on the side with the obstruction, not always obvious in the moment), and maintain 20mph without dropping to 10mph. Many candidates treat Monton Road as a hazard zone and crawl — which generates undue hesitancy faults. The balance is what DriveSQ teaches specifically for this road.

📍 Monton Road 20mph → Monton village → pub car park exits · parked cars both sides
7
Manoeuvre Zone

Eccles DTC Angled Bay Park

Angled bay parking is the most commonly tested manoeuvre at Eccles DTC — the car park has angled bays and the exit back onto Liverpool Road requires a right-turn into fast-moving A57 traffic. Gap selection and observation are specifically assessed here. Candidates who reverse into the angled bay cleanly but then rush the exit — selecting an unsafe gap because they feel the examiner is watching — fail on the exit, not the park. DriveSQ students practise the complete sequence: angled bay reverse, observation check, and a composed right-turn exit onto Liverpool Road.

📍 Eccles DTC car park · angled bay reverse · right-turn exit onto Liverpool Road A57

Local Knowledge

8 Worsley Hazards Other
Schools Never Cover

The specific, local details that separate a DriveSQ Worsley student from someone who learned without local knowledge.

Liverpool Road DTC Exit Right-Turn

The Eccles DTC car park exit requires a right-turn onto the A57 Liverpool Road — a fast 40mph arterial with heavy two-way traffic. In the morning peak, right-turns are restricted and gap selection is assessed by the examiner. Candidates who have not specifically practised this exit hesitate and either select an unsafe gap (serious fault) or wait so long they generate an undue hesitancy fault. DriveSQ students practise the Liverpool Road exit from multiple approaches, including after the angled bay parking manoeuvre, so the gap-selection decision is instinctive on test day.

A572 Winton Concealed Side Road

On the A572 Barton Road at Winton village, a side road emerges onto the major road without give-way signs for the side road traffic. The A572 carries priority through Winton, but there are no yellow give-way lines or hazard warnings at this junction to alert the candidate. Vehicles from the side road sometimes emerge slowly into the path of A572 traffic, relying on it to yield — which it is not obliged to do. Candidates who don't know this junction exists arrive at it without anticipation. Our Worsley instructors brief students on this specific junction by name before they first drive it.

Worsley Village Narrow Lanes B5211

The heritage area around Worsley Navigable Levels and the Bridgewater Canal junction brings tourist traffic onto the B5211 Worsley Road, especially at weekends. Tourists unfamiliar with the road park close to the lane edge or turn unpredictably to find canal access points. The road itself narrows near the old boathouse section, and the lane width is technically sufficient for two vehicles but only with precise positioning. Candidates who drift wide — which most do without specific coaching — clip the road edge marker or force oncoming traffic to stop. Knowing where the narrowing begins, and how to position through it, is a specific DriveSQ Worsley coaching point.

East Lancs Road A580 Slip Entry at 70mph

The A580 East Lancs Road has a national speed limit of 70mph on the dual carriageway sections near Boothstown. The slip road entry is short, which means candidates have limited distance to accelerate from the give-way line to 70mph before merging. The full MSMM sequence — Mirror, Signal, Manoeuvre, Mirror — must be completed on the slip. Candidates who have never driven at 70mph before, or who are used to urban 30mph driving, typically reach only 50–55mph before running out of slip. This results in the examiner using the dual controls or a serious fault for unsafe joining. DriveSQ specifically programmes A580 slip entry sessions for Worsley students before test day.

Walkden Precinct Independent Pedestrian Phase

At the Walkden shopping precinct, the pedestrian crossing phase operates independently of the vehicle signal cycle. The vehicle green light may show, but the pedestrian countdown is still active. Candidates who treat the vehicle green as permission to move, without first checking the pedestrian phase, drive across an active pedestrian crossing — a serious fault that ends the test. This sequence is unlike standard pelican crossings and is not covered in the Highway Code with enough specificity for most learners to recognise it when they see it for the first time. DriveSQ Worsley students practise this junction on every route that includes Walkden.

Monton Road 20mph Pub Exits

The pub car park on Monton Road exits directly onto the 20mph village road without a clear sightline for either exiting vehicles or approaching traffic. On weekend afternoon test slots, vehicles reverse out of the car park or exit at low speed without fully clearing the road. The 20mph zone reduces approach speed but also means candidates are close before they can react. The narrow road, parked cars on both sides, and the pub exit combine to create a series of micro-hazards within 100 metres that demand continuous scanning. Most candidates focus on the parked cars (correct) but miss the car park exit (specific to this road).

Barton Bridge High Winds

Barton Bridge on the A57 is an exposed high bridge crossing the Manchester Ship Canal. On windy days, the bridge creates a channelling effect — wind is funnelled across the deck and can noticeably affect steering, particularly in light vehicles. Candidates who have never driven on an exposed bridge in a cross-wind are startled by the steering feedback and either over-correct or briefly lose their lane position. The examiner expects candidates to maintain smooth lane position through the bridge. DriveSQ instructors specifically mention Barton Bridge in pre-lesson briefings when wind is forecast.

Eccles One-Way System

Eccles town centre has a one-way system on Church Street and Regent Street that requires confident route-reading. Candidates who do not know the system rely entirely on examiner directions, which can arrive too close to the required turning. The one-way streets also have bus lane sections that are easy to enter incorrectly when navigating under pressure. First-time drivers in Eccles town centre regularly take wrong lanes even when they know the rules — because the signs are only comprehensible if you already know the road. DriveSQ drives the Eccles one-way system in lessons so it is familiar, not a navigation challenge, on test day.

Worsley Knowledge

Know Worsley Before Your Test

The context that turns a technically capable driver into a locally aware one — and locally aware drivers pass.

🌿

Worsley Village & Bridgewater Canal

Worsley is a heritage village and the origin of the Bridgewater Canal network. Weekend tourist traffic on Worsley Road and the village approach creates conditions not present mid-week — slow-moving visitors, unexpected parking, and pedestrians crossing between the canal towpath and the road. Test routes through the village are unchanged but road conditions vary significantly by day and time.

🏭

Barton Aerodrome

Barton Aerodrome (City Airport Manchester) is located near Eccles and Barton. Light aircraft approach and departure paths pass close to roads in the area. While aircraft are not a road hazard, candidates occasionally become distracted by aircraft overhead — particularly on the A57 approach to Barton Bridge. DriveSQ instructors mention the aerodrome so students expect this distraction and don't drift off their road position because of it.

🚌

A580 East Lancs Road

The A580 is one of the north-west's busiest A-roads and forms part of the test route from Eccles DTC. Multiple slip roads, bus routes, and high-speed junctions appear on the route. Understanding the A580's speed limit changes, slip road discipline, and heavy goods vehicle presence is essential for any Worsley student — it is the road that most distinguishes Eccles DTC from quieter Greater Manchester test centres.

🛒

Walkden Town Centre

Walkden has a Tesco Extra and a town-centre precinct that generate heavy AM peak traffic. Roads around the precinct — including the A576 Walkden Road — back up significantly between 8am and 9am. Test routes through Walkden at AM peak expose candidates to queuing traffic, pedestrians crossing outside the designated crossing, and delivery vehicles blocking lanes near the supermarket. Knowing Walkden's peak hours helps instructors schedule route practice at the right times.

🏫

Worsley & Eccles Schools

Both Worsley Road (B5211) and Barton Road (A572) have 20mph school zones that operate during school hours. Camera enforcement applies. Morning test slots between 8:30am and 9:15am and afternoon slots between 3:00pm and 3:45pm will encounter school zone speed restrictions. Candidates must identify the 20mph zone signs and comply — driving at 30mph through a 20mph school zone is an immediate serious fault.

⛏️

Bridgewater Canal Towpath

The Bridgewater Canal towpath runs through Worsley and is used by cyclists and pedestrians, who emerge onto the road at canal bridge crossings. The crossings have no signals — pedestrians simply step onto the road from the towpath access point. On the B5211 Worsley Road and near the heritage area, these access points are unmarked. DriveSQ students are shown each towpath crossing on the test route so they anticipate pedestrian emergence without needing to see a crossing sign.
