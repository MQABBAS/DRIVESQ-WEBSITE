Planning a client-centred lesson for your ADI Part 3

Rules and fees checked on GOV.UK on 25 November 2026 · Updated 25 November 2026

The ADI Part 3 rewards a lesson built around the pupil, not a lesson you prepared in advance. This guide shows how to turn a three-minute opening conversation into a plan, and how to keep adjusting it. It is teaching advice from DriveSQ, not an official DVSA script.

What "client-centred" really means

In a client-centred lesson, the pupil decides what the lesson is for, and you shape the lesson around it. The pupil takes responsibility for their own learning, and you guide, question and support. The test is looking for evidence of this in the way you plan and teach. It is the opposite of arriving with a fixed route and set of topics and delivering them whatever the pupil says.

The three-minute opening, step by step

GOV.UK allows no more than three minutes for the goals and risk discussion, so a clear shape helps:

  1. Find out where they are (about 1 minute). "What have you been working on recently?" "What feels most comfortable? What doesn't?"
  2. Agree the goal (about 1 minute). "What would you like to get out of today?" Then shape the plan: "So we'll look at X, and use Y to practise it. Does that sound right?"
  3. Agree who is responsible for what (about 1 minute). Explain simply that the pupil is driving and you are teaching, that you have the dual controls, and how you will tell them if something needs to stop. Check they understand, in their own words.

Keep the language natural. If it sounds like a script, you have rehearsed too much.

Three pupils, three plans

The pupil you bring can be a partly trained learner, a fully trained learner or a full licence holder. The same three-minute opening produces very different lessons:

PupilA likely goalHow you might teach it
Partly trained learnerSmoother junctions, or moving off and stopping on a hillShort, focused practice runs; clear demonstration; plenty of questions so they explain the sequence back to you
Fully trained learnerTest-standard independent drivingLet them navigate; ask about their decisions after, not during; give them a task and step back
Full licence holderConfidence in a specific situation, such as a dual carriageway or parkingAsk what worries them; set a small challenge; ask them to rate their own performance, then compare

Questions that help the pupil think

Closed questions ("Was that okay?") get a yes. Open questions get thought. Compare:

  • Instead of "Was that a good observation?", ask "What did you notice before you moved out?"
  • Instead of "Do you understand?", ask "How would you explain what you just did?"
  • Instead of telling them the fault, ask "What would you do differently next time?"

Adjusting the plan mid-lesson

A plan is a starting point. If the pupil finds the first task too easy, step it up. If they struggle, drop back, break the skill into parts and try again. Examiners look for evidence that you noticed and responded. Carrying on with an unsuitable plan is a bigger problem than changing it.

Giving feedback they can use

  • Keep it specific: "You checked your mirror and signalled in good time, then moved a little late" is better than "good".
  • Ask for their view first.
  • Give one thing to keep and one thing to change.
  • Link it back to the goal you agreed.

The closing three minutes

GOV.UK allows no more than three minutes for the pupil to reflect at the end. A strong close asks the pupil what went well and what they would work on next, checks it against the goal you agreed, and lets them leave knowing what happens next. Common mistakes we see

  • Spending the opening talking instead of asking.
  • A risk talk that is a list of rules rather than a conversation the pupil understands.
  • Teaching a manoeuvre for the whole lesson. GOV.UK says you cannot spend the whole lesson on one.
  • Giving answers too quickly, so the pupil never has to think.
  • Not stopping to give feedback when stopping safely would help.

Questions people ask

What is client-centred learning in the ADI Part 3?

It means the pupil shapes the goals of the lesson and takes responsibility for their own learning, while you guide, question and support. Your plan is built from what the pupil says, not brought in fixed.

How long can the opening discussion be?

GOV.UK allows no more than 3 minutes for the discussion of goals and risk management at the start.

Can I spend the whole lesson teaching a manoeuvre?

No. GOV.UK says you cannot spend the whole lesson teaching a manoeuvre, although a short recap of one the pupil has met before is allowed.

What kind of pupil should I bring?

A partly trained learner, a fully trained learner or a full licence holder. A partly trained learner should be able to drive for 40 minutes without frequently stopping.

How long is the reflection at the end?

No more than 3 minutes for the pupil to reflect on their performance.
