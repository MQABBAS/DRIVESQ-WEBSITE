Test Centre

Stockport Driving Test Centre

The DTC Stockport students use — and the things no driving school bothers to tell you about it.

Address: Dialstone Lane, Stockport SK2 6AG — off the A6 Wellington Road South near Heaviley
Test Duration: Approximately 38–40 minutes driving + pre-test briefing
Parking: The DTC has a car park where bay parking manoeuvres are conducted — do not park in a test bay. Street parking on Dialstone Lane itself. Arrive 10–15 minutes early.
Documents: Provisional licence, theory pass certificate (within 2 years), glasses if prescribed for driving
Test Fee: £62 weekday / £75 weekend (DVSA 2025 rates)
What nobody tells you: Dialstone Lane is a residential road off the A6 Wellington Road South. The DTC car park is on a slight slope — reverse bay candidates who don't know about the gradient often have control issues that surprise them. The examiner starts recording from the moment you select gear.
A6 traffic timing: Morning slots (8:45am start) have heavier A6 traffic with bus lane enforcement cameras operating. Afternoon slots are calmer on the A6 itself but Bramhall Lane gets busier after 3pm school run. Choose your slot time with this in mind.
Typical first move: From Dialstone Lane, the left turn approach from the DTC onto the A6 crosses a busy bus lane. Examiners specifically watch signalling and mirror checks on this exit — it comes within the first 60 seconds of the test.
Pass rate: Stockport DTC is around the national average. Strong local road knowledge — especially the A6 Heaviley roundabout and Hazel Grove junction — is the key differentiator between prepared and unprepared candidates.
Real Test Routes

The 7 Stockport Test Routes
That Examiners Actually Use

Mapped from real test experience. Our students practise every one of these before their test day.

1
Lane Discipline

Wellington Road South A6 / Heaviley Roundabout

The A6 south from Stockport town centre appears on nearly every test route. The Heaviley roundabout (4 arms) is where most candidates show poor lane discipline — it is the single most common fault source at Stockport DTC. The approach from Dialstone Lane requires a left turn across a busy bus lane, and examiners specifically watch signalling and mirror checks here. Getting lane choice wrong at the Heaviley roundabout often means choosing the wrong exit or cutting across lanes mid-roundabout. There is no margin for guessing it on test day — it must be practised cold.

📍 Dialstone Lane → A6 Wellington Road South → Heaviley roundabout → onward routes
2
Complex Junction

Bramhall Lane / Buxton Road A6 — Davenport to Hazel Grove

30mph residential road dropping into a village stretch through Davenport toward Hazel Grove. The Hazel Grove junction (A6/A523 split) is a complex T-junction where candidates must identify the correct lane from 200 metres back. Google Maps and sat-nav frequently routes learners incorrectly here — the left lane is for Hazel Grove village and the right for the A6 continuation. Getting this wrong means either entering the wrong lane or stopping in the junction to reconsider — both are faults. This junction comes up on a majority of Stockport test routes heading south.

📍 Bramhall Lane → Davenport → Buxton Road → A6/A523 Hazel Grove junction
3
Residential

Dialstone Lane Residential Loop — Offerton Grid

Right out of the DTC through the residential Offerton grid: Dialstone Lane to Turncroft Lane. This is a 20mph zone throughout with unmarked junctions where priority is not obvious — candidates frequently give way when they have priority or push through when they should wait. Parked cars on both sides create passing challenges mid-route where one vehicle must reverse to a passing point. The road layout looks straightforward on a map but the volume of unmarked priority decisions in a short distance makes it one of the most fault-generating residential routes at Stockport DTC.

📍 Dialstone Lane → Offerton grid → Turncroft Lane → 20mph residential loop
4
One-Way System

Shaw Heath / Edgeley

Through Edgeley toward Stockport County's ground at Edgeley Park. Matchday traffic is zero on test day (tests avoid matchday Saturdays) but the one-way system around the stadium access roads trips candidates unfamiliar with the area — turns that look obvious are contraflow, and routes that look blocked are actually two-way. The railway bridge on Castle Street creates a hump with limited forward visibility; examiners watch whether candidates slow before the brow rather than coasting over it. First-time visitors to this area reliably make errors — locally practised candidates do not.

📍 Shaw Heath → Edgeley → Castle Street railway bridge → Edgeley Park area
5
Speed Cameras

Cheadle Heath / Adswood

South from Dialstone Lane toward Cheadle via A5145 Edgeley Road through Shaw Heath. Give-way crossings at the Cheadle Heath junctions require full stops and clear observation before emerging — rushed observations here are a consistent source of serious faults. The route passes through 20mph school zones on the A5145 that are active during school hours with camera enforcement. Morning test slots (8:45am start) coincide with school-run time — the 20mph zone is live, enforced, and camera-monitored. DriveSQ students know exactly which zones apply and when.

📍 Dialstone Lane → Edgeley Road A5145 → Shaw Heath → Cheadle Heath 20mph zone
6
Town Centre

Mersey Square / Stockport Town Centre Approach

Occasionally used on routes going north toward Stockport town centre. The one-way system in Mersey Square is complex — multiple lanes, bus bays, tram infrastructure works, and pedestrian crossings all converge in a small area. The A6 underpass (Lancashire Hill / Merseyway) catches candidates unfamiliar with the town-centre road layout — the underpass sign appears late and candidates who don't know it's coming brake abruptly. This route is less frequent than the southern routes but appears often enough that not knowing it is a real risk. Our students drive it at least twice before test day.

📍 Wellington Road North → Mersey Square one-way → A6 Merseyway underpass approach
7
Manoeuvre Zone

Dialstone Lane Bay Parking

Reverse bay park in the DTC car park is the most commonly tested manoeuvre at Stockport DTC. The car park is on a slight slope — the car will creep if not held by the clutch (manual) or brake pressure (automatic). Examiners are watching for controlled entry with clear observation in all directions, not just a completed manoeuvre. Candidates who practise on flat car parks are surprised by the gradient. Reference points for the bays at Dialstone Lane differ from any other car park. Every DriveSQ Stockport student practises specifically at the DTC car park before test day.

📍 DTC car park, Dialstone Lane SK2 · Slight slope gradient — practise controlled entry

Local Knowledge

8 Stockport Hazards Other
Schools Never Cover

The specific, local details that separate a DriveSQ Stockport student from someone who learned without local knowledge.

A6 Heaviley Roundabout — Wrong Lane Approach from Dialstone Lane

The Heaviley roundabout is the most fault-generating junction on any Stockport DTC test route. The approach from Dialstone Lane — the DTC's own road — requires candidates to cross a live bus lane on the left turn onto the A6. Examiners score mirror checks and signalling on this very first manoeuvre. On the roundabout itself, candidates who haven't practised from this specific approach frequently choose the wrong entry lane, cut across lanes mid-roundabout, or stall waiting for a gap they don't need. DriveSQ drills this junction from the Dialstone Lane approach until the lane choice is automatic.

A6/A523 Hazel Grove Junction — Complex T-Junction, Lane Choice from 200m

The junction where the A6 Buxton Road meets the A523 Macclesfield Road in Hazel Grove is the second most common serious-fault source at Stockport DTC. The correct lane must be identified from approximately 200 metres before the junction — too late and lane-changing in the approach is a fault. Sat-nav and Google Maps directions are frequently wrong here, routing candidates into the wrong lane. DriveSQ's Stockport instructors teach the approach from the correct line, with the correct lane cues, so the decision is made well in advance — not under last-second pressure.

Castle Street Railway Bridge — Blind Hump, Limited Forward Visibility

The railway bridge on Castle Street in Edgeley creates a pronounced hump in the road. At the top of the hump, forward visibility drops to nearly nothing — oncoming vehicles appear suddenly as you crest it. Candidates who don't know this bridge is coming either approach too fast (risk of meeting oncoming traffic in the road) or brake abruptly on the brow itself (fault). The correct approach is a controlled, anticipated slow before the brow. Every DriveSQ Stockport student is briefed on this bridge and practises it multiple times before test day.

Mersey Square One-Way System — Complex Town Centre Roads, Tram Tracks

Mersey Square and the surrounding Stockport town centre roads form a complex one-way network that is intimidating for candidates who have only practised residential routes. Current Metrolink infrastructure works have added temporary road closures and route changes. Tram tracks are embedded in road surfaces in sections — tyre groove-catch is a real hazard at low speed. Candidates who haven't specifically been taken through Mersey Square in a lesson make navigational errors, lane errors, or are distracted by the complexity of the environment when they should be focusing on observations.

Dialstone Lane Unmarked Priority Junctions in Offerton Grid

The Offerton residential grid contains multiple unmarked junctions where road priority is determined by the rule that the broader road gives way to the narrower — but in practice, the roads are similar width and the rule is ambiguous. Candidates who confidently proceed when they should wait, or who wait when they have priority, generate hesitancy or dangerous faults. These junctions are also complicated by parked cars reducing the visible turning radius. Knowing each specific junction in the Offerton grid — which ones to wait at and which to proceed through — is something that only locally experienced instructors can teach.

A5145 School Zone Cameras (Cheadle Heath) — 20mph Enforcement

Edgeley Road (A5145) through Cheadle Heath has fixed camera enforcement of 20mph school zones during school hours. Morning test slots at 8:45am fall squarely within the school-run window — the 20mph zone is live, and the cameras are active. Driving at 30mph (the default expectation on A-roads) in an enforced 20mph zone is a serious fault. DriveSQ's Stockport instructors specifically highlight every school-zone camera on the A5145 and its operational hours during early lessons, so the knowledge is automatic by test day — not a panicked read of signs on the day.

Bramhall Lane Parked Cars — Passing Priority on Narrow Residential Sections

Bramhall Lane between Davenport and Hazel Grove has sections where parked cars on both sides reduce the effective road width to single-file. Priority for passing is governed by which side the obstruction sits on — if parked cars are on your side of the road, oncoming traffic has priority and you should wait. Candidates who don't know this rule assert priority they don't have, or give way when they should proceed, generating faults in both directions. DriveSQ's Stockport lessons cover the specific sections of Bramhall Lane where this applies and when to act.

DTC Car Park Slope — Reverse Bay on Slight Gradient

The Stockport DTC car park on Dialstone Lane has a slight slope. Candidates doing a reverse bay park on this slope must manage the car's natural roll while simultaneously steering and observing. Manual candidates who release the clutch mid-manoeuvre without applying the handbrake will find the car rolling — a potential serious fault if it moves toward another vehicle or the bay lines. Automatic candidates still need to manage brake pressure rather than drifting. DriveSQ's Stockport instructors take every student to practise in this specific car park before test day. Reference points here are different from any other car park in the area.

Candidates should not confuse them with active tram signals, but should be aware of track grooves affecting lane position.

Home matches (usually Saturdays) increase pedestrian and parking pressure throughout SK3. Examiners avoid matchday Saturdays — tests near Edgeley Park are quieter mid-week. Dialstone Lane Reservoir

A large reservoir on Dialstone Lane creates a quieter section after the DTC. Examiners sometimes use this area to turn around before heading back via Offerton. DriveSQ Stockport students drive this section specifically during lessons.

Bramhall / Hazel Grove Commuter Belt

SK7 is one of the most densely car-owning postcodes in Greater Manchester. Driving lessons here involve high-standard expectations on road quality. Candidates who haven't driven Bramhall Lane at peak time are often surprised by the pace and volume of traffic on their test.

Cheadle Hulme / Gatley Schools

Multiple secondary schools on and near the A5145 Edgeley Road. Term-time 20mph zones are strictly enforced with camera systems. School run times (8–9am, 3–4pm) significantly increase traffic on Edgeley Road. Where is Stockport Driving Test Centre?
Dialstone Lane, Stockport SK2 6AG, off the A6 Wellington Road South near Heaviley. Parking available on Dialstone Lane. Morning slots have heavier A6 traffic; afternoon slots (1pm–4pm) are generally calmer.
What are the main test routes from Stockport DTC?
