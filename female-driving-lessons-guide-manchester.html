Learning to drive with a female instructor in Manchester: the complete guide

Updated 20 November 2026
Everything in one place.

From your provisional licence to your practical test: costs, how many lessons learners typically need, how to check any driving instructor, and what the DVSA figures show for women.

Why many women ask for a female instructor

Many women simply feel more at ease learning with another woman, especially in the first few lessons when everything is new. It is a personal preference and there is no need to explain it. Some learners are returning to driving after a long break or after a knock to their confidence, some prefer it for cultural or religious reasons, and some just find it easier to ask questions and say when they are unsure.

There are lots of reasons to ask for a female instructor: feeling more relaxed, being more comfortable having a one-to-one lesson in a small car, cultural or family preferences, or simply wanting to learn in a calm environment. You do not need to justify it. When you book we note your preference and aim to match you with a female instructor near your pick-up postcode.

How many lessons will you need?

The DVSA says the average learner needs around 45 hours of lessons with an instructor, plus about 22 hours of practice with family or friends, before they pass. Everyone is different, so some pass with fewer and some need more. At £35 an hour, 45 hours is £1,575, and you pay as you go rather than all at once.

See the DVSA's own guidance on learning to drive on GOV.UK.

From provisional licence to test: the steps

  1. Get your provisional licence. You need it before your first lesson. Apply on GOV.UK. It must be valid, and you must meet the eyesight standard (able to read a number plate from 20 metres).
  2. Start lessons. We match you with an instructor and agree a plan for your first lessons.
  3. Pass your theory test. You need to pass it before you can book a practical test. It costs £23 (check GOV.UK for the current fee). Book your theory test. Our free Student Portal has 700+ practice questions and 14 mock tests.
  4. Book your practical test. It costs £62 on weekdays and £75 on evenings, weekends and bank holidays (check GOV.UK for current fees). Book your driving test.
  5. Pass, and go on. Many new drivers add Pass Plus or motorway lessons for extra confidence.

Choosing and checking your instructor safely

Whoever you learn with, check them. A qualified approved driving instructor (ADI) must display a green badge on the windscreen while teaching. A trainee instructor displays a pink badge. You can read the DVSA's advice on finding a driving instructor on GOV.UK. At DriveSQ you will know your instructor's name, phone number and car before the first lesson. You can ask to be picked up from a public place, and you are welcome to tell a friend or family member the time and place of your lesson.

Frequently asked questions

Can I choose a female driving instructor?

Yes. You can request one on the booking form, and we match you near your pick-up postcode, subject to availability.

How do I know an instructor is qualified?

A fully qualified instructor displays a green ADI badge, and a trainee displays a pink one. You can read the DVSA's advice on GOV.UK.

How much does it cost to learn to drive?

It depends on how many lessons you need. The DVSA average is about 45 hours of lessons plus 22 hours of practice. At £35 an hour, 45 hours is £1,575, plus test fees.
