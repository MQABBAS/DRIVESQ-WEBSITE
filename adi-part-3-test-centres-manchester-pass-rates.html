ADI Part 3 pass rates at every Manchester test centre

Updated 25 November 2026 · Source: DVSA ADI part 3 test statistics (DRT522B, published 25 June 2026, data to March 2026)

Eight DVSA centres in the Manchester area publish ADI Part 3 results. In 2025/26 their pass rates ran from 14.9% at Cheetham Hill to 30.9% at Rochdale, against a Great Britain figure of 27.8%. Here is every centre, with the trend, and what the numbers can and cannot tell you.

Short version: only 2 of the 8 centres were above the national pass rate in 2025/26 (Rochdale, Bolton). 7 of the 8 centres have a lower pass rate than in 2018/19, when the eight were between 26% and 46%.

"vs national" is each centre's pass rate minus the Great Britain rate of 27.8% for the same year, in percentage points. DVSA publishes the number of tests only where it is large enough to be safe to show, so for most centres the count is withheld and only the pass rate is available.

What stands out

  • Rochdale is the highest at 30.9%, 3.1 percentage points above the national figure. Bolton follows at 28.1%.
  • Cheetham Hill is the lowest at 14.9%, 12.9 percentage points below national. Its rate has been in the range of 11.9% to 37.6% across the years shown on this site.
  • 2 centres publish their 2025/26 test count: Bolton (139 tests, 39 passes) and West Didsbury (231 tests, 60 passes). Together that is 370 tests and 99 passes, a combined pass rate of 26.8%.
  • Several centres are volatile. Bury, for example, went from 21.4% in 2023/24 to 44.4% in 2024/25 and 20.0% in 2025/26. With so few tests, swings like that are common and should not be read as a change in how tests are run.

Why not just pick the centre with the best number?

You may not get a choice of centre, and even if you do, a pass rate is a poor guide to your own chances. Three reasons:

  1. Small samples. Most centres run a few dozen to a few hundred Part 3 tests a year. A single result moves the rate by several points.
  2. The rate describes candidates as well as the test. Who takes their test at a centre, and how well prepared they are, differs from place to place.
  3. Your own preparation matters far more. A different centre means different roads, but it does not change what is being marked. See how the Part 3 is marked.

What you can do with this information

  • Use it to set expectations. Across Great Britain about 27.8% of Part 3 attempts pass, so a fail is common and not a sign that you cannot teach.
  • Prepare with a pupil who is comfortable on the roads around your booked centre, if you know which one it will be.
  • Plan for a retake in your timeline and budget, because each attempt costs £111. See the retake and third-attempt rules.

Questions people ask

Which Manchester test centre has the highest ADI Part 3 pass rate?

In 2025/26 DVSA published a pass rate of 30.9% for Rochdale, the highest of the eight Manchester centres, against 27.8% for Great Britain. Small numbers mean the ranking can change from year to year.

Which Manchester centre has the lowest ADI Part 3 pass rate?

Cheetham Hill at 14.9% in 2025/26. Check the year-by-year figures before drawing conclusions, because a few tests can move a centre's rate by several points.

What is the ADI Part 3 pass rate in Great Britain?

27.8% for 2025/26 across 13,609 tests, according to DVSA table DRT522B. It was 37.1% in 2018/19.

Can I choose which test centre I take my ADI Part 3 at?

GOV.UK explains how to book the Part 3 on its test page. We could not confirm from official pages how a centre is chosen, so check the booking service when you book.

Does a lower pass rate mean the test is harder at that centre?

No. The rate reflects who took their test there and how prepared they were, as well as the test. With small numbers, a few results can change a centre's rate by several points.

Source and method. All figures are from the DVSA tables DRT522B ("ADI part 3 test pass rate by gender: test centres", updated 25 June 2026, data to March 2026) and DRT521C ("ADI part 3 tests conducted, passed and pass rates", Great Britain, updated 3 September 2026), published on GOV.UK. Years are financial years (April to March); 2020/21 is left out because the pandemic disrupted testing. DVSA notes that ADI Part 3 data is recorded by hand, so accuracy can be affected. It also withholds counts where they are small enough to identify individuals (shown here as 'withheld'), and a centre appears only if more than one examiner carries out these tests. Small numbers move pass rates a lot: one extra pass or fail can shift a centre's rate by several points. Treat one year at one centre with care. DVSA also notes that before December 2022 not all ADI Part 3 tests were recorded, so older pass rates carry extra uncertainty. Differences, rankings and comparisons on this page are calculated by DriveSQ from the DVSA figures and are not DVSA headline figures. A centre's pass rate says nothing about how any individual test was marked.
