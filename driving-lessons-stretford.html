Test Centre

Manchester (Longford Park) Driving Test Centre

The DTC Stretford students use — and the things no driving school bothers to tell you about it.

Address: Longford Park, Stretford M32 8PR — situated within Longford Park, accessed via Edge Lane
Test Duration: Approximately 38–40 minutes driving + pre-test briefing
Getting There by Bus: 263 and X57 services stop on Chester Road, approximately 3 minutes' walk from the DTC entrance
Documents: Provisional licence, theory pass certificate (within 2 years), glasses if prescribed for driving
Test Fee: £62 weekday / £75 weekend (DVSA 2025 rates)
The exit every candidate must know: The DTC exit onto Edge Lane is left-turn only. Examiners specifically watch for candidates who cut the corner or swing wide into the oncoming lane. This is one of the first manoeuvres of the test — getting it wrong immediately puts you on the back foot. DriveSQ students practise this exit in every lesson that ends at the DTC.
Longford Park Setting: The DTC sits within Longford Park, a public park with pedestrian and cyclist activity near the entrance. Pedestrians cross the approach road without warning. The examiner's assessment begins from the moment you move off — treat the park approach as live test territory.
Bay Parking Manoeuvre: Bay parking in the Longford Park DTC car park is frequently tested. The bays are set on a slight gradient — vehicle creep during a reverse bay park is a common fault. Manual candidates must manage the clutch; automatic candidates must maintain firm brake pressure throughout.
First routes from the DTC: Most test routes head immediately onto Edge Lane and Chester Road A56 — the Old Trafford Metrolink tram crossing appears within the first few minutes of the test. Do not treat the departure from the DTC as a warm-up; it is the test.
Real Test Routes

The 7 Stretford Test Routes
That Examiners Actually Use

Mapped from real test experience. Our students practise every one of these before their test day.

1
Tram Crossing

A56 Chester Road Northbound

Stretford's main arterial road runs north toward Old Trafford and Manchester city centre. This route features multiple pelican crossings, bus lanes with operational hours that change throughout the day, and most critically — a tram crossing at the Old Trafford Metrolink stop. The tram signal must be obeyed: candidates who fail to observe the tram signal stop mid-road as the tram approaches, which is an immediate serious fault. Bus lanes on Chester Road also catch candidates who enter without checking operational hours. DriveSQ students practise this corridor in full, including tram signal procedure, from their first few lessons.

📍 A56 Chester Road: Longford Park → Old Trafford Metrolink stop → Manchester border
2
Shopping Zone

B5213 Edge Lane / Stretford Mall

A 30mph route passing Stretford Mall and its surrounding retail area. Pedestrian crossings are active near the shopping centre throughout the day. The major hazard is loading vehicles — delivery lorries pull out from loading bays outside restricted hours without warning, moving from a stationary position across the nearside lane. Candidates who haven't specifically driven this stretch during delivery periods are caught entirely off-guard. Observing loading bay areas ahead and reducing speed when lorries are visible is a skill DriveSQ builds deliberately on this route.

📍 B5213 Edge Lane → Stretford Mall → Chester Road A56 junction
3
One-Way System

A5181 King Street Stretford

The one-way system through Stretford town centre is one of the most route-reading-intensive sections on any Longford Park DTC test. Navigating a one-way system requires continuous reading of direction signs while simultaneously managing traffic, speed, and observation. The give-way lines at the Chester Road junction catch candidates off-guard — the junction geometry is unusual, with priority not immediately obvious from the road layout alone. Candidates who haven't specifically studied the King Street system before their test frequently make direction errors, each of which uses up examiner observation time and increases the chance of a further fault.

📍 A5181 King Street → Stretford town centre one-way loop → Chester Road A56 give-way
4
Dual Carriageway

M17 / Trafford Centre Link Road

A dual carriageway section near Trafford Quays with a 50mph speed limit — a significant step up from the 30mph residential routes that occupy most of the test. Lane discipline is formally assessed here: candidates must maintain lane position, check mirrors before any lane change, and avoid undertaking other vehicles. The route attracts heavy HGV traffic from Trafford Park industrial estate throughout the day. Candidates who are not comfortable at 50mph — either holding the speed or making lane changes at it — generate driving fault after fault. DriveSQ specifically includes dual carriageway sessions as part of every Stretford lesson programme.

📍 M17 / Trafford Quays link road: Trafford Wharf Road → Wharfside Way → dual carriageway section
5
Camera Zone

A5145 Barton Road / Davyhulme

A 30mph residential route through Davyhulme with several schools and pedestrian crossings. During school hours the speed limit drops to 20mph — this is camera-enforced. Signs indicate the 20mph school zone but candidates unfamiliar with the route miss the point at which the limit changes and continue at 30mph past the cameras. This is an immediate fail. DriveSQ students know exactly where each school zone starts and ends on Barton Road before they ever sit a test, because their instructor has flagged every camera point during lessons.

📍 A5145 Barton Road: Stretford → Davyhulme → school zones (20mph camera-enforced during school hours)
6
A-Road

A57 Salford Road / Urmston

A long straight 40mph route through Urmston with a significant hidden hazard: a blind crest before the Flixton Road junction. The road appears clear ahead until the crest, at which point the Flixton Road junction appears with reduced stopping distance. Candidates who are travelling at the correct 40mph but not maintaining adequate following distance are caught by the sudden appearance of the junction. The rule is simple: if you can't see the road ahead, treat the crest as a potential hazard and maintain distance. Our instructors walk through every road feature on this route verbally before students drive it.

📍 A57 Salford Road: Stretford → Urmston → blind crest before Flixton Road junction
7
Manoeuvre Zone

Longford Park Bay Park

Reversing into angled bays in the Longford Park DTC car park — the most frequently tested manoeuvre at this centre. The bays are set on a slight gradient, meaning the car will creep on the slope during the manoeuvre if not held correctly. Manual candidates must use clutch control or apply the handbrake to prevent roll; automatic candidates must maintain consistent brake pressure. The examiner observes from the entrance gate — meaning the entire manoeuvre is observed from the moment you select reverse. Reference points in a sloped car park differ from a flat town-centre multi-storey. Our students practise this specific car park, not a generic bay park elsewhere.

📍 Longford Park DTC car park · angled bays on gradient · examiner observes from entrance gate

Local Knowledge

8 Stretford Hazards Other
Schools Never Cover

The specific, local details that separate a DriveSQ Stretford student from someone who learned without local knowledge.

Old Trafford Metrolink Tram Crossing — A56

The Metrolink tram crosses the A56 Chester Road at the Old Trafford stop. Unlike a standard traffic light, the tram signal has a specific sequence that must be obeyed — including the obligation to stop if the tram is approaching even if the road ahead looks clear. Candidates who miss the tram signal stop mid-road as the tram enters the crossing — this is an immediate serious fault. DriveSQ students are taught the full tram signal procedure from lesson one, because it appears on nearly every test route from Longford Park DTC.

Stretford Mall Loading Bays — B5213

The B5213 Edge Lane alongside Stretford Mall has designated loading bays used by delivery lorries. These vehicles are legally permitted to load and unload outside standard restricted hours, meaning they can be parked in or pulling out of bays at almost any time during the test day. A lorry pulling out from a loading bay can occupy the entire nearside lane with little warning. Candidates who aren't scanning ahead for stationary commercial vehicles near loading zones are regularly caught. Our students know every loading bay location on this stretch and are taught to anticipate, not react.

King Street One-Way System — Chester Road Give-Way

The one-way system through Stretford town centre is route-reading-intensive. The give-way at the Chester Road junction is the most dangerous point: the junction geometry doesn't make the priority immediately obvious, and candidates following the one-way route too quickly miss the give-way lines entirely. Once past the give-way without stopping, candidates are either in a dangerous position or have to reverse — both are serious faults. Every DriveSQ Stretford student maps the King Street system in detail, including the Chester Road give-way, before they drive it solo.

Trafford Centre Link Road — 50mph HGV Traffic

The dual carriageway section near Trafford Quays has a 50mph speed limit and carries significant HGV volumes from Trafford Park industrial estate, one of the largest industrial areas in the UK. Many learners from residential postcodes have never driven near heavily loaded lorries at motorway-approach speeds. The correct technique is to maintain lane position, keep mirrors checked, and never undertake. A lane change with a lorry close behind at 50mph in poor lane awareness is a serious fault. DriveSQ builds dual carriageway confidence specifically, not as an afterthought at the end of a lesson plan.

Barton Road 20mph School Zone — Camera Enforced

Barton Road through Davyhulme passes several schools. During school hours the limit drops from 30mph to 20mph — this is not advisory, it is camera-enforced. The cameras are active during school start and end times. Candidates who haven't specifically been told where the 20mph zone begins continue at 30mph through camera-monitored sections. There is no immediate consequence during the test — but the examiner observes the candidate driving through a 20mph zone at 30mph and records it as a serious speeding fault. Knowing exactly where the 20mph signs are is the only protection.

Blind Crest A57 Urmston — Before Flixton Road Junction

The A57 Salford Road in Urmston has a blind crest that hides the Flixton Road junction from approaching drivers. At 40mph with a normal following distance, a driver who crests the hill has less than 2 seconds to react if a vehicle is stopped at the junction ahead. The rule is to treat any blind crest as a potential hazard — maintain following distance and be prepared to stop. Candidates who don't know the crest exists are invariably caught by the junction appearing suddenly. DriveSQ instructors flag this specific crest every time they drive the A57 route with a student.

Longford Park Gradient — Bay Park Vehicle Creep

The DTC car park at Longford Park is on a slight gradient. During a reverse bay park, a manual car in neutral will creep forward on the slope — undoing the manoeuvre. Manual candidates must hold the clutch biting point or apply the handbrake at each observation check. Automatic candidates must apply consistent brake pressure throughout. The examiner observes from the entrance gate — there is no moment of the manoeuvre that isn't watched. Vehicle creep mid-manoeuvre is a driving fault that adds up. Our students practise this specific gradient, not a flat car park that doesn't reflect the real test conditions.

Match-Day Old Trafford — M16 Road Gridlock

Manchester United play at Old Trafford in M16, and Lancashire County Cricket Club matches are held at Emirates Old Trafford on Talbot Road — within the M16 test route area. Up to 2 hours before and after major fixtures, the roads around Old Trafford (Chester Road A56, Seymour Grove, Ayres Road, Talbot Road) experience gridlock-level congestion beyond normal peak hours. A test booked on a match day morning or afternoon will encounter this traffic. DriveSQ instructors advise students on upcoming fixture dates and, where possible, help them choose test slots that avoid the worst match-day congestion patterns.

Every test route from Longford Park DTC that uses Chester Road A56 will encounter this crossing. Knowing the tram signal sequence (and what to do when it activates mid-approach) is essential knowledge, not optional preparation.
