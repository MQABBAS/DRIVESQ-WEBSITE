Unique Industrial Hazard — Pottery Lane / Ashton New Road Junction

This is an industrial-area hazard unique to east Manchester and not covered by any competitor’s lesson programme. At the junction of Pottery Lane and Ashton New Road A635, heavy goods vehicles making wide left turns from Pottery Lane routinely cut across both lanes of Ashton New Road. Because the turning vehicles require extra swept path to complete the manoeuvre, learners approaching from the right are frequently positioned directly within the vehicle’s turning arc — even when they believe they are travelling in a clear lane.

The compounding factor is limited cab visibility: HGV drivers initiating the turn from Pottery Lane have a restricted forward sightline across the nose of the vehicle and cannot always see a car positioned to the right of the swept path. DriveSQ instructors teach learners to read the early warning signs — indicator timing, the position of the cab moving toward the centre line, and mirror movement in the lorry cab — and to hold back well before the vehicle commits, rather than proceeding on the assumption that they have priority. Understanding swept-path geometry at industrial junctions is a genuine safety skill that will serve you for life.

Knowing which junctions require extra observation, where speed limits change, and where vulnerable road users are most likely to appear turns a competent learner into a genuinely safe driver for life. Key Driving Knowledge for Greater Manchester

  • The DVSA’s recommended following distance is a 2-second gap in dry conditions — double it in rain and triple it in snow or ice.
  • Speed limits are maximums, not targets — always drive at the speed appropriate for the road conditions, visibility and traffic density.
  • Highway Code Rule 170 requires you to give way to pedestrians when turning into a side road if they are already crossing.
  • Roundabout lane selection must be decided before the give-way line — changing lanes inside a roundabout is one of the most common causes of serious test faults.

Test centre facts: Greater Manchester has six DVSA practical test centres: Cheetham Hill (Manchester), West Didsbury, Sale, Stockport (Bredbury), Ashton-under-Lyne and Bolton. Openshaw learners most commonly use Hyde Road area or Manchester Plymouth Grove. Each centre has distinct route characteristics — choosing the right test centre for your training area is a strategic decision that can meaningfully affect your pass probability. Greater Manchester Road Network — Educational Guide

Greater Manchester’s road network covers over 4,500 miles of road managed across ten metropolitan boroughs. The network includes motorways (M60, M62, M56, M61, M67), A-roads of regional importance (A6, A34, A56, A57, A580, A635), and an extensive local road grid of B-roads and unclassified roads. Understanding the network hierarchy — and the different driving demands at each level — is a key part of driver education. The A635 Ashton New Road, which passes through Openshaw, is a key arterial route connecting east Manchester to the city centre and carries mixed traffic including significant HGV volumes from industrial premises along Pottery Lane and adjoining streets.

Manchester’s city centre road network is managed by TfGM (Transport for Greater Manchester) using a SCOOT adaptive traffic signal system that responds to real-time vehicle detector data. This means signal phases can be shorter or longer than expected — learners should always approach signals prepared to stop rather than accelerating in anticipation of a green phase.

Heavy Vehicle Awareness for Urban Drivers

One of the most important skills for any driver in east Manchester is understanding the behaviour of large goods vehicles at junctions. An articulated HGV requires a swept path significantly wider than its own width when turning — the rear wheels track inside the path of the front wheels (known as off-tracking), meaning a lorry turning left may initially move right before beginning the turn to give the trailer wheels clearance. Drivers who do not understand off-tracking sometimes move into the gap created by the lorry moving right, positioning themselves directly in the path of the rear wheels and trailer. DriveSQ Openshaw lessons specifically address this phenomenon at the Pottery Lane / Ashton New Road junction, teaching learners to hold back and observe until the full swept path is established and clear before proceeding.

DVSA Practical Test — What You Need to Know

Automatic and manual driving tests use identical assessment criteria and marking standards. The only operational difference is that automatic test candidates are not assessed on clutch control, gear selection, or biting point management. This removes approximately 15% of the driver fault categories from the assessment — which partly explains why automatic test pass rates are approximately 5 percentage points higher than manual test pass rates nationally. For learners in Openshaw who are dealing with the additional cognitive load of industrial junction hazard awareness, automatic transmission often accelerates overall learning by reducing the number of simultaneous tasks during early lessons.

After You Pass — What New Drivers Need to Know

After passing your driving test, your journey as a driver is just beginning. The first year of independent driving carries the highest collision risk of any stage of a driver’s career — new drivers are involved in road incidents at four times the rate of experienced drivers. Post-pass programmes including Pass Plus, motorway lessons with a qualified ADI, and telematics (black box) insurance all demonstrably reduce first-year risk. DriveSQ offers post-pass sessions from Openshaw specifically designed to build the additional skills that DVSA testing cannot fully assess: motorway driving via the M60/M67 at Fairfield, night driving on Ashton New Road, and adverse weather confidence on east Manchester’s road network.
