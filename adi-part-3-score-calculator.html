How this calculator follows the official rules

  • 17 areas, each 0 to 3. The most you can score is 51.
  • Fail: 0 to 30. Grade B: 31 to 42. Grade A: 43 to 51.
  • Automatic fail: 7 or less in risk management (five areas, so out of 15), or the lesson being stopped because of immediate danger.

The wording of each area is a short paraphrase of the questions on the DVSA's report form. For the exact wording, use the official form. Read how the Part 3 is marked and every box on the report form for the full picture.

Our own rule of thumb (not DVSA advice): do not book until a few observed lessons in a row score comfortably above the pass mark, for example 36 or more with risk management at 10 or more, so a single bad moment does not decide your result.

Questions people ask

How is the ADI Part 3 scored?

The examiner scores 17 areas of competence from 0 to 3, so the maximum is 51. 0 to 30 is a fail, 31 to 42 is Grade B and 43 to 51 is Grade A.

What is the risk management rule?

If you score 7 or less in the risk management category, which has five areas and a maximum of 15, it is an automatic fail whatever your total.

Can I use this to predict my test result?

It shows how a lesson would score on the DVSA's own scale if an observer marks each area honestly. It cannot predict an examiner's marks, so use it to find weak areas and to decide when you are ready.

Who should score my practice lesson?

Someone who knows the marking and will be honest, such as your trainer or an experienced ADI. Scoring yourself is useful but less reliable.

Does this store my scores?

No. The calculator runs in your browser and does not send your scores anywhere.
