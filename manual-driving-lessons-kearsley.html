Road-by-Road Breakdown

The Roads You'll Drive in Kearsley

Manchester Road A666 — 40mph Arterial Positioning

The A666 runs south through Kearsley between Moses Gate and the Ringley Road junction, carrying a continuous 40mph flow with right-turn filter lanes at three key junctions. We use the A666 / Ringley Road junction — a staggered crossroads with a ghost island — to teach correct approach lane position, early signalling, and patience waiting for a safe gap across the oncoming 40mph flow. Northbound the road widens to a short dual-carriageway section approaching Moses Gate, where lane discipline and merging confidence are tested. The bus layby at the Co-op introduces the hazard of stationary buses pulling back into traffic — reading their indicator and giving clearance is a basic but test-critical skill we reinforce here.

Moses Gate Roundabout — Five-Arm Junction Discipline

Moses Gate is shared between Kearsley and Farnworth — a large five-arm roundabout at the junction of the A666, A6053, and Plodder Lane. Approaching from the Kearsley side on the A666, learners must select the correct lane at a point where road markings are worn and the give-way line curves. The sight line to the right is blocked by a grassed central reservation until the final 15m before the junction, making approach speed critical. We run this roundabout in all five directions over multiple lessons until learners navigate it automatically. Bolton DVSA test routes regularly include Moses Gate on the A666 approach — familiarity is a direct competitive advantage on test day.

Ringley Road — River Irwell Bridge & Rural Connector

Ringley Road runs east from the A666 through Stoneclough, crossing the River Irwell via a single-carriageway stone bridge with priority from the Kearsley side. The bridge section has a 7.5-tonne weight restriction and is used by farm vehicles — learners practise identifying large vehicle approaches early and holding their position to wait, rather than committing to the bridge when it cannot be cleared safely. Beyond the bridge the road rises into Ringley village through a 1-in-10 gradient with a give-way at the Church Lane junction at the crest — a hill-start-on-give-way exercise identical in technique to the Holcombe Road work carried out by our Ramsbottom learners, but in a different context.

Stoneclough & Prestolee — Beginner Residential Foundation

The Stoneclough and Prestolee residential grid — Stoneclough Road, Radcliffe Road B6196, Hall Lane, and Hough Lane — is where all DriveSQ Kearsley beginners start. These wide, low-traffic roads are perfect for establishing clutch biting-point, smooth first-gear acceleration, and early mirror discipline. The T-junction at Stoneclough Road / Hall Lane is a gentle introduction to emerging — clear sightlines, low traffic speed, and good road markings allow learners to practise observation without pressure. The Kearsley Recreation Ground car park off Stoneclough Road provides a flat, open space for bay parking and three-point-turn practice in complete safety before moving to live-road manoeuvre work.

Local Road Knowledge and Driving Education

Building local road knowledge is one of the most underestimated aspects of driver training. Knowing which junctions require extra observation, where speed limits change, and where vulnerable road users are most likely to appear turns a competent learner into a genuinely safe driver for life. DriveSQ lessons are planned around the actual roads you will drive every day after passing.

Key Driving Knowledge for Greater Manchester

  • Highway Code Rule 170 requires you to give way to pedestrians when turning into a side road if they are already crossing.
  • Roundabout lane selection must be decided before the give-way line — changing lanes inside a roundabout is one of the most common causes of serious test faults.
  • Mirror checks should happen every 5-8 seconds on open roads and before every speed change, direction change or manoeuvre.
  • The DVSA's recommended following distance is a 2-second gap in dry conditions — double it in rain and triple it in snow or ice.

Road safety facts: Young drivers (17-24) represent 7% of all UK licence holders but are involved in 25% of fatal road collisions. The primary risk factors are inexperience, peer pressure, night driving and driving on high-speed rural roads. Comprehensive training that includes night driving, rural roads and post-test motorway experience directly addresses all four risk factors.
