Altrincham Driving Test Centre

The DTC Sale students use — and the things no driving school bothers to tell you about it.

Address: Moss Lane, Altrincham WA14 2TL — near the Altrincham town centre, opposite a school entrance
Test Duration: Approximately 38–40 minutes driving + pre-test briefing
Parking: Bay parking manoeuvre takes place in the DTC car park on Moss Lane. Do not park in a test bay on arrival. Arrive 15 minutes early and allow time for the Moss Lane school pedestrian zone at peak hours.
Documents: Provisional licence, theory pass certificate (within 2 years), glasses if prescribed for driving
Test Fee: £62 weekday / £75 weekend (DVSA 2025 rates)
What nobody tells you: Moss Lane has a school entrance directly opposite the DTC. At 9am, pedestrian movement — parents, children, school staff — is heavy. The examiner starts recording from the moment you pull out of the car park. Candidates who treat the DTC exit as a warm-up frequently pick up an observation fault before they have turned into the first road.
Metrolink access: Sale Metrolink stop (M33) is approximately 10 minutes drive from Altrincham DTC. Brooklands tram stop is the nearest Metrolink point on the test route heading north.
Typical test route direction: From Altrincham DTC, most routes head north toward Stretford via the A56 or south through Timperley. The A56 dual-carriageway section is almost always included — it appears within the first 10 minutes of test routes heading toward Sale.
Pass rate: Altrincham DTC operates near the national average. Route knowledge — especially the A56 dual carriageway, Brooklands Metrolink crossing, and Urmston level crossing — is the key differentiator.
Real Test Routes

The 7 Sale Test Routes
That Examiners Actually Use

Mapped from real test experience. Our students practise every one of these before their test day.

1
Tram Crossing

A56 Washway Road / Manchester Road

Sale's main arterial road is a 40mph dual carriageway that forms the backbone of most Altrincham DTC test routes from Sale. Bus lanes operate during peak hours — entering an active bus lane without checking the operational hours is a common fault. The Metrolink crossing at Brooklands uses a tram signal system: steady amber means stop, flashing amber means proceed with caution if the stop is clear. The tram signal obeys different rules from standard traffic lights and most candidates have never driven through a tram crossing before their test. Getting it wrong is an immediate serious fault.

📍 A56 Washway Road → Manchester Road → Brooklands tram stop → A56 toward Stretford
2
Residential

B5166 School Road Sale

A 30mph residential road running through Sale with speed bumps near Sale Grammar School. School crossing patrols operate on weekday mornings — an active patrol warden must be obeyed regardless of what the traffic lights show. The speed bumps can tempt candidates to slow below 20mph when approaching — correct technique is to anticipate the bump, release the accelerator, and maintain a consistent 20–25mph approach rather than braking sharply. The speed limit itself is 30mph between bumps; driving at 15mph throughout is an undue hesitancy fault. Our students learn the bump spacing so there are no surprises on test day.

📍 B5166 School Road → Sale Grammar School → residential network → rejoin A56
3
Speed Limit Trap

A6144 Carrington Road / Partington

Carrington Road runs west from Sale toward Partington. The road is 40mph through the open section but drops to 30mph at the Partington boundary. This limit change is signed, but the sign appears at a point where candidates are often focused on navigating the approach to the village — and the change from rural 40mph to suburban 30mph is easy to miss. Exceeding 30mph past the boundary while looking at the signpost is a common fail here. Our instructors mark the exact location of the limit change during lessons. Not knowing it exists means discovering it too late on test day.

📍 A6144 Carrington Road west → Partington boundary (40→30mph) → return via Carrington
4
Dual Carriageway

M33 / A57 Stretford

The A57 dual carriageway slip road merge toward Stretford tests mirror-signal-manoeuvre discipline at 50mph. This is the highest-speed section of any Sale test route and the one most learners from residential roads find most intimidating. The merge lane is short — candidates who slow on the slip road instead of accelerating to match traffic speed create a dangerous situation and a serious fault. Mirrors must be checked on approach to the slip, on joining, and while settling in the lane. Lane discipline at 50mph must be consistent. Our Sale students practise this entry sequence repeatedly until it is automatic.

📍 A57 Stretford approach → slip road merge at 50mph → dual carriageway discipline → exit
5
20mph Zone

B5159 Marsland Road / Sale Moor

Marsland Road through Sale Moor is a 20mph zone with narrow lanes and parked cars on both sides. The door-zone risk is real — drivers parking on Marsland Road frequently open car doors without checking their mirrors. The correct position in a narrow lane with parked cars is approximately one metre from the parked vehicles (a door's width), which sometimes means positioning closer to the centre line than feels natural. Priority situations arise frequently here: one vehicle must wait while the other passes through a gap. Knowing who has priority at each of the regular pinch points is the difference between a confident pass and repeated hesitation faults.

📍 B5159 Marsland Road → Sale Moor village → 20mph zone → return via Northenden Road
6
Level Crossing

A5181 Cross Street / Urmston

The Cheshire Lines railway level crossing on Cross Street, Urmston, uses an amber signal rule that differs from a standard traffic light: amber means stop unless you are already past the stop line. At a standard traffic light, amber after green means stop if safe — but at a level crossing, the signal is a warning that a train is imminent, and crossing once the amber appears when you haven't yet reached the stop line is dangerous and a serious fault. The white light phase means proceed. Candidates who have only driven standard traffic-light junctions find the level crossing sequence counter-intuitive under pressure. We cover it in every Sale lesson programme.

📍 A5181 Cross Street → Urmston level crossing (Cheshire Lines) → Flixton Road approach
7
Manoeuvre Zone

Moss Lane DTC Bay Park

Bay parking is performed in the Altrincham DTC car park on Moss Lane. The bays are angled opposite the school entrance, with significant pedestrian movement at 9am as parents and children cross the car park. Observation during the bay park manoeuvre — not just during the drive — is assessed. The examiner is watching whether you check for pedestrians before reversing, not just whether the car ends up between the lines. Our Sale students practise this car park specifically, including the 9am pedestrian pattern, so the manoeuvre under pressure feels routine rather than high-stakes.

📍 Moss Lane DTC car park · Angled bays · School entrance opposite · 9am pedestrian peak

Local Knowledge

8 Sale Hazards Other
Schools Never Cover

The specific, local details that separate a DriveSQ Sale student from someone who learned without local knowledge.

A56 Metrolink Crossing — Brooklands Tram Signal

The Metrolink crossing at Brooklands tram stop on the A56 uses tram-specific signals that car drivers are rarely taught: steady amber means stop, flashing amber means proceed with caution if the tram stop platform is clear of boarding or alighting passengers. The tram signal must be obeyed before proceeding — not treated as an advisory. Most candidates have never driven through a tram crossing before their test and either stop unnecessarily on a green phase or proceed when they should wait. DriveSQ covers the full tram signal sequence from the first lesson on A56 routes, because it appears within minutes of leaving Altrincham DTC.

School Road Speed Bumps — Straddle or Take Correctly

Speed bumps on B5166 School Road near Sale Grammar test technique as well as speed compliance. The correct approach is to straddle narrow bumps (position tyres either side) or take wide flat-top bumps at a steady 20–25mph — not to brake sharply to 10mph before each one. Candidates who treat every bump as a near-stop generate undue hesitancy faults while simultaneously annoying the examiner. Speed limit awareness must be maintained between bumps — the limit is 30mph on School Road and deviating sharply up or down around the bumps creates an inconsistent speed profile that examiners note.

Carrington Road Speed Limit Trap at Partington

The 40mph-to-30mph limit change at the Partington boundary on A6144 Carrington Road is a genuine fail trap for Sale test candidates. The change occurs at the settlement boundary, not at a junction — there is no obvious visual cue beyond the speed limit sign itself. Candidates navigating while maintaining 40mph for the open road section frequently arrive at the sign a fraction late. Exceeding 30mph past the village boundary while reading the sign is a serious fault. Our instructors walk students through the exact geography of the transition so the speed is already changing before the sign appears.

A57 Stretford Slip Road — Mirrors at 50mph

The A57 slip road merge toward Stretford demands mirror-signal-manoeuvre discipline at approach speeds around 50mph. This is the fastest section of any Sale test route. The merge lane is deliberately short — the expectation is that you use the acceleration lane to match motorway-adjacent traffic, not slow down and wait for a gap. Candidates who approach at 30mph, then attempt to merge with 50mph traffic from a slow speed, create dangerous situations and immediate serious faults. Full mirror checks on approach, during merge, and after settling in lane are all assessed. Practice at speed is the only way this becomes routine.

Sale Moor 20mph — Priority and Door-Zone Risk

Marsland Road through Sale Moor is a 20mph zone with permanently parked cars creating a series of one-vehicle gaps. Priority rules in these gaps are informal — there is no formal give-way sign at every pinch — and candidates who don't read the road far enough ahead either commit to a gap that belongs to an oncoming vehicle or stop unnecessarily when they had clear priority. The door-zone (approximately one metre from parked vehicles) must be maintained even when the natural road width means this brings you close to the centre line. Our Sale instructors know every priority pinch on this route and teach the give-way decisions explicitly.

Urmston Level Crossing — Amber Means Stop

The Cheshire Lines level crossing on Cross Street, Urmston uses a signal sequence that differs critically from a standard traffic light: amber at a level crossing means stop unless you are already past the stop line. At a normal junction, amber after green means stop if safe — the stop is conditional. At a level crossing, once the sequence begins it means a train is imminent and stopping is mandatory if you have not yet crossed the line. The white light means proceed. Candidates who have only driven standard traffic-light roads find this counter-intuitive under pressure. The examiner is watching for the correct amber response — not the white light reaction, which is easy. We cover this every time the Cross Street route is used.

Sale Town Centre Loading Bays — Washway Road Deliveries

Washway Road through Sale town centre has loading bays that are active during business hours. Delivery vehicles double-park or occupy loading bays with open rear doors extending into the driving lane. Candidates unfamiliar with the stretch either swerve out sharply without checking mirrors, or stop unnecessarily behind a vehicle that has left adequate passing room. The correct response — check mirrors, indicate if changing position, pass with a safe clearance — must be executed smoothly. Hesitation or an abrupt swerve in the same scenario are both recordable faults. Our students know which blocks on Washway Road regularly have deliveries during test hours.

Moss Lane DTC Exit — School Pedestrians at 9am

Altrincham DTC on Moss Lane has a school entrance directly opposite. At 9am, the volume of pedestrians — children, parents, school staff — crossing the DTC exit is high. The examiner's assessment begins the moment you put the car in gear in the DTC car park. Candidates who check their mirrors for other cars but not for pedestrians stepping out between vehicles pick up an observation fault before they have completed the first left turn. This is a well-known fail point at Altrincham DTC that generic driving schools never mention. Every DriveSQ Sale student is briefed on this before their test day — and specifically practises the Moss Lane exit approach.

Sale Grammar School on School Road (B5166) and Blessed Thomas Holford Catholic College generate 20mph school zones and school-run congestion on weekday mornings and afternoons. Our students know every school zone on the Sale test route and its hours.

Trafford Centre Proximity

The Trafford Centre is approximately 15–20 minutes from Sale via the M60 / A57. On Saturdays and Sundays, A57 Chester Road and the M60 approach roads carry significantly heavier traffic than weekday test hours. Candidates tested on a Saturday should expect more vehicles at A57 junctions and more lane-change activity on the approach roads. Our instructors prepare Sale students for weekend traffic conditions specifically, not just quieter weekday patterns.

Old Trafford Match Days

Old Trafford stadium (M16) is approximately 4 miles from Sale via A56 / A5067. Match days — predominantly Saturdays and some midweek evenings — cause A56 gridlock between Sale and Old Trafford that backs up well before kick-off and for an hour after the final whistle. If your test falls on a match day, the A56 Washway Road section will have significantly more traffic than usual. Heavy traffic does not change the test route — but it does test your composure under pressure.

Carrington Industrial Area

The Carrington industrial estate west of Sale generates HGV traffic on A6144 Carrington Road throughout the working day. Large vehicles travelling to and from the estate use the same A6144 approach road that forms part of the Altrincham DTC test route. Overtaking or being overtaken by HGVs on a 40mph road requires mirror discipline and confidence in lane position. Candidates who have only practised on quiet residential roads find HGV interaction on a rural A-road significantly more demanding than expected.
