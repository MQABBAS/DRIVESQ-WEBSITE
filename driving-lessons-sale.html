Altrincham Driving Test Centre

The DTC Sale students use — and the things no driving school bothers to tell you about it.

Address: Moss Lane, Altrincham WA14 2TL — approximately 10 minutes' drive from Sale town centre (M33)
Test Duration: Approximately 38–40 minutes driving + pre-test briefing
Parking: Angled bays on Moss Lane opposite a school entrance — heavy pedestrian and vehicle movement during 9am test slots. Arrive 15 minutes early and do not park in a test bay. Note the school entrance activity before your test starts.
Documents: Provisional licence, theory pass certificate (within 2 years), glasses if prescribed for driving
Test Fee: £62 weekday / £75 weekend (DVSA 2025 rates)
Metrolink Access: Sale Metrolink stop (M33) is a 10-minute drive to Altrincham DTC. Navigation Road Metrolink stop is closest to Moss Lane for candidates using public transport
What nobody tells you: The DTC exit on Moss Lane sits directly opposite a school entrance. At 9am test slots, school-run pedestrians and parents in vehicles create a busy, unpredictable exit. The examiner is watching from the moment you select gear. Do not rush out of the bays — observe, signal, and check both the pedestrians at the school crossing and oncoming traffic on Moss Lane before moving.
Test route direction from Sale: From Sale, most test routes head north toward Stretford or south through Timperley — the A56 dual-carriageway section is almost always included. Do not treat the initial drive out of Altrincham DTC as a warm-up. The examiner is recording from the first moment.
A56 dual carriageway: The A56 Washway Road / Manchester Road is included in the majority of Sale test routes. It features 40mph sections, bus lanes, and the Metrolink crossing at Brooklands. Candidates who have not driven the A56 dual-carriageway in a lesson are unprepared for the lane discipline required.
Pass rate: Altrincham DTC is around the national average. Strong knowledge of local route specifics — particularly Metrolink crossings and the Carrington Road speed limit — is the key differentiator between prepared and unprepared candidates.

1
Metrolink Crossing

A56 Washway Road / Manchester Road

Sale's main arterial road runs at 40mph with bus lanes and a Metrolink tram crossing at Brooklands tram stop. At the Brooklands crossing, candidates must stop for the tram signal — it operates independently of the road traffic lights. Drivers who treat the tram signal as optional face an immediate serious fault. The bus lanes on Washway Road also have peak-time operational hours that catch candidates who enter them at the wrong time. This route is included in the majority of tests from Altrincham DTC for Sale candidates.

📍 A56 Washway Road Sale → Brooklands tram crossing → Manchester Road toward Stretford or Timperley
2
Residential 30mph

B5166 School Road Sale

School Road is a residential 30mph road with speed bumps near Sale Grammar School. School crossing patrols are active during morning test slots — candidates must stop when the patrol steps out, regardless of their speed. The speed bumps near the school require candidates to approach at a speed that allows the car to be controlled across them without straddling — doing this correctly while maintaining awareness of the 30mph limit is tested here. Many candidates unfamiliar with the road brake too late or too sharply for the first speed bump.

📍 B5166 School Road Sale → Sale Grammar School zone → 30mph residential network
3
Speed Limit Trap

A6144 Carrington Road / Partington

Carrington Road is a long, straight 40mph road with a rural feel and few hazards — until the Partington boundary, where the limit drops to 30mph. The signage is present but easy to miss when candidates are focused on the open road ahead. Driving at 40mph into a 30mph zone at the Partington boundary is one of the most common serious faults on this route. Our students learn exactly where the limit changes — not just to look for the sign, but to anticipate the location — before their test day.

📍 A6144 Carrington Road Sale → 40mph rural section → 30mph speed limit trap at Partington boundary
4
Dual Carriageway Merge

M33 Sale West / Stretford — A57 Slip Road

The A57 dual-carriageway slip road merge from Stretford requires mirror-signal-manoeuvre discipline at 50mph. Candidates who have only driven in residential areas find the speed and volume of traffic on the A57 intimidating. Lane discipline at 50mph — not drifting between lanes during the merge — is the specific skill tested here. The approach from Sale West via the M33 boundary also requires understanding which lane to be in before the dual carriageway begins. DriveSQ teaches this merge specifically because it requires different muscle memory from any residential junction.

📍 M33 Sale West → A57 dual-carriageway approach → 50mph slip road merge from Stretford
5
20mph Village

B5159 Marsland Road / Sale Moor

Marsland Road passes through the Sale Moor village 20mph residential area. The lanes are narrow with parked cars on both sides creating a door-zone risk — candidates must position away from the gutter sufficiently to avoid opening car doors while maintaining lane position. Frequent pedestrians, including elderly residents and families, cross between parked cars. Priority management — knowing when to give way and when to proceed — is tested repeatedly on this route. A candidate who has only driven in open 30mph roads will find this route technically demanding.

📍 B5159 Marsland Road → Sale Moor village 20mph zone → narrow lanes with parked cars
6
Level Crossing

A5181 Cross Street / Urmston Level Crossing

Cross Street in Urmston includes a level crossing for the Cheshire Lines railway. The rules at this crossing are exact: amber means stop unless you are already past the stop line; white means proceed. Candidates who stop unnecessarily when past the stop line, or who proceed on amber when they are not past it, fail here. This crossing has specific stopping rules different from a standard traffic light — most candidates who fail here have not been specifically taught what to do, only how to drive in general. DriveSQ covers this crossing in every Sale area lesson programme.

📍 A5181 Cross Street Urmston → Cheshire Lines railway level crossing → amber/white signal rules

Manoeuvre Zone

Moss Lane DTC Bay Park

The Altrincham DTC bay park uses angled bays on Moss Lane, directly opposite a school entrance. Heavy pedestrian and vehicle movement during 9am test slots makes this the most pressurised manoeuvre environment on any Sale-area test. Candidates must check both directions along Moss Lane and observe the school crossing before, during, and after the manoeuvre — the examiner continues recording throughout. The bays are angled rather than parallel, so reference points are specific to this location. We practise Moss Lane specifically, not just any bay park.

Local Knowledge

8 Sale Hazards Other
Schools Never Cover

The specific, local details that separate a DriveSQ Sale student from someone who learned without local knowledge.

A56 Metrolink Crossing at Brooklands — Tram Signal

The Brooklands Metrolink stop on Washway Road has a tram signal that must be obeyed independently of the road traffic lights. Candidates who have not driven through a tram crossing before are often uncertain whether to stop or proceed. The tram signal takes priority. If the tram signal is red or amber, you must stop — even if the road traffic lights are green. Driving through on the road-green when the tram light is against you is a serious fault. DriveSQ teaches this signal system from the first lesson because Brooklands appears within minutes of leaving the A56 on most Sale test routes.

School Road Speed Bumps — Technique and Speed

The speed bumps near Sale Grammar on B5166 School Road require a specific approach. Do not straddle the bump with wheels either side — this is a fault. Take the bump straight on at a speed that allows the car to cross without bottoming out or causing the steering wheel to be jolted. The correct approach is to brake before the bump, allow the car to slow to a comfortable speed, and maintain a straight line across. Candidates who brake on the bump or swerve to approach at an angle are penalised. Additionally, a school crossing patrol may be active on morning test slots — you must stop when the patrol steps out.

Carrington Road Speed Limit Trap — 40 to 30

The A6144 Carrington Road feels rural and open at 40mph, which creates a false sense that the limit stays consistent. At the Partington boundary, the limit drops to 30mph. The sign is present but positioned where candidates are focused on the road ahead rather than the verge. Many candidates drive 200–300 metres into the 30mph zone at 40mph before noticing. Our instructors teach candidates the exact location of the limit change — a specific road feature (a junction or building) that acts as a memory trigger so candidates don't rely solely on spotting the sign under pressure.

A57 Stretford Slip Road — Mirror-Signal-Manoeuvre at 50mph

The A57 dual-carriageway slip road merge from Stretford is one of the most technically demanding parts of any Sale-area test route. At 50mph, the MSM routine must be smooth and deliberate — not rushed. Candidates who apply MSM at 30mph but have not practised it at higher speeds often perform the sequence incorrectly: checking mirrors too late, signalling too close to the merge point, or failing to maintain consistent speed during the manoeuvre. Lane discipline during the merge — not drifting — is what examiners record. DriveSQ practises this slip road specifically.

Sale Moor 20mph Priority — Narrow Lanes and Door Zone

Sale Moor village runs at 20mph with narrow residential lanes lined with parked cars. The door-zone risk is real — a parked car whose driver opens their door suddenly can put a candidate in immediate danger. The correct position is to drive one metre from parked cars where possible, which means accepting a position near the lane centre. Candidates who hug the gutter risk the door zone; candidates who position too centrally create oncoming conflicts. Priority management — when to give way, when to assert — is also tested frequently on this route due to the narrow lane widths.

Urmston Level Crossing — Amber and White Signal Rules

The Cheshire Lines railway level crossing on A5181 Cross Street, Urmston, has signal rules that differ from a standard traffic light. At this crossing: steady amber means stop unless you are already past the stop line (if you are past the stop line when amber shows, continue safely across); white means proceed. Candidates who have not been specifically taught this often either stop unnecessarily past the stop line (creating an obstruction on the crossing) or proceed on amber when they should stop. Both are serious faults. This rule is specific to railway crossings and is not the same as a standard amber traffic light.

Sale Town Centre Loading Bays on Washway Road

Washway Road through Sale town centre has loading bays used by delivery vehicles throughout the day. Delivery lorries parked in loading bays often protrude slightly into the traffic lane, and drivers step out into the road while unloading. Candidates must position appropriately to pass safely — which may mean waiting for a gap in oncoming traffic — and must anticipate delivery drivers stepping into the road without warning. This is a specific, local road characteristic that generic driving instruction ("watch for emerging hazards") does not prepare you for specifically. Our Sale instructors identify loading bay locations on Washway Road during the first lesson.

Moss Lane DTC Exit — School Entrance at 9am

The Altrincham DTC bay park on Moss Lane sits directly opposite a school entrance. The 9am test slot is also peak school-run time. As candidates complete the bay park manoeuvre and move to exit the bays, parents in vehicles are arriving and departing the school entrance opposite, and children and parents are crossing Moss Lane on foot between the pavement and school gate. The examiner continues to record through the entire exit. DriveSQ students who have practised Moss Lane know to complete a full observation of both directions of traffic and the school crossing area before moving from the bays.
